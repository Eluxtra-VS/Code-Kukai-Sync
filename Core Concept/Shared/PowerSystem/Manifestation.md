# Manifestation

## Short Definition

A **Manifestation** is the named, unique signature ability that an [[Akarui]] unlocks when they integrate their [[Reiva]] and resolve [[Pressure]] outward in their first meaningful action. It is not a general skill — it is _theirs_, tied to their psychology, their history, and the specific shape of their Pressure.

---

## Terminology: Manifestation vs. Signature

**Signature** is the broad term for a character's deepest named ability, regardless of Path. Weavers and Reiki have signatures too: for example, Kaito's design-name is Divergence and Mel's signature is Flare.

**Manifestation** is the Akarui-specific form of a signature. It is the outward, named ability produced when an Akarui resolves Pressure externally. Every Manifestation is a signature, but not every signature is a Manifestation.

## Characteristics

- **Named** — every Manifestation has a specific name personal to the host.
- **Visually distinct** — its appearance reflects the host's emotional world, not just their combat style.
- **Personally tied** — it cannot be replicated by another person. It is the Akarui's fingerprint.

---

## Evolution

A Manifestation changes as the host changes:

|Trigger|Effect|
|---|---|
|**Deepening Pressure**|Escalation — the ability grows more powerful but more unstable and costly|
|**Resolving Pressure**|Reconfiguration — the ability refines, becomes more precise and sustainable|
|**Bond growth**|Expanded range of use; new expressions of the same core technique unlock|

The Manifestation is a story signifier. When it changes, the character has changed.

---

## Mechanics

- Uses [[Rei]] from the working Reserve or, at great risk, the Core Reserve.
- Stronger Manifestations require a deeper [[Bond Stages|bond]] and clearer emotional alignment.
- Using a Manifestation while Pressure is critical produces volatile, wasteful, unpredictable output.

---

## Examples

The following examples show how a Manifestation expresses the user's Class, Pressure, and emotional pattern. They are not interchangeable techniques: each one is a personal externalisation of the host's unresolved problem.

- **[[Shackles]] — Aiden Takeda, Forge:** Aiden builds bindings that become less stable when he is performing for attention and cleaner when he is solving the problem sincerely. The construct is the proof he keeps trying to make visible.
- **[[Monarch]] — Mai Takeda, Forge:** Mai produces autonomous mechanical ant constructs that seed deference into the surrounding Rei architecture. She does not ask for the bow; she builds the conditions in which bowing feels inevitable.
- **[[Fracture]] — Kashi Kizu, Veil:** Kashi attacks active emotional suppression as a structural weakness in Rei. The target's refusal to process the present emotion becomes the opening the Manifestation uses.
- **[[Judgement]] — Dante Vargas, Veil:** Dante returns intentional harm to a target who still carries present guilt. The signature externalises consequence, but cannot decide whether punishment is the same thing as healing.

These examples demonstrate the design rule: a Manifestation should be mechanically legible, visually distinct, and psychologically specific. If the ability could belong to another character without changing its meaning, it is not yet personal enough.
