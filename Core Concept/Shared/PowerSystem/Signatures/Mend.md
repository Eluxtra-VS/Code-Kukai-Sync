# Mend

[[Aoki Sora|Sora]]

> **Class revision (2026-08-20):** Sora was previously classified as Weaver Forge with a signature called Mending. She is now **Weaver Vigor**, signature **Mend**. The old classification followed the *function* (repair = building = Forge). It failed the class test: Sora's desire is not to build something real — it is for her hands to **be enough** not to ruin what they touch. That is Vigor's desire. See [[Signature Design]] for the framework this revision follows.

---

## Short Definition

**Mend** is Sora's Weaver Vigor signature, Thread: Hands. On touch, her hands push a damaged thing's Rei toward functional wholeness — not restoring the past, but building a repair from what is there.

The same hands have a second mode. **Unmake** is Mend's destructive application: on touch, she unravels structure and weakens the target's Rei integrity. It is the inverted mirror of [[Beat-Break]] — Ku's power amplifies when his rhythm is *honest*; Sora's destructive mode amplifies when her self-belief is *dishonest*.

> _She never breaks anything. She has spent her whole life believing otherwise._

## Pressure Logic

Sora's core wound is self-blame as worldview: if she was in the room when something broke, some part of her already believes the break is hers — and underneath that runs the desire for her hands to be enough not to ruin what they touch. That desire is Vigor's desire (be enough), located in hands because hands are where responsibility becomes action, which makes her a Weaver Vigor rather than the Forge her repair function would suggest. The signature expresses the pressure both ways: Mend is what the hands do when they believe they are enough; Unmake is what the same hands do when they agree with the wound, fueled by her dishonest self-belief that she ruins everything she touches. Full character context: [[Aoki Sora]]

---

## Why Vigor

Vigor's desire is to be enough. Sora's wound is the exact shape of that desire, inverted into self-blame: everything she gets close to, everything she touches, eventually goes wrong — so her hands must become enough to help instead of ruin.

Her Thread is her hands because hands are where responsibility becomes action. Hands hold, fix, reach, fail to catch, fail to stop, fail to save. Sora's hands are the place she has always put the question: *what should I have done?*

Weaver Vigor gives her a passive whole-body baseline, with the Thread hands reaching a ceiling when activated. Mend is what the hands do at that ceiling when they believe they are enough. Unmake is what the same hands do at that ceiling when she believes they are not.

---

## Mend — Constructive Mode

Sora can reshape damaged structures by touching them and forcing their Rei toward functional wholeness. This is not the same as Kaito's State function, which moves Rei through its own history. Sora does not restore the past. She creates a repair.

What she can mend:

- Broken tools and structures.
- Torn clothing, straps, bindings, weapons.
- Damaged Forge constructs.
- At higher mastery, surface-level wounds and Rei channel disruptions.

What she cannot do easily:

- Reverse death.
- Undo deep psychological Pressure.
- Restore something she does not understand.
- Fix a person who is refusing to be fixed.

The limitation matters. Sora's arc requires her to learn that not everything broken is asking for her hands.

---

## Unmake — Destructive Mode

The same hands, reversed. On touch, Sora unravels the target's Rei integrity: structural cohesion loosens, techniques destabilise, an opponent's output degrades the longer she holds contact. Against a person, it does not kill — it *undoes*: strength, focus, and coherence drain as the integrity of their Rei comes apart in her hands.

**The gate:** Unmake is a Mode Gate condition (see [[Signature Design]]). It is always available, but it is *easier, cleaner, and stronger* the more Sora believes the damage is her fault. In a spiral — after a failure she has decided is her responsibility — Unmake flows like breathing. When she is grounded and honest, the destructive mode is harder to hold; Mend is what wants to come out of her hands instead.

This is the inversion of Beat-Break. Ku's ceiling rises when his emotional state is clean. Sora's destructive ceiling rises when her emotional state is corrupt. Her worst belief — *I ruin everything I touch* — is literal fuel. Unmake is what the hands do when they agree with the wound.

---

## Cost

Mend carries the emotional cost of responsibility.

At low control, every repair leaves feedback in her hands: tremors, numbness, stiffness, pain, or temporary loss of fine motor control. The more she believes the break was her fault, the more aggressively the Thread consumes her.

Unmake's cost is different and worse: every time she uses it, she proves the wound right. Each unmaking is evidence that her hands are dangerous — which feeds the belief that fuels the ability. It is a self-reinforcing loop only her resolution can break.

---

## Fix and Worse

**Fix — what honest processing unlocks:** Mend becomes clean when she stops asking "how is this my fault?" and starts asking "what does this need?" The hands stop consuming her when she treats damage as information rather than accusation. Her breakthrough is failing to fix something and staying present without taking ownership of the whole damage — the first time her hands are enough without having to prove it.

**Worse — what suppression deepens:** Unmake grows stronger and easier the more she believes she ruins things. The more she hides inside repair work, the more the destructive mode waits for her. Her resolution is refusing to spend it — learning that unmaking someone is the same act as believing she ruins everything.

---

## Arc Direction

Early: Sora is the capable one behind the capable one. Ku moves first, Sora makes sure the movement holds. Her Mend is useful, and the more useful it becomes, the easier it is for her to hide inside repair work.

Middle: The group starts noticing she apologises for things no one accused her of. Unmake surfaces for the first time in a fight — clean, strong, effortless — and it should be the most alarming moment in her early story: she has never felt more powerful than when she believes she ruins things.

Pressure point: The truth of the K.E.F. order comes out — that she tried before she stopped. This should hurt Ku because he loves her, not because he rejects her.

Breakthrough: Sora fails to fix something and does not collapse into self-blame. She stays present without taking ownership of the whole damage. Unmake loses its grip on her hands.

Resolution: Mend becomes cleaner when she understands that repair is care, not confession. She can still unmake — when it is the honest choice, not the self-hating one.
