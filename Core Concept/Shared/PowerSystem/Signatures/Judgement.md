# Judgement

[[Dante Vargas|Dante]]

---

## Short Definition

**Judgement** is the signature ability of Dante Vargas - Akarui Veil. Dante can perceive intentional harm a person caused while carrying present guilt, then return that harm in a worse, contextually appropriate form.

## Pressure Logic

Dante's Wrath is refined, not hot: years of watching harm dodge consequence condensed his anger into certainty, and his deepest desire is accountability — a world where harm meets cost even though institutions will not deliver it. That desire makes him Veil, and the condition encodes his whole philosophy: only intentional harm still carrying present guilt qualifies, because guilt is the evidence his verdicts run on. The return expresses the pressure exactly — he hands back a heightened version of what the target knowingly put into the world, Wrath delivered as sentence, with the unresolved question of whether seeing guilt clearly entitles him to punish it. Full character context: [[Dante Vargas]]

---

## The Condition

**Akarui Veil condition:** the target intentionally harmed another person and still carries guilt for it.

Both halves matter.

| Requirement | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Intentional harm** | The target knowingly chose an action that harmed someone else. |
| **Present guilt** | The guilt still exists in their Rei architecture, whether acknowledged, buried, rationalised, or denied. |

Accidental harm alone does not meet the condition.

Remorseless harm alone does not meet the condition unless guilt exists underneath the denial.

This makes Judgement narrower than "everyone who has ever hurt someone," but more psychologically dangerous. Dante's ideal targets are people who know what they did, cannot fully escape knowing it, and have built their lives around not paying the cost.

---

## Detection

When the condition is met, Dante perceives the harm.

He sees:

- Who was hurt.
- What was done.
- What the target intended.
- What guilt remains.
- How the target has explained, buried, or justified it.

Judgement does not require confession. The guilt is the evidence. It is not legal proof, but to Dante it feels cleaner than proof because it comes from the target's own Rei.

---

## The Return

Once Dante has detected the harm, he can deploy the return.

The return mirrors the nature of the original harm and amplifies it in a contextually appropriate form.

- Physical harm returns physically.
- Betrayal returns through trust collapsing.
- Abandonment returns as forced helplessness.
- Concealed crime returns as exposure and consequence.
- Repeated cruelty returns as accumulated weight landing all at once.

The ability does not produce random punishment. It makes the target experience a heightened version of what they knowingly placed into the world.

---

## The Problem

Judgement is accurate about harm and guilt. It is not automatically wise.

The ability can show Dante that someone caused harm and carries guilt. It cannot decide mercy. It cannot decide context. It cannot decide whether punishment heals anything. Dante does that.

His sin is Wrath refined into certainty: the belief that because he can see guilt clearly, he is qualified to decide what it deserves.

The story question is not whether Dante punishes the guilty. The story question is whether guilt is the same thing as a sentence.

---

## Limits

- No intentional harm, no activation.
- No present guilt, no activation.
- Guilt processed honestly weakens or breaks the condition.
- Guilt buried under denial still counts if it remains structurally present.
- Dante must remain within effective Veil range to read and return the harm.
- Larger returns cost more Rei and focus.
