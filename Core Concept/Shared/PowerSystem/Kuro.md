## Short Definition

**Kuro** is the negative expression of [[Rei]], channelled through negative emotional states. Where [[Luz]] is the long game - saturation, disable, accumulated breakdown - Kuro is the **short game**: immediate, precise, and strategic.

> "The entire time, they were using Luz for everything. Attacks, defense, manifestations - all of it. Kuro was always there. Nobody told them."

---

## Rei and Kuro

Rei is the raw spiritual lifeforce. Kuro is what Rei becomes when it flows through:

- **Rage** - heat, aggression, drive.
- **Sorrow** - grief, loss, heaviness.
- **Fear** - dread, avoidance, paralysis.

Kuro is not a separate or inherently corrupting substance. It is Rei in its negative state. The danger is feedback: sustained Kuro use raises the user's own negative emotion levels. [[Kukai]] are the warning of what happens when Kuro becomes the only thing left.

---

## The Hidden Half

At the story's beginning, **Kuro does not exist in the main cast's understanding**. They use [[Luz]] for almost everything, and most ordinary people have no clear idea Kuro exists.

Kuro is not hidden from everyone. Knowledge of it exists in fragments and institutions:

- **The [[Tenebri]] organisation** - trained deliberate users; this gives them a major early advantage.
- **K.E.F. specialists and certain institutional researchers** - enough to understand incidents and technology, not always enough to explain it honestly.
- **Extractor engineers and advanced users** - because Extractors interface with the Luz/Kuro ratio.
- **Some elders and old [[Kyumin]]** - those who encountered Kuro without always having clean language for it.

When the main cast discovers Kuro, it hits on three levels:

1. They feel cheated: they were fighting with incomplete information.
2. [[Kukai]] are recontextualised: what looked like pure corruption was also the hidden expression of the same lifeforce.
3. The question of who knew becomes urgent.

## Offensive Use - The Short Game

Kuro offense operates on **immediacy and precision**.

- Kuro damage is direct; no saturation phase is required.
- A skilled Kuro fighter aims for specific gaps, weak points, and openings.
- Kuro is not chaos. Its identity is time horizon: Kuro wins now, Luz wins eventually.

Against an opponent already carrying significant foreign [[Luz]], Kuro becomes more dangerous because the target is slower and less coherent.

---

## Defensive Use

Kuro defense works by **blocking and redirecting** rather than absorbing.

- Kuro barriers stop attacks more cleanly than Luz barriers.
- A Kuro defender can redirect force to a tactically useful location.
- Kuro defense is shorter-duration and more active than Luz defense.

---

## Emotion Effect

In addition to direct damage, Kuro attacks can install negative [[Emotions]] in the target:

- Primary: **Fear** - a precise strike that gets through creates dread.
- Secondary: Sorrow and Rage can be installed depending on the technique and emotion used.

---

## Reserve and Risk

Kuro draws from the same two-pool reserve system as Luz:

| Reserve | Function |
|---|---|
| **Core Reserve** | Essential Rei sustaining the Reiva bond. Drawing from this is dangerous and must never reach zero. |
| **Reserve** | Expendable pool for normal ability use. Standard combat resource. |

Overdrawing carries catastrophic risks:

- Physical and mental strain.
- Bond degradation.
- Increased [[Pressure]] susceptibility.
- Accelerated corruption toward [[Kukai]] in the worst case.

Using Kuro actively raises the user's own negative emotion levels. It is controlled and precise, but the emotional cost is real.

## Kuro vs Luz

| | Luz | Kuro |
|---|---|---|
| **Game length** | Long - accumulation and saturation | Short - immediate and precise |
| **Damage method** | Foreign Luz -> disable -> poison -> overflow | Direct damage on contact |
| **Defense style** | Dampen and absorb | Block and redirect |
| **Emotion effect** | Erodes positive emotions | Installs negative emotions |
| **Rei cost** | Efficient for defense, costly for offense | Efficient for offense, costly for sustained defense |
| **Strategic identity** | Control space, extend the fight | Control the moment, find the gap |
