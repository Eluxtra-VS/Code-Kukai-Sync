## Short Definition

**Buki** is a universal technique — available to any awakened person regardless of path — that channels [[Luz]] into objects, reshaping and manipulating them through repeated contact and emotional investment. It is not a path, not a faction, and not exclusive to the unawakened.

Among awakened people, Buki carries a social stigma: it is commonly regarded as a **sign of low control or wasted potential** — a crutch for those who route their power into objects rather than directing it cleanly inward or outward. That dismissal reflects the culture's bias toward visible, dramatic power expression. It is not an accurate assessment of Buki's ceiling.

> _"If you can't use your own body or your own projection, you use objects. It's the last resort of someone who doesn't trust themselves."_ — Common awakened sentiment.

That dismissal is not entirely wrong. It is not entirely right either. But Buki users learn quickly that the world does not take them seriously — which is, for some of them, exactly the kind of Pressure that makes the technique develop fast.

---

## Key Properties

- **Requires direct touch** — Luz must be routed into an object through physical contact.
- **Seeding:** Repeated use on a single object deposits Luz into it permanently, allowing the user to manipulate it later at greatly reduced cost.
- **Feedback Burn:** First-time manipulation of an unfamiliar object costs significantly more Luz and weakens the user proportionally. This risk never fully disappears.
- **Anchors:** Objects that have been heavily seeded over time are called **Anchors** — they respond to the user almost instinctively.

## Who Uses Buki

Buki is a technique, not a path. Any awakened person can learn and use it. What differs is how naturally it fits their direction and how efficient it is relative to their primary method.

| Path | Buki Relationship | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **[[Kyumin]]** (unawakened) | High — primary method | For unawakened hosts, Buki is the main way to engage with their Reiva's power before Awakening commits them to a direction |
| **[[Akarui]]** | Available — supplementary | Akarui externalise Luz directly; Buki is available but costs more and is less efficient than projection |
| **[[Weaver]]** | Available — supplementary | Weavers internalise Luz through Thread; Buki is available but works against the natural grain of inward channeling |
| **[[Reiki]]** | Core — foundational | Buki mechanics are not supplementary for Reiki; they *are* the path. Displacement through Anchors is the Reiki identity, elevated from technique to full awakened expression |

The awakened community's consensus treats low Buki usage as confirmation of the technique's ceiling. What that consensus misses: a Blame-stage Buki practitioner on their home ground, surrounded by decades of seeded Anchors, is not fighting at a disadvantage. The community just hasn't updated its assumptions yet.

## Mechanics

### Manipulation Limit

- Users can only control a finite number of seeded objects simultaneously.
- This limit increases with progression.

### Affinity Channeling

- At high mastery, Buki users can channel their Reiva's affinity ([[Classes]]: Vigor, Pulse, Forge, Veil) *through* an Anchor.
- This is **weaker and more Luz-expensive** than direct Akarui use, but it allows any practitioner to access class-based techniques through their objects.

## Progression Stages

Buki has three progression stages. Unlike [[Akarui]] or [[Weaver|Weavers]], Buki progression is measured by the user's **relationship with their objects** and the emotional weight they invest in them — not bond depth alone.

| Stage | State |
|---|---|
| **Connection** | First contact; objects barely respond; high cost |
| **Hinan** | Objects become refuge; reliable but dependent |
| **Blame** | Peak mastery; the deepest, most costly relationship with Anchors |

---

### Stage 1 — Connection

_"The first time you touch something and feel it remember you."_

- **Mechanics:** Can channel [[Luz]] into one object at a time through sustained touch. High feedback burn on all unfamiliar objects. Seeded objects respond to basic intent only (move, hold, resist).
- **Drawbacks:** Cannot maintain control at range; must remain in contact or very close proximity. Feedback burn can temporarily numb the contact limb.
- **Feel:** Slow, intentional, tactile. Every use is a deliberate commitment. The user is learning what it means to trust something outside themselves.

---

### Stage 2 — Hinan

_"The objects know you. You go to them the way other people go to sleep."_

- **Meaning:** *Hinan* — refuge. At this stage the user doesn't just use their Anchors. They retreat into them. Objects become the emotional shelter they struggle to find in people. It is powerful, and it is a warning sign.
- **Mechanics:** Can control multiple seeded Anchors simultaneously (limit: 3–5). Feedback burn on familiar Anchors is greatly reduced. Range improves — Anchors respond without touch within meaningful proximity. Affinity begins to bleed through Anchors (weak class-based effects emerge).
- **Drawbacks:** New/unseeded objects still carry full feedback burn. The user's reliance on their Anchors deepens — losing them in this stage hits harder than a physical injury. Controlling multiple Anchors simultaneously splits focus; quality degrades under emotional stress.
- **Feel:** Practised, reliable. But the ease comes from isolation — a Hinan-stage Buki user has started letting their objects replace the relationships they find too difficult.

---

### Stage 3 — Blame

_"You don't control them anymore. You need them."_

- **Meaning:** The word "Blame" sits at the peak because a Blame-stage Buki user has reached a quiet, dangerous truth: they have **projected their emotional weight onto their Anchors**. The objects carry what the user cannot — grief, guilt, longing. The power is real. So is the dependency.
- **Mechanics:** High Anchor count with minimal concentration cost. Full affinity channeling through Anchors. Anchors respond to emotional state alone — intent replaces explicit control. Can rapidly seed new objects under combat pressure (reduced but not eliminated feedback risk).
- **Drawbacks:** Destroying a Blame-stage user's Anchors is not just tactically effective — it is psychologically devastating. The objects carry emotional weight that has nowhere else to go. Over-seeding creates heavy [[Drift]] signatures. The user is at highest risk of losing the ability to form meaningful human bonds.
- **Feel:** Effortless on familiar ground. Dangerous when isolated. A Blame-stage Buki user fights best surrounded by their history — and is most fragile when that history is taken from them.

## Narrative Role

- Buki practitioners represent **adaptability and environment mastery** rather than raw personal power.
- Their stories often centre on the relationship between a person and the things they carry, build, or protect.
- The loss of an Anchor can be written as emotionally devastating — equivalent to the loss of a body part for a Weaver.
