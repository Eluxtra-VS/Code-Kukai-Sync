# Veil

## Short Definition

**Veil** is the fourth Class: conditional direct manipulation of Rei. Where [[Vigor]] amplifies, [[Pulse]] projects, and [[Forge]] constructs, Veil waits for a condition to be met.

**Veil is the only class where the condition is the entire mechanism.** Without the condition, a Veil ability does nothing at all. When the condition is met, the effect bypasses normal physical defense and acts directly through Rei within its defined scope — absolute, because you already walked into it.

> "You cannot block something you already walked into."

> **Note — the universal condition layer:** conditions are not exclusive to Veil. Any class can attach a condition as a perk, amplifier, or stabilizer (e.g. Beat-Break's honest rhythm, Shackles' sincerity). On a non-Veil signature the ability works without its condition; the condition makes it better or cleaner. **If removing the condition makes the ability cease to exist, it is Veil — classify it as such.** See [[Signature Design]] for the full framework.

---

## The Core Distinction

Pulse fires Rei through physical space. It can be dodged, blocked, absorbed, or redirected.

Veil does not travel toward the target in the same way. It activates when the target, user, or Anchor satisfies the condition. The counter to Veil is not a stronger shield; it is avoiding, breaking, or invalidating the condition.

You can shield against a beam. You cannot shield against eye contact you already made, smoke you already inhaled, or a signature name you already spoke.

---

## The Three-Part Rule

Every Veil ability must define three things:

| Rule | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **Condition** | What has to be true before the ability can activate. |
| **Scope** | What the ability is allowed to affect once active. |
| **Cost** | What maintaining or exploiting the condition costs the user. |

If one of these is vague, the ability will feel unfair.

---

## Conditions

Veil conditions must be concrete. They can be emotional, physical, verbal, sensory, or object-based, but they need a clear yes/no trigger.

Good conditions:

- The target looks into Daiki's eyes.
- The target intentionally harmed someone and currently carries guilt.
- The target is actively suppressing an emotion.
- The target hears their own signature name spoken aloud.
- The target inhales smoke from a seeded Anchor.

Weak conditions:

- The target is "nearby."
- The user "wants it enough."
- The target "has emotions."

The more common the condition, the narrower the scope or higher the cost should be. The rarer the condition, the more absolute the effect can be.

---

## Scope

Veil effects should be powerful, but not infinite. Scope answers:

- Does it affect body, emotion, memory, movement, perception, Rei flow, or ability output?
- Does it affect one target or an area?
- Does it require line of sight, hearing range, touch, inhalation, or Anchor range?
- Does it last only while the condition remains true, or does it leave a lingering effect?
- Can it affect objects, humans, Reiva, Kukai, or all Rei-bearing things?

Veil is unblockable after the condition is met, but it is not undefined. A Veil ability only does what its scope says it does.

---

## Cost

Veil cost is usually paid in one or more of these ways:

- **Setup cost:** The user must engineer the situation before the fight.
- **Attention cost:** The user must keep focus on the condition or target.
- **Range cost:** The effect weakens with distance from user or Anchor.
- **Emotional cost:** The user's own Pressure pattern is reinforced by repeated use.
- **Rei cost:** The effect draws more Reserve the longer it is sustained.
- **Backlash cost:** If the condition breaks suddenly, the user loses control or suffers feedback.

This keeps Veil from becoming "I win because the condition happened once."

## Veil Across the Three Types

### Akarui - Veil

The condition is **external**. Something about the target, environment, or situation triggers the ability.

The Akarui does not usually force the condition directly. They engineer circumstances where the condition becomes likely or inevitable.

Examples:

- [[Daiki Amori]]: eye contact. Longer gaze means deeper control.
- [[Dante Vargas]]: intentional harm plus present guilt. Judgement cannot fire on accident alone or guiltless harm alone.
- [[Jakar Offei]]: the target registers his presence as an object of attention.
- [[Kashi Kizu]]: the target is actively suppressing an emotion in that moment.

### Weaver - Veil

The condition is **bodily**. The Thread body part is both the trigger mechanism and the place where the ability lives.

Examples:

- [[Niko Juul]]: hearing a signature name through his Thread ears.

> *Former example: [[Kaito Amori]] (reclassified Weaver Vigor 2026-08-20 — his honest-focus requirement is an amplifier gate, not a Veil condition). The bodily-condition pattern he exemplified is now carried by Niko's naming lock alone.*

### Reiki - Veil

The condition is **the Anchor**. Something must happen through or around a seeded object.

Examples:

- [[Yeshi Tanaka]]: skin contact with or inhalation of seeded cigar smoke.
- A future Reiki Veil user might require someone to willingly touch, accept, open, wear, or enter the Anchor's field.

---

## Resistance

The counter to Veil is partly behavioural and partly internal.

Behavioural counters:

- Avoid the condition.
- Break eye contact.
- Do not speak the signature name.
- Leave the Anchor's range.
- Cover skin or breathing against smoke.
- Stop suppressing and name the emotion honestly.

Internal counters:

- Clean Rei flow makes Veil harder to grip.
- Honest emotional processing creates fewer exploitable distortions.
- Strong bond health stabilises the target's Rei architecture.
- Knowing the condition reduces panic and prevents accidental re-triggering.

This does not make a target immune. It makes the effect harder to start, weaker in scope, shorter in duration, or more expensive to maintain.

---

## Why Veil Fits the Code

Veil users carry the desire to control conditions rather than outcomes. At its healthiest, this produces tacticians who understand context. At its most unresolved, it produces people who engineer suffering because they cannot tolerate entering a situation they did not define.

This makes Veil the Class most directly tied to the true [[Code]]. Suppression gives Veil more entry points. Honest processing closes them.

---

## Balance Rules for Writing

- A Veil ability should have one clean condition, not several fuzzy ones.
- **If the condition is easy to trigger, the effect must be narrow.** A Veil whose condition is trivially satisfied is a Veil violating its own class. (The original Faultline — condition: the target merely notices him — was this violation. Fixed in [[Faultline]].)
- If the effect is massive, the condition should be risky, rare, or costly.
- The target should usually have at least one meaningful way to avoid re-triggering it once they understand it.
- Emotional honesty should be the best universal resistance, but not a perfect shield.
- Veil should feel like a trap, not a beam with a different name.
- **Veils should be rare.** If more than a third of the cast's signatures are Veil, the class has stopped being a psychological niche and become the default. Diversity across Vigor/Pulse/Forge/Veil is a canon priority.
  > *Count note (2026-09-28): the current signature index holds 15 distinct signatures, 6 of them Veil (Warden does not count — unified into Faultline 2026-08-20). That is 40%: above the line. **Rarity remediation:** no existing Veil is misfiled (all pass the strict condition-is-the-mechanism test), so the rule is enforced going forward — the next 3 new signatures must be non-Veil, bringing the ratio under a third without retroactive reclassification. Do not add new Veil signatures until then.*
