# Version Swap (True Divergence)

> **One ability. One verb. Two eyes.** Kaito swaps things with other versions of themselves from the diverged worlds. Everything else - "Rate," "State" - is what he *thought* it was. [[Divergence]] documents the believed layer; this file is the truth. Both are canon. The gap between them is the story.

## The Mechanic - in plain words

Every thing that exists - people, objects, wounds, actions - has other versions of itself across the diverged worlds. Not copies: **drafts**. The same you, shaped by slightly different outcomes. The same punch, thrown a thousand slightly different ways. The same arm, whole in one world, scarred in another.

Kaito looks at a thing and **swaps it with one of its drafts.** The draft he chooses becomes real, here, now. The version that was here gets displaced - sent to whatever world made sense of it.

- **Left eye - events.** He swaps what a thing *does*. Your slow punch out; the fast draft of that same punch in. The punch is genuinely faster - real speed, not a head start.
- **Right eye - states.** He swaps what a thing *is*. Your cut arm out; the draft of you that never got cut in. **Memories travel with the person** - you remember being cut - but your body is the body that never was.

Nothing is created. Nothing is erased. **Versions trade places.** The swap is seamless: the draft arrives with its own history attached, and the world re-frames around it without a seam. That seamlessness is exactly why Kaito misread his own power for fifteen years.

## The Cost - the displaced don't vanish

The swapped-out version still exists. The injured Mel is now in a world where the cut never happened - hurt, confused, surrounded by people who don't know her. Every swap makes two worlds slightly wrong. The trades accumulate.

The Stranger's world is that accumulation. He swapped his way through his own world's collapse - and what's left of it, and of him, is the ledger.

## The Gate - decision honesty, not fighting-while-calm

The demand is not "perfect focus." It is **decision honesty.** The swap locks to whichever draft Kaito's intent is pointed at *in the moment it lands.* If he commits - save them, now, this way - clean swap. If he doubts mid-reach - *no wait, should I heal them or move them, which version* - the intent splits across drafts and the swap comes back sloppy, wrong, or nothing at all.

This is his [[Kaito Amori|Inadequacy]] made mechanical: the boy who re-edits every sentence before it leaves his mouth is the boy whose power re-edits itself mid-cast. **Fixing his self-editing as a character literally fixes his power.** The ability is the psychology - same as every signature in the system.

## Why "Rate and State" Made Perfect Sense

The real explanation requires a concept - diverged worlds - that doesn't exist in his world's vocabulary. He couldn't have reached it. So he built the most reasonable model that fit what he saw:

> "My left eye changes how fast energy flows through you - that's why you got faster."
> "My right eye moves the injury backward through your own history - that's why it healed."

Both wrong. Both explaining 100% of what he witnessed. He wasn't stupid; he was pattern-matching with the only physics his world has. That's what makes the reveal fair: not a retcon - a **frame shift**. Same facts, bigger picture. Every fight scene he ever fought stays true; it just wasn't what he thought he was doing.

## The Dreams - the resonance thread

Kaito has **multiple recurring dreams**, not one: the Kukai mid-strike from the Code prologue panel, and others - flashes of places he's never been, a fall he's never taken, a sky the wrong color. He's always dismissed them as nightmares.

Truth: **dreams are the resonance between versions of the same self.** The connection was always there - long before any eye did anything. The prologue Kukai is a diverged Kaito, mid-fall. The dreams are how the Stranger found this world: he followed the resonance. Foreshadowing structure: page-one imagery the reader reads as worldbuilding; recurring dream-motif the reader reads as trauma; the reveal re-frames both as literal.

Not everything is a dream-clue. His insomnia and night-time conversation-replaying stay **pure character** - the Inadequacy, not the multiverse.

## The Name

"Divergence" - the name he claims at the naming scene - is not a label he chose well. It's the truth slipping out: the power reaches the selves that *diverged*. He names it honestly without knowing why it fits. And saying it out loud is what makes the connection loud enough for the Stranger to finally walk through.
