# The Black Box

> _"He named it after the thing in aircraft that records everything up to the moment of the crash. He thought that was clever."_

## What It Was

The **Black Box** was a covert experimental facility operating in the margins of the Tidal Plate — funded privately, staffed by people who asked few questions, and run by a man named **Ren Mochizuki** whose bitterness had found a purpose.

Ren had been sent to the Tidal Plate because of his brother — a sequence of events he had decided, years before the facility opened, was the fault of the son born to him there. He named the boy **Kukai**. He meant it as a verdict. The boy shortened it to Ku and said nothing about it for years.

The facility's stated purpose, to the limited number of people aware it existed, was research into the Awakening process — specifically, the conditions under which Kyumin could be reliably awakened and their resulting abilities directed. The Sovereign's Crown had interests in this. The K.E.F. had interests in this. Neither organisation officially acknowledged the facility. Both found its research useful enough not to ask where the subjects came from.

The subjects were children.

---

## How It Operated

The Black Box recruited through two channels:

**Volunteers** — parents, primarily from the Tidal Plate's most economically precarious communities, who were told their children showed exceptional potential and would receive training and care that the plate could not otherwise provide. Some parents understood more of what they were signing than others. Most did not understand enough. The paperwork described educational programmes. The facility described itself as a developmental centre.

**Kyumin** — individuals identified through methods the facility had developed for detecting dormant Reiva bonds. Kyumin children showed up in the facility's intake records as having "elevated sensitivity profiles." The language was clinical. The meaning was that these children had something the facility wanted to see expressed.

The tests varied. Some were psychological — structured stress induction, emotional extremity under observation, situations designed to push Pressure toward critical levels in controlled environments. Some were physical. Some involved contact with objects the children were not told were Relics. None of the tests were explained to the subjects beforehand. The reasoning was that informed subjects produced contaminated data.

The children who Awakened were transferred to a different part of the facility. The children who did not were retained and tested further. Among the retained group, the rate of Kukai transformation was significant. The facility's documentation referred to these outcomes as "unresolved resolutions." The language was clinical. The meaning was that children were becoming Kukai in a supervised environment and the facility was studying what happened when they did.

---

## The Three

Among the children who could not Awaken — despite sustained testing, despite every escalation the facility attempted — were three:

**Ku Mochizuki.** His father's facility. His father's test subject. He knew the building's every routine and ignored all of it, focused instead on the small mathematics of daily survival — which staff were kind, which hours were quieter, which rooms were never checked after a certain time. He did not express fear visibly. He expressed nothing visibly. He was eleven years old and already very good at this.

**Kashi Kizu.** Present in the facility through circumstances that have not been fully documented. What is known: he was there when Ku was there, he could not Awaken through any of the facility's methods, and he was observant in a way that the facility's staff noted in their records as unusual. Not distressed. Watching. Filing things away.

**The girl.** Her name does not appear in any facility record that has survived. Whether this is because it was never recorded, because records were destroyed, or because something else explains the absence is not known. She was there. She was friends with Ku and Kashi in the way children become friends in places where friendship is the only thing that belongs to them. The staff called her by a number. Ku and Kashi called her something else. Neither of them uses the word anymore.

---

## The Day

The alarms started before any of them were at their test stations.

Ku and Kashi were together when it happened — the specific corridor, the specific hour, the specific absence of the girl from where she should have been. They ran. The hallways were empty in a way that the facility never was, which meant something had already happened that had moved everyone to somewhere else.

When they turned the corner, they found her.

She was suspended by the neck in Ren Mochizuki's grip. A bloody knife on the floor below her feet — not in his hand, not in hers, simply there, present, part of what had already occurred. Ren's expression was not angry. It was the expression of a man confirming something he had long suspected. He looked at the two of them watching from the corridor entrance and did not let go.

Then the girl began to convulse.

What happened next was the fastest Kukai transformation either of them had ever seen, before or since. The Pressure that had been building in her — through every test, every escalation, every day in that building that was designed to accumulate exactly this — resolved in the only direction left. She absorbed Ren Mochizuki before he could process that what was happening was happening.

Kashi did not move. He watched with the specific quality of attention that someone gives a thing they are trying to memorise completely.

Ku grabbed him and ran.

---

## After

The facility did not continue operating after that day. Whether it was formally shut down, whether the remaining staff dispersed independently, whether someone with the authority to close it chose to — the records do not say clearly. The building exists in some form. No one uses it officially. The Tidal Plate's official documentation does not acknowledge it ever existed.

The children who were in the facility at the time of the incident are not accounted for in any surviving record.

---

## What Ku Took From It

Ku took two things.

The first: the knowledge that the facility existed, that it was funded by interests connected to the K.E.F. and the Sovereign's Crown, and that the documentation of what it did was likely held somewhere he could not reach. This knowledge shaped everything he did afterward — joining the K.E.F. not out of loyalty but because proximity to the institution was the only way to understand what the institution was.

The second: the name. The Black Box — the device that records everything up to the moment of the crash. He took it and made it into a training method. A diagnostic tool. Something given to young awakened people in the dark, asking them to make something visible from nothing. The name was originally a cruelty, applied by a man who thought it was clever. Ku made it into the first lesson he teaches anyone worth teaching. This is not forgiveness. It is reclamation. The distinction matters to him, even if he would not explain it that way.

---

## What Kashi Took From It

Kashi took a conclusion.

He watched the girl — who had been tested past every limit the facility attempted, whose Pressure had been manufactured deliberately, whose bond with her Reiva had been stressed until there was nowhere left for it to go — transform into something that destroyed the man who had done all of it to her. He watched an eleven-year-old girl who could not Awaken through any of the facility's methods Awaken at the end in the only way available to her, and in doing so, end the person who had been the source of everything.

He called this a victory. He has not changed his assessment.

The girl became a Kukai and destroyed the thing that needed destroying. Ku saw tragedy. Kashi saw evidence. The same corridor, the same moment, and they walked out of it with completely opposite understandings of what they had seen. They have not agreed on it since.
