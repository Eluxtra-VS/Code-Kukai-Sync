> _"The tide takes what it wants. The fog keeps the rest."_

## Overview

The **Tidal Plate** is a coastal region built on the bones of what was once called _Perish Island_ — a place that earned its name long before it became the dumping ground for the unwanted. Today it is home to the descendants of the original [[Tenebri]], the outcasts and rebels who built something real out of exile.

---

## History

After the fall of the [[World History|Seven]], the world's authorities gathered every identifiable relative of the Tenebri bloodlines and banished them here. The expectation was that the island would contain them through attrition — the tides, the fog, the isolation.

Instead, **civilisation took hold**. The descendants didn't die. They built docks, fishing traditions, oral culture, and a community defined by the fact that the world didn't want them.

---

## Identity & Culture

- **Outsiders by birth.** Every person in the Tidal Plate carries an inherited stigma — not for anything they've done, but for what their ancestors were.
- **Seen as rebels or criminals** by the broader world, particularly [[Sovereign's Crown]], even when they've done nothing wrong.
- **Stubborn pride** runs deep. Tidal Plate people don't explain themselves to outsiders. They don't justify their existence.
- **Oral tradition is strong** — stories of the sea, warnings about the fog, local superstitions that are actually [[Code]]-adjacent wisdom passed down without its original context.

---

## Geography & Environment

- **Coastal with mixed elevations.** The shoreline and dock level are low and flood-prone, while the main southeast/east settlement sits on a higher cliff terrace. Tides are powerful and unpredictable.
- **Perpetual fog** in the early mornings and late evenings — visibility drops dramatically, and sounds carry strangely. Locals navigate by habit.
- **Dock culture is central.** Fishing is the primary economy. The docks are the social hub — arguments, gossip, deals, and legends all happen there.
- **[[Drift]] signature:** Grief, stoicism, and quiet endurance. Relics found in the Tidal Plate often reflect themes of loss and silent strength.

---

## Internal Geography — Working Layout

The Plate's inhabited southeast/east side is elevated rather than uniformly low. Kaito's neighborhood sits on an east-facing cliff terrace: the road and houses occupy the upper level, a protected sidewalk follows the cliff edge, and large public stairs descend to the cargo/fishing dock below. The dock is where boats, fishermen, crates, and incoming trade traffic arrive; it is not at the same elevation as the homes.

The central district lies toward the Plate's northeast. One primary road runs from the southeast/east settlement through the central district and onward to the capital region, making the Plate's public centre reachable without requiring a maze of roads. Smaller paths branch inland toward the community centre, abandoned structures, and the wooded lake route.

This internal arrangement is a working story map. The broader regional outline and exact distances remain flexible, but the elevation relationship between Kaito's house, the cliff road, the stairs, and the dock should stay consistent in scenes.

## The Tidal Plate and Kyumin Activity

- The plate has a **disproportionately high concentration of [[Kyumin]]** — bloodline sensitivity runs in Tenebri descendants.
- [[Kukai]] activity near the Tidal Plate is higher than average. The Drift signature draws them, and the unawakened population is a rich feeding ground.
- Local legends about "white hands in the fog" and "eyes that don't blink" are [[Kukai Shades]] being described without the vocabulary to name them.
- "**Listen nights**" — what locals call extremely low-tide nights when the fog recedes slightly — are considered dangerous by tradition, though few know why. In truth, these are nights when Kukai Shade activity peaks.

---

## The Lie They're Told

Children born in the Tidal Plate are not told the truth about why they are there.

Instead they are raised on a story: the rest of the world **needs** the Tidal Plate. The land is rich with resources. Other plates depend on what comes from here. The Tidal Plate is not the exile — it is the foundation. Being born here is a privilege, not a punishment.

It is delivered with the same warmth and conviction as any childhood myth. Most children accept it for the same reason children accept any story told by people they trust — because the alternative is that the adults in their life lied to them about something important, and that is hard to hold.

---

## The Cracks

The story doesn't hold up to observation. There are no significant resources being exported. Ships from the other plates don't come often. The "importance" that was promised doesn't manifest in how other plates treat Tidal Plate people when they encounter them — which is mostly with contempt, or simply no recognition at all.

Children notice. They are told they are misreading it. They are told the trade routes are complicated, the relationships are complex, the full picture takes time to understand. Most children accept this too.

Some don't.

---

## Kaito's Group

Kaito, Hitori, Mel, and Aiden are the original core — the ones who didn't accept it. [[Ku Mochizuki]] and [[Aoki Sora]] join later once the group meets them; they are not part of the original four.

Not because they are special — because they spent enough time together, asking questions out loud, to notice that the answers were always thin. They don't yet know the real reason their ancestors were sent here. They don't know about the [[World History|Seven]], the banishment, or what their bloodlines actually carry.

What they know is that they were lied to. What they want is the truth.

---

## How the Truth Arrives — In Pieces

The group never receives a single clean revelation. The real history of the Tidal Plate arrives **in fragments** — a detail here, a contradiction there, an old person who says something they shouldn't, a document that doesn't match the story, a reaction from someone in [[Sovereign's Crown]] that doesn't fit the version they were raised on.

Each fragment doesn't prove anything on its own. But each one adds to a **suspicion that doesn't go away** — and it's that suspicion, more than any single discovery, that drives them forward. They keep looking not because they found a clue but because something consistently feels wrong, and they are the people who refuse to be told that wrongness is their misunderstanding.

The full picture eventually clicks. But it clicks the way things click when you've been building a case for a long time — suddenly, and then obviously, and then with the quiet weight of knowing you were right to keep pulling on it.

> _The lie about the Tidal Plate is the first place Kaito's group learns that adults will construct a comfortable fiction and enforce it rather than tell children something hard. It makes everything that follows legible in a way it wouldn't be otherwise._

## Thematic Role

The Tidal Plate represents **surviving despite everything**. It is the setting that says: you can build a life somewhere the world threw you away, and that life can be real and worth protecting. It also represents the cost of cultural suppression — people here endure because they were never given the option not to, and that same endurance slowly becomes the [[Code]]'s trap.
