## Short Definition

The **Luz Tree** stands at the centre of the [[Oscuro]]. It is the origin point of all [[Reiva]] — converting the accumulated [[Pressure]] that seeps in from the human world into sentient sparks of light, releasing them as falling leaves. Every leaf that falls is a new Reiva born.

---

## Key Facts

- **Every leaf = a new-born Reiva.** The moment a leaf detaches and falls, a Reiva comes into being — aware, sentient, and immediately subject to the Oscuro's pressure to find a vessel.
- **The tree feeds on human Pressure.** Emotional weight that goes unresolved in the physical world bleeds through into the Oscuro and sustains the Luz Tree. In this sense, the suffering and tension of the human world is directly productive — it is the fuel that keeps new Reiva being born.
- **Reiva must leave before the Oscuro corrupts them.** The tree produces life. The environment around it — particularly the lower levels, where [[Kukai Shades]] dwell — works against that life. The Luz Tree gives, and the Oscuro's depths take. Every Reiva is born into a race it doesn't fully understand yet.

---

## What It Looks Like

The Luz Tree should feel _ancient and impossible_ — too large, too still, too bright in the upper [[Oscuro]] layers where everything else is dim. Its light is the primary source of warmth in the entire plane. Moving away from it means moving away from the only reliable light source in existence here.

Its leaves drift downward constantly — slowly, in no hurry, as if the tree itself does not feel the urgency that its children are born into.

---

## Thematic Role

The Luz Tree is the answer to where [[Reiva]] come from — but it raises the more uncomfortable question of what they are _made from_. Every Reiva is built from human Pressure. From grief, guilt, fear, longing, unresolved anger, and suppressed love. The Reiva that bonds with a host is, in some sense, a crystallisation of everything that host and people like them have been unable to say.

That framing is not stated directly in the story. But it is true, and it is worth holding when writing bond scenes.
