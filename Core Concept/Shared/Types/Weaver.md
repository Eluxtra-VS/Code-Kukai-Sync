# Weaver

> _"They carry their power inside. Every technique costs a piece of them."_

## Short Definition

**Weavers** are hosts who weave [[Luz]] into parts of their own body — called **Threads** — because their psychological response to [[Pressure]] is _inward_: they blame themselves and attempt to change themselves to be better. Their power is _internal_ — turned back against the self.

> **[[Akarui]] are External. Weavers are Internal. 

---

## How They Work

### Pressure Resolution

When a [[Kyumin]] first resolves Pressure and the [[Reiva]] awakens, those who **internalise** — who turn Pressure back on themselves, who believe the problem is something _they_ must physically correct — become Weavers.

This is a psychological orientation: Weavers look inward. They modify. Their power is not projected into the world — it is embedded in their body, which is both their greatest resource and their greatest cost.

### Luz Channeling

- Weavers route [[Luz]] **into specific body systems via Threads**.
- This contrasts with Akarui (Luz projected outward) and [[Buki]] users (Luz into objects).
- The body part chosen reflects their deepest insecurity — what they believe is the _root_ of their Pressure.

---

## Thread

Every Weaver has a **Thread** — the Luz-infused body part that defines their technique and serves as the focal point for their Class at its highest expression.

- **Determined by insecurity:** The body part that a Weaver believes, consciously or not, is what needs to be fixed, strengthened, or corrected.
- Examples: hands (guilt over action), eyes (need to see/know), vocal cords (silence used as armour), legs (need to escape or be faster), heart (emotional suppression).
- **Named technique** tied to that body part (e.g., "Stitchgrip" for hands).

---

## Class Access — Passive and Active

Weavers have **full access to their Class** across their entire body — but in two distinct modes:

### Passive (Whole Body)

- The Weaver's Class operates as a **constant, passive field** across the entire body — no Luz cost, no debuff.
- **Vigor:** Permanent baseline enhancement to speed, strength, and durability across the whole body.
- **Pulse:** Can emit Pulse energy from **any point on the body** — resonance, burst on contact, or redirection of incoming energy. Flexible and unpredictable, but not at peak power outside the Thread.
- **Forge:** The body is malleable — surface hardening, density shifts, minor structural reshaping from any body point. Nothing leaves the body; no independent constructs.

### Active Thread (Amplified)

- When the Thread body part activates, the Class expression at that point is **significantly stronger** — in the same way a focused beam is stronger than ambient light.
- **Vigor Thread:** The Thread part reaches a drastically higher physical ceiling. Force reverberates _through_ the target past the point of impact — the damage continues after contact. A leg-Thread Vigor Weaver at passive is faster than most; when the Thread fires, the leg operates at a level that makes the rest of their own body look slow.
- **Pulse Thread:** All three Pulse modes — resonance, burst, and redirection — operate simultaneously at maximum intensity through the Thread. At Unravel, the Thread's resonance grows strong enough to affect the immediate surrounding space without contact: a pressure shockwave that extends slightly outward. This is the **only exception** to the Weaver no-release rule. It is rare, Thread-exclusive, and costs heavily.
- **Forge Thread:** The Thread body part undergoes **complete transformation** — it _becomes_ something else entirely. Not a construct built from it; the part itself reforms. A Thread hand might become a blade, a crushing mass, a lattice of hardened Luz-fused structure. A Thread leg becomes a piston, a spike, an anchoring root. The transformation goes deeper and holds longer than anything the passive system can produce. At Unravel, transformations can be sustained and layered — at the risk of the Thread consuming the part entirely if pushed past the limit.
- **Debuff cost applies on Thread activation**, not on passive use. Scales with how intensely the Thread is pushed.

## Weavers vs. Akarui — The Core Distinction

||[[Akarui]]|Weaver|
|---|---|---|
|**Direction**|External — power projects outward|Internal — power routes through the body|
|**Class passive**|None — all use is active|Yes — whole body benefits at baseline|
|**Class peak**|Manifestation (full external expression)|Thread activation (focal point amplification)|
|**Cost**|Luz per use|Thread debuff on activation; passive is free|

## Progression

|Stage|State|
|---|---|
|**Loom**|Volatile; harsh immediate debuffs after use|
|**Seam**|Reduced debuffs; must be actively managed|
|**Unravel**|Full power; high cost and catastrophic risk if misused|

_(See [[Progression]] for full detail.)_

### Loom

- Harsh immediate debuffs follow every use: numbness, motor control loss, blurred vision, cognitive strain.
- Powerful but punishing — each use is a sacrifice.

### Seam

- Debuffs are reduced but must be actively managed.
- The Weaver has learned to pace themselves; the Thread is becoming more natural.

### Unravel

- The Thread operates at full power.
- The Weaver risks catastrophic consequences if they push beyond their limit: the Thread can consume the body part entirely.

---

## Drawbacks

- **Loom:** Severe immediate physical debuffs after each use.
- **Seam:** Reduced but persistent debuffs; cannot be ignored.
- **Reassigning body parts** — changing which body part is Threaded — is extremely costly and causes permanent damage unless a long ritual is completed first.

---

## Psychology

Weavers are **tragic** by design. Their power comes from self-blame. Their signature move is wounding themselves to fight.

Their storylines are emotionally heavy — their arcs are about **learning to stop blaming themselves** as the source of their strength. True growth, for a Weaver, means reconfiguring their Thread from a weapon of self-punishment into something else entirely.

---

## Narrative Role

Weavers are intense, story-rich characters whose dramatic beats centre on the question: _what would you stop sacrificing yourself for?_

They carry the themes of the [[Code]] most visibly — they have internalised the world's lesson that strength means not showing weakness so deeply that they are literally building weakness into their own body to compensate.
