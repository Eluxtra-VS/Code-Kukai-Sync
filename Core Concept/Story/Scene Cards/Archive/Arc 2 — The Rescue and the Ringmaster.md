# Arc 2 — The Rescue and the Ringmaster

The restructured back half of Arc 2 (Aiden is captured by the Ku-Clowns; Kaito is taken by a Tenebri and speculated dead; the group fights through a monochrome circus to find him; the Announcement closes the arc, followed by the sting. Connecting cards: [[Arc 2 — The Knot and the Announcement]]. 

---

### Why Niko Juul takes Kaito

[[Niko Juul]] was shopping at the Ku-Clown site — specimens, relics, signatures. Canon: Rapt consumes *spoken signature names*, Divergence is untouchable only while unnamed, and Niko's documented approach is manufacturing urgency to force the naming. Kaito is the locked door he cannot open. He intercepts the rescue, takes Kaito, and leaves the Ku-Clowns nothing. Consequences: (1) the naming-scene fuse is lit on-page; (2) Ku establishes a names-ban at the wreckage as "someone took him that collects what he hears" without naming the Tenebri; (3) Niko delivers one unexplained line about a former Tenebri who left. (Daiki,Alice reveal stays early Arc 4).

### The Five Stages (masked Kyumin) 

Five masked Kyumin, each carrying a Relic from a human who died stuck in one stage of grief. The masks are relic-white housings over hand/eye/heart Relics and deform toward the dead face as each Stage draws power — and each stage deforms differently: **Denial's** features smooth out until the mask is nobody's; **Anger's** cracks and chars; **Bargaining's** smile widens past where a smile should stop; **Depression's** sinks, hollowing; **Acceptance's** never moves at all — it is already a face at rest. They are ushers, not killers: their job is to seat the living. Every Stage's power is **ring-bounded**: a visible circle inside which one law holds. Rings cannot overlap; crossing a boundary frees the crosser only; the lawless seams between rings are patrolled; Relic cooldowns (see [[Relic]]) are real — a Stage caught between uses is just an exhausted masked person. The cast fights the stages **in grief order**, because the show is structured that way: the venue runs its patrons through the sequence on purpose.

**House law (critical distinction):** "movement that does not belong to anyone is a trade" is not Bargaining's ring — it is the troupe's **claimed-space authority**, the house rule of a Ku-Clown venue, brokered by the house and never by guest request. Bargaining's personal ring obeys the same ring rules as everyone else's. This is why swaps can move people across the whole venue while rings still cannot overlap, and why nobody can simply ask for a useful swap. Separately: **Rapt takes claimed selves, not words** — a spoken common-technique name is just vocabulary and feeds Rapt nothing, but Ku does not know that for certain, so his names-ban covers everything. The over-caution is characterization.

| Stage | Relic (body part) | The human it came from | Ring law |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Denial** | Jaw | A man who kept setting the table for someone the tide took | **"It didn't happen."** Inside the ring, outcomes unwritten, and the law is symmetric: nothing done inside sticks. Wounds close, broken constructs unbreak, trails and passages erase — and strikes un-happen as fast as they land. You cannot be hurt inside it; you also cannot be saved inside it. A stalemate zone where nothing happens, which is the point |
| **Anger** | Fist | A woman who burned her own life down rather than lose it slowly | **"Everything is against you."** Heat and repulsion; the ring shoves, herds, and consumes cover |
| **Bargaining** | Tongue | A stage manager who spent forty years opening shows nobody thanked her for, and offered anything for one more curtain | **"Trade me."** Within her ring, any two things swap places — and every swap takes something small and unnamed in return: a sound, an hour of sleep, the words someone was about to say. The soothing voice. House-brokered swaps (see house law above) are the venue-scale version |
| **Depression** | Eye | A woman who saw the thing that drained the color out of her | **"It's too heavy."** Inside her ring: weight doubles and color drains to grey. Outside it, her **ambient** reach is passive: the venue's resting dimness, no law, just less light. Crossing her line returned color for the crossers only, and only while the ring held them |
| **Acceptance** | Heart | Someone who made peace too early and let go of everything, including the will to live | **"It's over."** Inside the ring things conclude: fights end, pain stops, doors open. The resets are for guests — the Stages refuse them for themselves (Acceptance offers, never serves). The mask never deforms. The exit is Acceptance's offer, and everyone who has ever walked out through it was never seen again |

**Venue:** the Grey Ring — a dead fairground in a flooded quarry, west [[Tidal Plate]], secluded by washed-out roads. **Ku-Clown property**: a franchise venue the troupe performs in, which is how the K.E.F. learns who was there. Monochrome: grey canvas, white masks, dim light that is Depression's resting radius. The seats face inward, all of them, always. There is no master villain behind the curtain — the troupe is the antagonist, and the show has run longer than anyone in it can remember.

**The teleportation:** Bargaining's swap. The cast is not teleported in — crossing the tree line put them inside a claimed performance space, and "the trees are gone" is the boundary snapping shut. From then on, movement that does not belong to anyone is a trade.

### Imprint (replaces Echo Beat — user canon)

**Imprint is a [[Common Techniques|common technique]]**: see the trail of where a specific Reiva has been, trainable, mastery reveals the person glowing through obstacles. The glow dims under negative emotion — a terrified person is a dying light. Ku Mochizuki replicates it from knowledge of K.E.F.-trained users; most awakened people never bother. Limits on-page: trails fade with time and weather, reading takes stillness and focus, and a cacophony of trails (like Niko's library of stolen capacities) muddies the read. Inside the venue, **Denial erases trails** — the map lies because the venue un-happens what passed through it. Kaito's trail is faint because Kaito is terrified, and Aiden is the one who knows what Kaito's light looks like when it goes small.

## Card D — The Snare and the Theft

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit (replaces the separate capture card and "Sora Captured" as the arc's midpoint catastrophe)
**POV / Emotional Center:** Aiden, then Kaito, then the group
**Pressure Movement:** Shame needs an audience → Aiden goes to earn one back alone and is taken → Kaito goes alone to correct a mistake nobody asked him to correct → the group reads the wreckage as a death
**Power-System Beat:** Aiden performs the whole approach — narrated techniques, big constructs — and every output is watched-quality: loud, hollow, breaking. The Ku-Clowns took Hyoga's notes literally: Aiden's pattern is loud, performative, predictable, so they left the door open. Niko never fights Kaito at all; Rapt does not need to.
**Visual Motif:** Footlights without a stage / a gate too clean to trust / a trail that stops mid-step
**Emotional Thesis:** Two boys walk into the dark for the same reason — an audience and a correction — and only one of them is coming back.

### Beat Breakdown
1. Aftermath of the Ku-Clown notes; the group doubles the watch. Aiden cannot sit in the shame of being read.
2. He almost wakes Hitori on the way out. He doesn't. He announces his exit quietly to nobody, which is the tell.
3. The site is too clean. He performs his approach — big constructs, narrated techniques — and the constructs break exactly like they always do when someone is watching.
4. Someone has been watching the whole time. A net of thin white filament. A mask leaning down: **"The scouts said you'd come alone."**
5. Kaito, meanwhile, has already slipped the doubled watch — a misdirected patrol round, insomnia turned useful — and arrives on the same conclusion from the other side: this is his to fix, silently, before anyone can see him need it.
6. The Ku-Clown site is wrecked in ways that read like something immense passed through. Aiden is out of his net in the chaos and cannot find Kaito anywhere.
7. Niko steps out of the dark. He is polite. He has been listening for weeks. The conversation is bait: describe the ability, offer lives for the name. Kaito cannot give what he does not have. His right eye tries and fails to focus.
8. Niko takes him anyway — a locked door is not a closed invitation. One unexplained line about a former Tenebri who left, and asks after Amori children. Then gone.
9. Aiden crawls out of the wreckage and has to be the one who says it: he was the bait, and Kaito stepped on it.
10. Ku reads the site with Imprint. Kaito's trail is faint — terror dims the glow — and it ends mid-step, drowned under a stranger's cacophony of trails. He also reads what took him: a collector's marks, residue he recognizes and will not explain. Before anyone moves: "Something took him that collects what it hears. From now on, nobody says their ability's name. Don't ask me how I know."
11. Nobody says the word death. Everybody hears it.

### Character Threads
- **Aiden:** The wound walks him into the cage. He gets free by accident and carries the accounting home.
- **Kaito:** His self-correction instinct, aimed at saving someone else, delivers him to the one listener he can never afford.
- **Ku:** Recognizes the marks and imposes the ban without explaining — management as protection, and as another debt his silence is stacking.

### Must-Include
Aiden's capture is his fault by his own mechanics, not bad luck. Kaito evading the doubled watch is shown, not assumed. **Reader-facing tell: the sequence closes on a single panel of Kaito alive in the dark of a moving wagon — dramatic irony over the group's grief.** Niko does not kill what he intends to open; the reader sees it, the cast does not. The Daiki hint is one line, never explained here.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** Ku-Clown Observation (the notes; pattern-reading); Kaito's insomnia.
- **Sets up:** Card E (the ban, the grief); Card F (Imprint); Card I (the conversation); the names-ban price paid in the circus.

## Card E — What Brave Meant

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** Mel and Aiden; the group as chorus
**Pressure Movement:** Mel's care loses its addressee and turns inward into silence → Ku calls Aiden's guilt out as mismanagement → Aiden breaks and redefines bravery
**Power-System Beat:** Mel's Flare will not light: the beacon runs on being seen, and the one who saw her is gone. Aiden's hands shake too hard to build anything while he is performing grief — the small clean shackle only forms later, when he stops performing it, and nobody notices.
**Visual Motif:** A bracelet that does not glow / a needle and strap abandoned mid-stitch / one clean shackle in a fist nobody is looking at
**Emotional Thesis:** Grief makes people honest, because dishonesty is a performance and no one has the strength to keep performing.

### Beat Breakdown
1. **Hyperfunction flash first:** at 3 a.m. Mel is found re-rigging everyone's gear — all of it, perfectly, frantically — and then her hands just stop over Aiden's harness strap, and she walks away mid-task. The silence arrives as a fall, not a switch.
2. Days of nothing after that. No offers, no kit, no chatter. The bracelet stays dark.
3. Hitori holds the group together logistically and it is visibly costing him (his Arc 3 spiral is being pre-loaded here: the anchor holding everyone while drowning).
4. Ku, managing, tells Aiden his guilt is a liability: loud, unfocused, useless to the search. Ku is not wrong. Ku is also not kind.
5. Aiden snaps. The speech (below). The most sincere thing he has ever said, said loud, said falling apart — with one stumble in it, because no one gets through this fluently.
6. Ku does not apologize. A half-beat first: he looks at Aiden, chooses to use the wreckage, and asks the question: "You've watched his Reiva since the rooftop. What does his light do when he's scared?"
7. Aiden answers without hesitating: "It goes small. Then it doubles." (The right eye. Kaito's specific flicker.) Imprint's canon rule says fear dims the glow; the doubling is the trail's *shape*, not its brightness — the right eye's Divergence leak leaves a doubled print even at low light. That is the search parameter: Imprint needs a shape to read for.

### The speech (polished from user draft)

> "No. No, you don't get to manage this one."
> "You want to know what happened? I wanted them to look at me. That's it. That's the whole trick. I walked into the dark so somebody would watch me do it, and they took me, and Kaito came after me anyway. Nobody sent him. Nobody knew. He just came, because that's what he does when one of us is too stupid to ask for help."
> "You all called me brave for going forward. For fighting back. Kaito is a million times braver than I'll ever be, because he didn't do it to look cool. He did it for ME."

### Character Threads
- **Mel:** Hyperfunction collapses into silence; her arc's pressure point arrives early and stays loaded.
- **Aiden:** Redefines bravery against himself. The shackle that forms when he stops performing grief is the thesis arriving without an audience.
- **Ku:** Management fails as comfort and succeeds as method — choosing to extract the glow-shape is the rescue's first act.

### Must-Include
The speech keeps the user's shape and register (including the profanity) and contains one stumble. Ku's callout is reasonable, not cruel. Intercut a light beat or search-montage into the grief stretch so the catastrophe-to-grief run is not unbroken.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** The Announcement's confession machinery; Mel's Flare beacon condition (by absence).
- **Sets up:** Card F; Mel's Arc 3+ depression thread; the reunion.

## Card H — The Circus Fight

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit. The arc's set-piece. Choreography must survive paneling.
**POV / Emotional Center:** The whole cast, rotating
**Pressure Movement:** Confidence → the environment turns → a fake-out victory the venue applauds → separation → the realization they were never fighting, they were being seated
**Power-System Beat:** The Five Stages, ring-bounded, fought **in grief order**. Ring rules on-page: crossing a line frees the crosser only; rings never overlap; the lawless seams are patrolled; cooldowns are real and visible; Denial erases what happens inside it and un-happens mending at its boundary. Mel's light is the tactical key not because her signature reveals anything new but because **it is the only illumination in a Depression world** — where her glow or its reflection reaches, ring lines are readable; where it doesn't, the cast is blind. The cast's counter to every Stage is geometry, not power: cross the line, end the law — for one person.
**Visual Motif:** One color in a grey world / rings like stage lighting / masks bowing / a map that lies
**Emotional Thesis:** A fight where the enemy never once tries to win is a fight you are losing. And a fight through the stages of grief, in order, is a funeral for someone who is still alive.

### The arena (spatial ground truth)

Main ring: roughly sixty meters across, five stage-marks at pentagon points, the cast entering at center. Two overhead maps anchor the sequence: **Map A at Phase 2 open** (rings igniting, five circles, five figures), **Map B at Phase 3 close** (seating complete — the same page rotated: rings as stage lighting, the cast as five tiny spots, seats facing inward). Matchups on entry: **Denial↔Ku** (his Imprint reads keep being unwritten — maddening), **Anger↔Aiden** (the performance duel, pre-loading Phase 3), **Bargaining↔Sora** (you cannot hear a swap coming), **Depression↔Mel** (the thing that eats light against the girl who is one), **Acceptance↔Hitori** (the calm mask against the anchor — the door that opens against the boy who holds).

### The choreography (phase breakdown, manga-panellable)

**Phase 0 — The Mask Change (dread, 2 pages).** The masks deform per their griefs — one smoothing, one cracking, one grinning too wide, one sinking, one perfectly still. Nobody charges yet. The dim eats sound. Open on the program page: crossed-out names.

**Phase 1 — Denial Opens (the charge, 2 pages).** The five come from five directions on their matchups, and the show leads with its first grief. Ku fights Denial and fights fog: Denial unwrites his reads as fast as he makes them, and for the first time since the rooftop crisis the boy who taught himself to see is blind. The concrete version: the flanking route he used three minutes ago does not exist when he tries to leave by it. Sora fights Bargaining and loses ground constantly without losing her feet — a swap needs no wind-up, no rhythm, nothing to hear. Hitori faces Acceptance, who does not fight at all: it walks toward him, slowly, with its palm out, saying nothing, and every instinct Hitori has tells him the palm is honest. His counter is pure retreat-and-observe — he gives ground the whole phase, memorizing the venue while the rest of them bleed for position, which is how he earns the layout the anchor-mark carve depends on. Acceptance never presses; the offer works better on the unharried. Aiden and Anger trade openings, and Anger fights like a *performer* — flourishes, invitations, a theatrical duel offer — while Aiden fights ugly, functional, and visibly unimpressed, which is the sequence's one dry joke: two showmen, and only one of them is ashamed of it. Mel opens her Flare against Depression's ambient dim and the arena gets its first color.

**Phase 2 — Anger, Then Bargaining (rings open, 4 pages).** Stages withdraw to their marks and open their rings. **Map A lands here.** Anger's circle shoves and herds everyone toward the seams; Depression's ring doubles every step and drinks the light beyond Mel's reach; Denial's ring erases the ground the cast already crossed — the map the reader trusted starts deleting itself; the house swaps troupe positions at will; Acceptance's ring just opens a door, calmly, in the middle of the floor, and waits. **The fake-out victory:** Hitori and Mel cross Depression's line together — color comes back, the weight lifts, the law is beaten, the reader learns the rule — and all five masks stop, and *applaud*. Then the house trades them back to the center anyway, and the applause was the price. Sora's "they're herding us" lands here: she saw the applause, not the trap. **Cooldown beat:** Depression, caught between uses, is suddenly just an exhausted masked person — chest heaving, mask sinking — and Ku commits to the sprint at her mark, his one aggressive beat of the whole fight — before the house re-seats her. The futility has a rhythm now.

**Phase 3 — Depression, Then Acceptance (separation and the lying map, 4 pages).** **Map B lands here.** Ku is cut off west behind Denial's ring, his own trail unwriting itself behind him — the route gone, the exits deleted, the tracker sealed inside a law where nothing he does sticks. Sora and Mel end up in a *seam* — the lawless gap between Depression's ring and Anger's — and it is Sora's choice, not chance: she steers Mel into it because swaps and rings do not govern there, and the ushers patrolling its edges are the cost of the one correct read anyone makes all fight. Hitori is alone inside Depression's ring, very still in the grey — it fits him too well. Aiden is alone against Anger in a burning dressing corridor. **Mid-fight, the house fires the lie:** the corridor Aiden memorized swaps places with one across the venue — walls he mapped are somewhere else now, and something he did not notice losing is gone. The map was not wrong; it was traded. **Anger's price:** its ring does not just burn — it pulls. Aiden's rage wants out of him in Anger's register, the shout gathering at the base of his throat as honesty arriving in the wrong language, and he swallows his signature's name mid-swing with a killing opening — the thing you reach for at maximum commitment — and holds it. Same throat that will confess at the arc's end, different thing held: a secret is not a wound. In the grey, when an usher's patrol drifts toward him, Hitori does not swing Aiden's blade — he kneels, carves an anchor mark into the floorboards where he stands, his own mark, so that if her light ever sweeps this far it has something to catch on. The blade pays off as the mark, not the swing. Mel's light sweeps, catches the mark, and reaches into the grey — and from inside it, quiet: "It's you. We're only lost when you stop." Her light steadies. (Micro-resolution; the beacon does not unlock; one person saw her and she let it land for one panel.) **Acceptance's cost:** Hitori's palm-refusal leaves no mark on the mask, but it leaves one on him — for the rest of the arc his hands stay cold, a phantom warmth he keeps flexing for, and nobody notices but the reader. The masks bow.

**Phase 4 — The Bow (release, 1 page).** All five Stages stop at once and bow toward the main stage. Drawing instruction: a five-panel strip repeating Phase 0's mask order left to right — smooth, cracked, grinning, sunken, still — so the grief order is felt twice and becomes the troupe's usable tell. The rings hold. The arena is silent. The soothing voice, through a program-speaker, welcomes the audience to the show. Spread: the cast, separated, small in enormous grey space — and the seats, hundreds of them, all empty and all facing them.

### Character Threads
- **Mel:** Her light is the map, the only color, the reason anyone can see a ring line. Hitori's grey line is the first payment on her beacon condition.
- **Sora:** First to name the herding; fights the unhearable enemy.
- **Aiden:** Fights functional, refuses the duel, swallows the shout. The arc's theme in one phase.
- **Ku:** Blinded by Denial — the tracker who cannot track. His discipline has to become patience, which is the one thing his management never taught him.
- **Hitori:** Faces Acceptance and does not take the palm; maps the venue; the grey fits him and he carves instead of sinking. The grey fitting him too well is planted for Arc 3.

### Must-Include
The five never kill and never menace past professional courtesy — the horror is being handled. Ring lines visible only within reach of Mel's light. No signature names spoken by anyone; show what the silence costs. Bargaining fires mid-fight and the map lies. The fight runs in grief order and the text should let the reader feel the shape without naming it.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** Mel's beacon condition; Aiden's unwatched register; the herding pattern from Card F; Hitori's anchor role; the common-techniques layer (Imprint's Denial counter).
- **Sets up:** Card I (Kaito is above, watching this); Card J; Card K (Bargaining's biggest trade); Hitori's Arc 3 spiral; Mel's beacon unlock; the toll's reveal.

## Card J — Found

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** The group; Kaito; Mel last
**Pressure Movement:** A body-sized hope → it is him → Aiden's threat of love → Mel's collapse into speech → the hold nobody breaks
**Power-System Beat:** Minimal. The group finds him because Aiden, running on fumes, builds one last unwatched thing — a small stair of interlocking platforms to the dressing-room level, plain and load-bearing, made for someone else's rescue. No announcement. The shackles hold.
**Visual Motif:** A door in a grey wall / a hug that hits like a tackle / one color returning to a grey world
**Emotional Thesis:** People come back, and the ones who stayed have to survive their own relief.

### Beat Breakdown
1. Regrouping behind a Denial seam; Mel's light dying; Aiden's stair; the door.
2. Kaito, alive, shook, still half-frozen from the conversation.
3. Aiden goes quiet. Kaito looks at him. Aiden crosses the room and hugs him hard enough to hurt, tears he does not hide, then wipes his eyes and tells him: if Kaito ever tries saving his sorry ass again, he'll kill him himself.
