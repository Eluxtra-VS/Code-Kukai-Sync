# Arcs 2–3 — Pursuit and Tenebri: Scene Cards

These cards convert the existing treatment into modular production units. They are not full prose scenes. They define what must emotionally change so the prose can remain flexible.

## 1. Adjacent Warmth

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** Kaito and Mel
**Pressure Movement:** Care is offered → both redirect it → one small part of the care remains accepted
**Power-System Beat:** Kaito's left-eye Rate appears in low-stakes training; Mel's Anchor responds to voluntary care
**Visual Motif:** Shared food / hands almost touching / motion briefly slowed
**Emotional Thesis:** Intimacy begins when neither person successfully turns care into a task.

### Start Image
Mel places food beside Kaito without asking whether he is hungry.

### End Image
Kaito uses Rate to slow a falling object, then lets Mel catch it instead of correcting the outcome himself.

### Beat Breakdown
1. Mel notices Kaito has not eaten and offers something practical.
2. Kaito turns the offer into a question about her own needs.
3. Mel answers the question but does not accept the deflection as the whole conversation.
4. Training begins; Kaito's Rate activates too early and makes his movement arrive out of sequence.
5. Mel creates a simple Anchor route that gives him a second attempt.
6. Kaito succeeds by allowing the situation to remain imperfect.

### Character Threads
- **Kaito:** First small interruption in the revision cycle.
- **Mel:** Offers care without making acceptance proof of her worth.

### Must-Include
No romantic declaration. The care should land halfway, which is progress.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** Arc 1 care-kit and Kaito's first misdirected Awakening.
- **Sets up:** Flare, insomnia escalation, romance through repeated failed deflection.

Canon status: WORKING. Production status: NEEDS PROSE.

**Visual Motif:** Ceiling lines / heartbeat spacing / unfinished sentence
**Emotional Thesis:** Advice about endurance becomes harmful when it gives a person a better vocabulary for hiding.

### Start Image
Ku gives a practical instruction: endure the next ten minutes.

### End Image
Kaito hears the same word as a verdict; Ku's heartbeat rhythm breaks once and he pretends it did not.

### Beat Breakdown
1. A training situation requires the group to endure discomfort.
2. Ku uses the language of control and restraint because it is the language he knows.
3. Kaito asks whether enduring is supposed to make the feeling disappear.
4. Ku answers technically, avoiding the personal question underneath.
5. Kaito mislabels his reaction as anger; his output flickers.
6. Ku's Beat-Break loses sync when he suppresses the answer he almost gives.
7. Neither apologises. The silence has changed meaning.

### Character Threads
- **Kaito:** Begins to see the misread Code as something he has internalised, not just culture around him.
- **Ku:** His management becomes visible as a cost rather than competence alone.

No full Ku backstory yet. The scene earns a future disclosure by failing to resolve this one.

### Setup / Payoff

## 7. The Cover-Up Witness

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** Sora and the child witness
**Pressure Movement:** Institutional procedure appears impersonal → Sora recognizes a human life inside the order → obedience becomes impossible to complete
**Power-System Beat:** Sora's Hands Thread begins a repair she cannot finish; Mend responds to the child's immediate need rather than Sora's guilt
**Visual Motif:** Sealed evidence bag / small hand / a door left unlatched
**Emotional Thesis:** A system becomes monstrous when it teaches ordinary people to call a child a problem.

### Start Image
A K.E.F. incident report labels the witness as an exposure risk, with the child's name reduced to a line that can be crossed out.

### End Image
Sora leaves a door unlatched and steps away from the child instead of taking the final action.

### Beat Breakdown
1. The group witnesses a contained K.E.F. cover-up: records of deliberate Crown neglect are being rewritten as a routine relocation or casualty correction.
2. A child who saw the records or the operation becomes the remaining civilian witness. The child is ordinary, frightened, and not a chosen-one device.
3. Sora is identified by the K.E.F. system as the operative who can complete the order cleanly.
4. She approaches the child using the same calm procedure she once trusted.
5. The child recognizes that Sora is afraid and asks a small, human question rather than pleading for their life.
6. Sora reaches the decisive position and freezes; Mend activates reflexively on a torn sleeve or broken door, but she cannot repair the moral break by repairing the object.
7. Ku arrives and refuses the order without turning the moment into a speech.
8. Sora creates the appearance that the child has disappeared or died, then sends them through the unlatched route.

### Character Threads
- **Sora:** Her self-blame becomes a moral choice; she cannot pretend she refused immediately, and she cannot complete the order.
- **Ku:** Recognizes the Black Box pattern in the institution's language and crosses from interference into open refusal.
- **Child witness:** Survives as a recurring supporting character; name and later identity remain OPEN.

### Must-Include
- The child survives.
- The cover-up remains limited and plausible; it does not instantly expose the entire world.
- Sora genuinely tried to obey before stopping.
- The child is not a supernatural prodigy or exposition machine.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** K.E.F. institutional procedure, Sora's self-blame, Ku's unfinished explanations, Crown neglect.
- **Sets up:** Sora's capture, Ku's confession, the child's later survival as living evidence.

### Notes
Canon status: WORKING; Production status: NEEDS PROSE. The child's exact name, family history, and future reappearance remain OPEN.

### Continuity Bridge
After refusing the order, Sora uses the unlatched route to rejoin the group rather than disappearing into a separate escape. The K.E.F. identifies her deviation through the altered report, the failed witness confirmation, and the route audit. Her capture is therefore the institutional consequence of the same refusal, not a second unrelated plot mechanism.

## 8. Sora Captured

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** Sora / the group
**Pressure Movement:** Sora believes she can contain the consequences alone → the institution reclaims her as a liability → the group must choose whether truth is worth rescue
**Power-System Beat:** Mend cannot solve a containment system; Sora's Hands Thread becomes unreliable when she treats captivity as proof of personal failure
**Visual Motif:** Redacted file / hands behind transparent restraint / a route map with one missing line
**Emotional Thesis:** A person cannot repair a system by offering their own body as the missing piece.

### Start Image
The K.E.F. issues a clean report: the witness is gone, the incident is closed, and Sora is listed as an operative who deviated from protocol.

### End Image
Sora sees the group through containment glass and refuses to mouth an apology before anyone asks for one.

### Beat Breakdown
1. The K.E.F. reconstructs the cover-up as a successful operation while quietly marking Sora for retrieval.
2. Sora realizes the child survived but understands that any visible contact will endanger them.
3. A K.E.F. team intercepts the group through procedure, not a sudden ambush; the operative calls Sora a compromised asset.
4. Sora is separated and taken because she knows the internal route, the report language, and the order's real purpose.
5. The group debates escape versus rescue. Kaito wants a plan, Mel wants to keep everyone alive, Aiden wants to act before the window closes, and Ku says nothing.
6. Sora attempts Mend on the restraint system and makes it function long enough to reveal a route, but the feedback locks her hands.
7. Through the glass, she begins to apologize. She stops before the word finishes.
8. The group chooses rescue, not because Sora is innocent, but because abandoning her would reproduce the K.E.F.'s logic.

### Character Threads
- **Sora:** Begins separating accountability from self-erasure.
- **Kaito:** Sees that a clean tactical choice can still be morally incomplete.
- **Ku:** Is forced toward the truth he has been withholding.

### Must-Include
- The K.E.F. remains competent and institutionally credible.
- Sora is captured for knowledge and liability, not because the plot needs a hostage.
- The child remains protected off-screen during the rescue.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** The Cover-Up Witness and Sora's former K.E.F. expertise.
- **Sets up:** Ku's confession and the group's new trust condition.

### Notes
Canon status: WORKING; Production status: NEEDS PROSE. The exact facility and rescue route remain flexible.

## 9. Ku's Confession Under Pressure

**Arc:** Arc 2 — The Pursuit
**POV / Emotional Center:** Ku
**Pressure Movement:** Silence appears protective → silence becomes the institution's weapon → Ku tells the truth because the group chooses to hear it
**Power-System Beat:** Beat-Break loses sync while Ku gives the managed version; the rhythm returns only when he names his refusal and the Black Box history plainly
**Visual Motif:** Heartbeat through a wall / unfinished sentence / a Black Box shape made from emergency light
**Emotional Thesis:** Confession is not absolution; it is the first condition for a relationship to become accurate.

### Start Image
Ku stands before a containment door and gives the group a route without explaining why he knows it.

### End Image
He says the name Black Box aloud in the open, and nobody pretends the word is only a diagnostic tool.

### Beat Breakdown
1. Ku directs the rescue with precise knowledge of K.E.F. procedure.
2. Kaito asks how he knows the facility. Ku gives a partial answer and Beat-Break stutters.
3. The group reaches Sora through a sequence of physical and procedural counters rather than brute force.
4. The child witness's survival is revealed only as a reason the order cannot be allowed to stand, not as a full exposition dump.
5. Ku admits he was a Black Box subject and that the facility made people into outcomes. He does not yet define Kashi's exact role; that relationship remains open for a later confrontation.
6. He admits he has been withholding the K.E.F. order because he believed carrying the truth alone would protect Sora and the group.
7. Beat-Break breaks completely when he says he does not know whether management ever counted as healing.
8. He chooses the next strike without hiding the feeling underneath it; the rhythm returns cleanly enough to open the rescue route.
9. Sora is freed. She and Ku do not forgive each other or resolve the order; they stand in a more accurate relationship.

### Character Threads
- **Ku:** Moves from protective vagueness to accountable truth.
- **Sora:** Is rescued without being absolved; her own confession remains a later choice.
- **Group:** Chooses to stay together while making truth a condition of trust.

### Must-Include
- Ku's confession cannot solve his trauma.
- The Black Box name must carry both horror and reclamation.
- The child survives, but their exact identity remains protected and OPEN.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** Black Box Test, Ku's management, K.E.F. familiarity, unfinished Arc 2 explanations.
- **Sets up:** Hyoga's accurate disclosure in Arc 3, Sora's later order confession, the group's deliberate truth pact.

## 15. Hitori's White-Wing Recognition

**Arc:** Arc 3 — The Tenebri
**POV / Emotional Center:** The original group
**Pressure Movement:** Absence is treated as a solvable delay → the group encounters a recognizable presence → recognition is returned without reunion
**Power-System Beat:** Hitori's Kukai enters a brief coherent window; human Hitori is gone, and the window cannot restore him
**Visual Motif:** White wings in fog / a familiar turn of the head / the group framed behind an empty anchor position
**Emotional Thesis:** Recognition can survive transformation without restoring the person who was recognized.

### Start Image
The group assembles in the familiar composition and finds only fog moving through the empty place where Hitori should be.

### End Image
The white-winged Kukai turns toward them, recognizes them instantly, then folds the wings and drifts away before they can reach him.

### Beat Breakdown
1. Everyone initially assumes Hitori is late; each person performs a different part of his old routine.
2. A distant Kukai silhouette appears with unmistakable white wings. The characters do not know what they are seeing.
3. Kaito says Hitori's name or repeats an ordinary group phrase.
4. The Kukai turns immediately. The recognition is not a human reunion; it is a coherent window in which old relational patterns remain legible.
5. For one brief visual sequence, the Kukai's posture echoes Hitori's old way of standing near people who needed him.
6. The Kukai begins to approach, then stops. The group reaches toward it.
7. It avoids them—not from hatred or conscious shame, but because the remnant pattern recognizes the group as the gravitational field of obligation it can no longer survive.
8. The wings fold. The Kukai drifts into the fog.
9. No one can honestly say whether Hitori chose to leave or whether the coherent window closed before choice could exist. The reader understands both truths are painful.

### Character Threads
- **Hitori:** Human Hitori is gone permanently; recognition is a fragment, not recoverability.
- **Kaito:** Tries to solve the encounter and cannot convert recognition into restoration.
- **Mel:** Wants to pursue but understands that pursuit may repeat the group’s old claim on Hitori.

- It avoids them rather than attacking or returning.

- Do not reveal the full Relic function here.
- Do not imply that human Hitori can simply be restored.

### Setup / Payoff
- **Pays off:** Anchor motif, Hitori's quiet spiral, the group’s dependence, the Arc 3 absence.
