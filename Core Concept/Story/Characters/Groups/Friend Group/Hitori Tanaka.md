## Pressure

Hitori wants to be free, free in the simplest sense: no obligations, no one depending on him, somewhere quiet, not needed by anyone. He has wanted this as long as he can remember and has never once moved toward it. If he resented the people he loved, leaving would be easy. He loves them more than he wants what he wants, and he has never figured out what to do with that except keep going.

## Link to Ability

There's no signature to link. His Reiva has watched him for years, waiting for a resolution: a choice about what *he* wants instead of what he stays for. It never comes. Every time the moment approaches, he looks at the people around him and stays. The Reiva can't prevent what follows; it can only be present for it. What he leaves behind becomes his only power: [[Relic|Wings]] that let someone else move like absolute freedom.

## Backstory

Nephew of Yeshi Tanaka, who worked the Gear District for years before his wife's death finished whatever was left of him. Hitori grew up watching his uncle go quieter, answering check-ins without saying much. He knows his uncle is struggling. At story start he doesn't know the full reason, and he doesn't know Yeshi is Tenebri.

## Design

The anchor image everywhere: his imagery, how others describe him, the weight in every scene. For a long time it reads as stable and positive. Then comes the pull-back reveal before his threshold: the anchor is still there, but so is Hitori behind it, dragging it. As Kukai he presents a drifting absence of urgency, white folded wings on his back in every form, the shape of something that was supposed to carry him somewhere and never did.

# Full Detail

## The Uncle

What he knows: Yeshi has been different since his wife died, and the Gear District wore him down for years before that. What he doesn't know at story start: Yeshi is Tenebri.

## The Spiral

When Ku and Sora join the group they bring Tenebri context: seven sins, members, history. Hitori starts connecting fragments he wasn't looking for: the withdrawal, the timing, the things said and unsaid. He confirms it, and he isn't wrong. Nothing dramatic follows, just a quiet reframing of everything he understood about family and weight. This alone doesn't break him. He has been accumulating weight for a long time, though, and this arrives when there's no more room for it. He becomes Kukai not long after.

## Why He Does Not Awaken

His Reiva waits for him to choose what he wants over what he stays for. That moment never comes. The Reiva watches the Pressure climb past Awakening into the territory that only produces one outcome. It doesn't abandon him; it can only be present.

## The Transformation

There's no explosion and no catastrophic break. The accumulated weight, every freedom chosen away for the people he loved, passes any threshold he can survive as the person who made those choices. Rage and grief would both make sense. What comes through is **absence**: the hollow freedom of a thing no longer choosing to stay because it can no longer remember how to choose at all.

In a specific, broken sense, it resolves his Pressure. He has no affiliations left and no one to anchor. He got what he wanted, with nothing remaining inside the freedom to experience it.

## As Kukai

He drifts. There's no pursuit and no hunt. The white part is his back, wings always folded, never used; they cannot be shapeshifted away and mark every form he takes. His coherent windows are the most haunting in the story: he appears, briefly, to be the same person he always was, no regret visible, nothing wanted differently, and then the window closes and what remains drifts on.

## The Relic: Wings

When Hitori is eventually killed, the white wings solidify over decades into a Relic. Whoever carries it moves like absolute freedom: faster, lighter, unencumbered in ways unrelated to physical weight. He spent his life as an anchor, and what he left behind lets people fly.
