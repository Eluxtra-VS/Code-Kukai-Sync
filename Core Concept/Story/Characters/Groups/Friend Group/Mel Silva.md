# Mel Silva

> She makes sure everyone is taken care of, because letting herself be taken care of is the thing she never learned to allow.

## Pressure

Mel provides before being asked. She remembers what someone mentioned three weeks ago and shows up with the solution. Caring for others is the thing she's most comfortable doing; caring for herself is something she's never figured out how to justify. It presents as redirection: compliments, rest, help, all converted into another chance to do something for someone else. She isn't fishing for reassurance. She honestly doesn't believe she deserves the same quality of attention she gives away.

## Link to Ability

Her first response to danger is *"what do I have that can help?"*, and the bracelet answers. That displacement makes her Reiki. Flare runs on being seen. Pulse's desire is to close a gap by sending something of the self outward, and Mel's gap is the distance between invisible-and-useful and seen-and-worthy. The beacon condition is her whole arc in one mechanic: it only catches when someone sees *her* and she stops deflecting long enough to let it land.

## Backstory

Her father worked for an organisation connected to [[Sonya Vance]]; her mother divorced him out of fear and rebuilt with an abusive man who blamed the failing relationship on Mel: how ugly and useless she was. Her mother absorbed that view without ever examining it: snarky remarks, shifting standards, affection easiest to earn when Mel was useful. When her mother left for good, she fell into alcoholism, the suppressed resentment finding its voice through drink. In the space her absence created, Mel redirected sadness into friends, skills, and refusing to accept she was the problem. Sonya is the reason Mel ended up on the Tidal Plate. Mel doesn't know this yet.

## Design

Soft, practical clothing built for usefulness: pockets, satchel, things that fix things. The bracelet is always on her wrist in every scene; on reread it was always there. Visual language: she frames others in compositions, stands at the edge of warmth she created. Her light should read as a distress signal demanding eyes rather than hearth-glow.

# Full Detail

## Awakening

During the Yoshi incident, Mel reaches for the bracelet on her wrist, the small woven thing she made for her mother as a child, and it responds before she understands why: a small warm light, protection she did not consciously summon. Black Box Test places her Reiki.

## Anchor: The Woven Bracelet

She made it for her mother as a child: *"if I am useful enough, if I make something beautiful enough, maybe she will love me properly."* Her mother accepted it without warmth and never wore it. Mel took it back eventually, without comment. It's old, visibly handmade, frayed at the edges. It was the first thing she ever made to earn love, and it didn't work. She kept making things anyway.

Why it works as an Anchor:

- Intimate without being flashy; private history.
- Contains the origin of her displacement pattern.
- Always on her body, so it cannot be lost or left behind.
- Counter-shape to Sonya's hat: Sonya stores what she cannot bear to lose; Mel carries the first love she tried to earn and failed to receive.
- Can grow over time, threads and charms given in genuine exchange as she learns to let care land inward.
- Present in every scene; the audience may not register it until Awakening.

**Secondary:** a small satchel care-kit (bandages, thread, snacks, tools) reflecting her personality; the bracelet carries the emotional weight.

## Signature: Flare

> *A flare is still fire. It is the fire she always built for others, lit where she stands.*

**Effect:** the bracelet emits a signal of light and attention: draws eyes, marks position, guides allies through darkness or confusion, refuses to be ignored. Focused outward, it becomes a flash that disorients, blinds, cuts focus. It reveals what has been hidden, including what people try not to see.

**The Beacon (condition layer, not Veil):** when someone genuinely sees *Mel*, her rather than her usefulness, the flare intensifies into a steady beacon, impossible to redirect. Compliments she can deflect and gratitude she can redirect don't feed it, and neither does appreciation of her work. The only fuel is being looked at, and staying looked at.

This inverts Hearth. Hearth was fire built for others, sheltering everyone before sheltering herself. Flare is fire lit for herself, a signal for everyone before bearing being seen. Power growth has nothing to do with the breakthrough. The breakthrough is letting someone's seeing land.

**Why the name changed:** Hearth offered a place to rest; Flare asks to be looked at. A flare is a distress signal, so using her power is asking to be seen outright. Retired names: [[Hearth]], [[Harbor]].

## The Sonya Mirror

[[Sonya Vance]] takes advantage of giving: voluntary relinquishment is her Veil condition, and her hat stores what she cannot bear to lose. Mel is the counter-shape: voluntary acceptance, asking whether care can be received without becoming debt. Deeper down, Mel's father worked for an organisation tied to Sonya, and her mother fled that danger. **Sonya is the reason Mel is on the Tidal Plate**, and Mel does not know. Sonya believes the world takes, so she got better at taking. Mel believes others deserve care more than she does, so she got better at giving. Both were shaped by displacement, and both drew the wrong lesson in opposite directions.

## Arc Direction

- **Early:** too useful; the group relies on her because relying on her is easy. Bracelet glows on instinct; no conscious control yet. Deflects everything.
- **Middle:** Flare grows, but only as signal. Lights the way for everyone except herself. Notices the bracelet catching light when someone truly sees her. Draws no conclusions.
- **Pressure point:** someone refuses her help, or accepts it and gets hurt anyway. Care isn't control, and her mother's voice surfaces while she's still trying to be useful enough to deserve love.
- **Breakthrough:** someone (likely Kaito) sees her without her doing anything, and the flare catches for the first time. The bracelet finally receives the looking it was made for. The beacon burns steady.
- **Resolution:** she doesn't stop caring. She stops using care as an excuse to disappear. Usefulness doesn't earn love; love is something you let happen to you.

## Relationship With Kaito

Mel sees the unedited version of Kaito and does not treat it as a problem. He notices when her care leaves herself out; she sees what he tries to correct and does not flinch. The romance grows from small failures of deflection, times when the care lands anyway. The lake scene (Chapter 2) is the first time it fully lands: hands on his face, arm around his shoulders, presence in the dark, and neither of them names it. *"The moon sure does look beautiful, doesn't it"* works as a confession that never admits to being one.
