## Pressure

Daiki's Pressure is hunger shaped like certainty: the conviction that visibility is power, that attention is control, and that being truly seen is the only thing that matters. It presents as entitlement settled into bone rather than insecurity, because the Tenebri did not corrupt him into Pride, they recognised it and named it. It shows in his behaviour as management: tinted eyewear whenever multiple people are present, eyes aimed at the space beside a person's face, positioning slightly off-axis in conversations, a son observed comprehensively from a distance he never closes. Leaving the organisation removed the container. The hunger stayed.

## Link to Ability

Class follows deepest desire, and Daiki's deepest desire was never to function. It was to be seen, fully and completely, as the central character of his own story. Four lessons carved by invisibility made seeing-and-being-seen the axis of his self, so his Akarui Veil expresses exactly that: **All Seeing Eye**, the desire to look into people taken to its logical conclusion. The condition requires the target to give him their gaze, which makes every act of looking an exchange of power, and makes his marriage with Alice, who looks at him only when she chooses, the one place where the ability is welcome rather than terrifying.

## Backstory

Born noticed, then systematically made irrelevant: at ten, a teacher addressed his accountant father about "business ventures" while his double-shifting mother stood invisible beside him; at fifteen, judges walked past the working hydroelectric turbine he built alone and handed trophies to a leaking volcano team; at twenty-two, his CTO asked a senior engineer whether six months of his latency-cutting architecture seemed plausible and kept the old design on three seconds' glance; at twenty-four he waited two hours in a hospital lobby that served everyone but him, then went home, built spreadsheets, and saved his father's life himself. Each wound taught one lesson: attention is power; if you are not seen, learn to see; if the world will not see you, see it first; if it will not protect you, protect yourself. When he found people who could manipulate perception, All Seeing Eye was a correction, not a gift. His family was sent to the Tidal Plate before he left the Tenebri; leaving cost substantially, and he chose them anyway.

## Design

Design containment worn as style: tinted lenses treated as ordinary accessory, always on in groups, removed only in controlled intimacy. His body positions off-axis, standing beside conversations rather than in them, gaze landing on cheekbones and earlobes instead of eyes, the specific social texture people find unsettling without knowing why. Motifs: reflections and glass used to watch without looking; spreadsheets and plans as his form of care; distance measured precisely. Colour palette is muted professional greys with one deliberate accent, the visible reminder that he decides what gets seen. His son's mismatched right eye should echo in his design language: blue as inheritance, connection, and danger.

# Full Detail

## Overview

Daiki Amori was the former Sin of Gluttony within the Tenebri, a successor-faction member who left the organisation. This is not something people do; leaving is not clean, and the reasons for surviving it are not always comfortable ones. His family had already been sent to the Tidal Plate before he left, consequence-as-management used on members whose commitment the organisation suspects. His son was born on the Tidal Plate. Daiki left afterward, choosing family over the organisation at a point where his family was already beyond his immediate reach, at substantial cost. He is on the Tidal Plate now, has been for years, and is not particularly present in Kaito's life. The absence comes from what his ability does and the specific, exhausting architecture of living with it while trying not to hurt people.

## The World That Made Him

He was born noticed, then systematically made irrelevant. Modest family: father working two jobs, mother keeping the books, a house with a porch, a dog that greeted him every afternoon. At ten, at a parent-teacher conference, his mother (clean hands, tired eyes, polite questions about arithmetic after double shifts to afford his shirt) was passed over while the teacher turned to the father: "Tell me about your business ventures." The father, an accountant with a spreadsheet and no ventures, played along. First lesson: attention is power, and power goes where the world chooses to look. At fifteen, at the regional science fair, he presented a working hydroelectric micro-turbine built alone from scavenged parts, months measuring water flow, calibrating angles, debugging circuits, explaining Bernoulli's principle in plain words. The judges asked "Where's your partner?", invoked a team requirement that did not stop them, and awarded the trophies to two boys whose volcano leaked baking soda and vinegar across the floor. Participation ribbon. Drawer. That night he practiced holding eye contact just to memorise the feeling of attention lingering. Second lesson: if you are not seen, you learn to see. At twenty-two, after six months building network architecture cutting latency by 40%, his CTO studied the spreadsheets for three seconds, turned to the senior engineer (older, someone the world was already built to see) and asked "Does this seem plausible?" One answer, "I'm not convinced," ended it. He kept building in private on his own time, knowing they would see his mouth move but not hear the words. Third lesson: if the world will not see you, learn to see it first, reading rooms, reading unspoken currents of power. At twenty-four, when his father's heart attack put his mother sobbing on the phone, he drove to the hospital and waited two hours while nurses checked his mother's pulse and a social worker collected insurance information and left; the nurse took a message for the surgeon and nothing came back. He took his mother home, saying "We can't wait here for people who don't have time for us," then spent the night making spreadsheets, calling hospitals, checking surgeon credentials and outcomes, and executed the plan. His father survived; the surgeon called it a miracle. Fourth lesson: if the world will not protect you, protect it yourself. These four moments, ten, fifteen, twenty-two, twenty-four, made Daiki Amori. When he found the Tenebri, he did not think of perception-manipulation as a tool. He thought of it as a right. All Seeing Eye was a correction.

## All Seeing Eye

Path: Akarui Veil. Condition: the target looks directly into his eyes. Duration of the gaze determines the degree of control: a brief glance produces mild suggestion fading within minutes; sustained eye contact of several seconds produces significant control over immediate decision-making; prolonged unbroken contact produces full control for the duration. The control is not possession: the target is aware something is wrong, aware their actions are not entirely their own, and cannot override it while eye contact persists. The awareness is the horror, watching yourself do something you would not choose. Limitation: genuine eye contact required. Closed eyes, averted gaze, reflective surfaces, or appropriate eyewear all protect; the target must give him their gaze. Living with it: tinted eyewear whenever multiple people are present; no sustained eye contact even one-on-one; eyes aimed at the space beside a face; off-axis positioning. People who know him understand why; people who do not find him unsettling in a way they cannot articulate. His relationship with Alice is built around this. She knows what his eyes do and looks at him sometimes when she chooses, briefly, in controlled circumstances, in moments where the control is welcome rather than terrifying. Their relationship has its own specific architecture unlike anything else and is, in its particular way, honest.

## Pride : What It Is

Pride for Daiki is the absolute certainty that he deserves to be seen: truly, fully, completely seen, not as role or function or background setting, but as the central character in his own story. The Tenebri did not corrupt him into Pride; they recognised it and gave it a name, framework, and community. They offered the power to be seen, and he said yes. All Seeing Eye is that expressed as ability: looking at people and having them recognise you as the centre of their world. Leaving did not resolve it; it only removed the organisation containing him. He left because he outgrew their philosophy: they offered him a seat at their table and he wanted to build his own table, his own terms, his own rules. This is Pride: refusal to accept someone else's table, certainty that worth does not depend on anyone else's recognition, belief that the world should bend to accommodate his presence.

## His Absence from Kaito's Life

Present on the Plate, absent from daily existence. Partly the ability: fatherhood logistics are unsolvable when sustained eye contact with your child risks controlling them. Partly the Tenebri: he left but they did not stop existing, and visibility invites consequences. And partly, unexamined, that he does not know how to be father to a child he was not present for, raised well without him, becoming someone Daiki can observe but has no foundation with. He watches Kaito from a distance that feels safe and is, metaphorically, the All Seeing Eye turned inward on his own life: comprehensive understanding through zero contact. The irony is not lost on him. He does nothing with it. Alice knows where he is and does not discuss him with Kaito. She will when necessary. It is not yet necessary.

## What Kaito Does Not Know

Kaito knows he has a father somewhere; he does not know what his father was. He does not know that his specific sensitivity (reading rooms, feeling emotions before they are spoken, emotional states registering almost physically) is partially inherited, Daiki's Veil sensitivity running through the family, layered on his own Kyumin nature. He does not know that his mismatched eyes, the blue right eye he has spent fifteen years wanting to correct, connect to his father's ability in ways that will matter.
