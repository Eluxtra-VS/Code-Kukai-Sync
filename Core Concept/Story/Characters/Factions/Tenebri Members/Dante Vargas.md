## Pressure

Dante's anger never explodes; it refines. Years of watching people carry harm they would not acknowledge, dodge accountability they had earned, and reframe damage they caused as something that simply happened condensed his rage into certainty: calm, deliberate, and convinced of its own correctness. It shows as stillness and precision rather than heat: measured speech, exact recall of other people's harms, the composure of a man who has already finished deciding before the conversation starts. What he suppresses is the question underneath the anger, whether anyone, himself included, has the right to do what he does with it.

## Link to Ability

Class follows deepest desire, and Dante's deepest desire is accountability: a world where harm meets consequence instead of resources. Institutions failed to deliver it, so his Akarui Veil became the instrument that delivers it himself. **Judgement** perceives intentional harm committed while the harm-doer carries present guilt, then returns that harm in a worse, contextually appropriate form. His Veil condition is guilt itself: he cannot punish everyone, only the guilty who still carry the shape of what they did. The ability is the fantasy of the detective who no longer needs the court, and his arc is the question of whether seeing guilt clearly makes him fit to sentence it.

## Backstory

Dante was a detective long enough that the work shaped him: finding what people buried, connecting facts they preferred kept separate, producing evidence for outcomes institutions preferred to leave unresolved. He believed in proof and consequence. Watching people walk free because they had resources refined belief into something personal: he kept his faith in accountability and lost faith in whether institutions were capable of it. Working alongside the K.E.F. as a consultant, useful but far enough away to deny responsibility, he found documentation proving the Crown's deliberate neglect of the Tidal Plate and filed it as evidence. The K.E.F. classified it, then issued the order that broke Ku and Sora away from the organisation. Whether he understood what they would do with that evidence is the slow-burn question.

## Design

Cold precision as silhouette: tailored restraint, nothing loose or theatrical, the visual grammar of a man whose files are always complete. Detective motifs rendered sinister: notebooks of other people's worst days kept in immaculate order, evidence-tag imagery turned toward souls instead of scenes. Colour palette: institutional neutrals broken by a single verdict-red accent used sparingly. Body language is economical and level; he never raises his voice and never speeds up, and the stillness should feel more threatening than any rage would. When Judgement activates, the design tell is exposure: the target's own guilt made visible on them, reflected back worse than it went in.

# Full Detail

## Core Personality

Precise. Deliberate. Certain all the way down. Dante does not perform certainty; he has it. He spent long enough watching people carry harm they would not acknowledge, dodge accountability they had earned, and reframe damage they caused as something that simply happened. He is not angry in the explosive way Wrath often presents; he is cold, the anger refined into something more functional than rage. That is what makes him dangerous. The threat is not a person who loses control but a person who calmly believes he is the right one to determine what harm deserves.

## The Detective

He found things people buried, connected facts people preferred to keep separate, and produced evidence for outcomes institutions preferred to leave unresolved. He believed in proof and consequence. What the structure delivered over years, people walking free because they had resources, harm unanswered because it was inconvenient, refined his belief into something sharper and more personal. He kept faith in accountability and lost faith in whether institutions were capable of delivering it.

## The K.E.F.

Dante worked alongside the K.E.F., not within it, as a consultant: useful, close enough to provide value, far enough away to deny responsibility. During consultancy work he found documentation proving the Crown's deliberate neglect of the Tidal Plate and filed it as evidence. The K.E.F. classified it, then issued the order that broke Ku and Sora away from the organisation. From Dante's perspective he was doing what evidence required. Whether he understood what the K.E.F. would do with that evidence is the slow-burn question.

## Thematic Role

Dante is not Kaito's sin mirror anymore; Kaito's mirror is inverted Pride. Dante still matters to Kaito because he represents a different danger: the fantasy that if you can name harm precisely enough, you are allowed to become the consequence. For Kaito, who is learning to stop treating weakness as something that must be corrected, Dante is a warning about another false solution: turning pain into a perfect verdict.

## Signature Ability: Judgement

See [[Judgement]] for full mechanics. Dante can perceive intentional harm a person caused while carrying present guilt, then return that harm in a worse, contextually appropriate form. The condition is not "has ever hurt someone." It is intentional harm plus present guilt. This means Dante does not punish everyone; he punishes the guilty who still carry the shape of what they did. His arc is about whether seeing guilt clearly makes him fit to sentence it.
