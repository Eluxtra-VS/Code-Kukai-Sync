## Pressure

Jakar's Rei runs clean. He doesn't suppress; he processes with the efficiency of someone who has done it alone for a long time: something happens, he identifies its nature, routes it, nothing accumulates. The thing he cannot route is the mechanism itself. Every person whose projection-reaching attention lands on him is Ama's absence presented again, the image-hunger that killed her repeating forever, unchoosable and unstoppable. Its visible indicators are composure under everything and hatred worn as polish ("he hates that people love him"), plus one deviation from efficiency: the feeding schedule he maintains with a consistency he has never applied to anything else, because as long as Ori's coherent windows exist, some version of the answer stays available.

## Link to Ability

Class follows deepest desire, and Jakar's deepest desire split into two fused needs: real contact with what sits behind people's masks, and an environment where nothing can love his surface hard enough to kill again. **Faultline** is both at once: an Akarui Veil ability that grips a target's emotional pressure lines only once the target is real, their mask down, feeling something they cannot perform or manufacture. The condition is the entire mechanism. Against a composed person it finds nothing to hold. That is why he manufactures suffering: pain is the most reliable mask-remover in existence, and the realness it exposes is the only human contact he has had since Ama died. At maximum realness, with himself as direct cause, Faultline becomes Warden, the permanent bond.

## Backstory

His parents came from the Crash Region with the last evacuee wave. His father played music loud enough to cover the machinery, his mother carried the fighting forms, and both came from expressive people whose feelings moved and passed, so Jakar absorbed emotional architecture as a fact of the world before learning it was unusual. Born with a Veil sensitivity that felt other people's states as texture against his skin, he spent childhood being looked through: attention reaching toward the surface of him, never the person behind the window. Ama, the girl next door who argued with his conclusions and felt, through his sensitivity, like something with his actual shape, confronted a fixated older man publicly on Jakar's behalf. He was told afterward, at twelve, and understood that his presence was a weapon other people fired without knowing they held it. Between fourteen and seventeen he was used as a tool by people with resources, reverse-engineering precise emotional adjustment out of necessity, until he learned to tell manufactured emotion from genuine the way furniture differs from the room itself. At seventeen he stopped taking orders and started making his own decisions.

## Design

Design effort without visible strain: locs past his shoulders kept with intentionality, presence that rooms register before he speaks, clothing chosen for function rather than display because attention arrives regardless. His visual tell should be stillness. He is always the most composed person in any room, hands unhurried, eyes that catalogue rather than linger. Motifs: windows and glass (being seen through), fire producing light automatically, chains of routine (Ori's feeding schedule as ritual). When Faultline works, show the mask falling: posture breaking, performance collapsing, the moment a face becomes a person, and Jakar watching with something closer to hunger than cruelty.

# Full Detail

## Appearance

The kind of person rooms notice before he opens his mouth. Locs fall past his shoulders, kept with the intentionality of someone who understood long ago that appearance is infrastructure. Features carry the Crash Region's lineage visibly. His presence has a quality he cannot turn off: physical existence producing attention the way fire produces light, automatic, unconditional, nothing he does. He knows this with a precision past clarity into something colder: the exact effect he has entering a space, the way eyes move, the quality of the attention. It concerns the surface he stands behind and has never been about him. He doesn't dress for attention; he learned to make it functional instead.

## The Parents

His parents came from the Crash Region with the last wave of evacuees, father with the music, mother with the fighting forms, both carrying the grief of people who watched home get renamed into a warning. They settled in the northern industrial quarter of the Gear District, in rooms built for neither thing they brought. His father played music loud enough to cover the machinery, a deliberate daily choice and its own statement about what matters. They were warm in the way of a culture rooted in the knowledge that feeling things isn't weakness, expression isn't indulgence, and joy and grief and love are meant to move through the body and be visible. Too much loss kept them from being healed people, but they were expressive people, which meant their Pressure had somewhere to go. It moved. Jakar absorbed this as fact rather than lesson, and as a child he didn't know it was unusual.

## The Sensitivity

Aware of other people's emotional states before he had language for awareness. His parents' grief registered in his body as texture, a specific weight in the air felt against skin. Lower-level workers carried exhausted resentment like a temperature; teachers carried ambitions sharp, clean, or sour with suppression. He adjusted. He read rooms before entering them, learned which spaces sat heavier, positioned himself in conversations to feel the edges of what people carried beneath their words. He thought everyone did this. He learned otherwise later, at the worst possible time.

## What Attention Feels Like from the Inside

By ten the attention was constant enough to study. Through his sensitivity he could feel the specific Rei configuration of being looked at: a reaching toward the image of him rather than toward him, toward what they had decided he was before he spoke. Being looked through, a window with interest fixed on the light coming through the glass. He found it exhausting, then useful. If projection was inevitable, the rational response was managing what got projected. He didn't become a manipulator out of ambition. He concluded from evidence that unmanaged emotion aimed at him produced chaos and managed emotion produced predictability, and predictability could be survived.

## Ama

Her name was Ama. She grew up in adjacent buildings, within each other's perceptual range since before either could say what perceptual range meant. Her looks weren't what drew his specific attention. She was precise, occasionally devastating about things deserving precision and devastation, and had no particular interest in his face because she had watched him make faces at walls since before either of them understood why only he had the sensitivity. She saw straight through the surface to the actual Jakar, exhausted by what people did with his face and horrified by how rarely anyone asked what was behind it. She was the only person who argued with him as if his conclusions mattered, expected engagement with corrections instead of deflection, treated his intelligence as real and accountable. Through the sensitivity, what she felt in his company had his actual shape rather than the shape people made of him: something that knew the difference between the glass and what stood behind it. He didn't understand how much weight he had placed on that until it was gone.

## The Man

A man in the district, older, with resources sufficient to act on fixations, spent months in proximity before Jakar registered the specific quality of the attention. When he did, he recognised it instantly: projection-reaching toward something that wasn't him, emotion that doesn't see its object and doesn't care. He managed it as always, careful distance, careful deflection, skills practiced since childhood. What he failed to account for was Ama. She saw the man and saw Jakar managing alone with years-practiced efficiency, and she said something directly, publicly, with her usual precision, telling the man to leave Jakar alone. Jakar wasn't there when it happened. He was told afterward. He was twelve years old. There was no gentle version of the telling, so it wasn't gentle.

## What Twelve Felt Like

He felt the absence before processing it: a specific Rei configuration present his entire life, gone from the environment's baseline. He worked out why alone, since the only person he would have processed it with was the reason he was processing it. What he understood at twelve, with unwanted clarity, was that she died because someone looked at him the way people always had, projection-reaching, emotion blind to its object, and he was the surface onto which something powerful enough to produce violence had been projected. Ama had been the resistance. He caused nothing and chose nothing except to exist in the specific way he existed. His presence was a weapon others fired without knowing they held it, and the people it hit were the ones standing between it and him. Then came the worse part. In the raw unmanaged grief of the district afterward, his sensitivity felt what people actually were beneath every performance: grief stripped to structure, fear of the world as it was, people being themselves. He had never felt that much real contact with other people in his life. At twelve, with a child's automatic efficiency, he filed that among things that didn't yet have homes.

## The Development

Between fourteen and seventeen he was used as a tool, the clinical phrasing. People with resources and without scruples identified what proximity to him could do in difficult moments: emotional shifts in targets unaware he existed, difficult people made cooperative, resistant people made uncertain, witnesses confused about what they had seen. At fourteen he lacked the leverage to refuse. He became good at it, reverse-engineering from necessity: finding the specific Rei distortion matching each emotional configuration, applying pressure or release to specific channels with precision. He also developed comprehensive knowledge of manufactured versus genuine emotion from inside the production. Manufactured emotions feel like furniture, arranged, functional, belonging to no one. Genuine negative emotions feel like the room itself, walls and floor, what was there before anything was arranged in it. At seventeen he stopped obeying and started deciding for himself. His decisions weren't better than the orders had been. They were his.

## What Negative Emotions Actually Are

The simple reading is sadist, manipulator, power-hungry, and it misses everything. When Jakar amplifies negative emotions, pushing fear, grief, or rage until it is all a person can hold, he feels through his sensitivity the exact moment the surface drops: projection, management, the ongoing fiction of who they pretend to be, all of it falls away and leaves the thing behind the window, the person. For a man seen as a surface his whole life, with the real contact of exactly one person lost at twelve, the mask-drop is the only moment of contact he has found since. He doesn't enjoy their pain. He is ravenous for the realness underneath. Pain is the price, because suffering is the only state in which people cannot maintain projections: in real fear or grief they are only themselves, unable to love his face, stripped briefly and present. He is a sadist because suffering is the only door into real human contact he has found. That makes it unacceptable. It also makes it tragic in a way that's hard to look at directly, which is probably why he has never examined it.

## Ori

Ori was the second person he felt real contact with since Ama. Nearly a year of proximity through northern-district geography: adjacent buildings, overlapping schedules. What made Ori different wasn't warmth. Ori argued, called out manipulation on sight with clarity most people lack, told him when conclusions were wrong and expected engagement, treated his intelligence as real and accountable. Through the sensitivity, what Ori felt had the actual shape of him, knew glass from what stood behind it, found what stood behind it worth arguing with. He had learned since Ama that connection endangers the connector, and he watched Ori's Pressure building from the outside, from Ori's own life, nothing to do with him, like feeling a storm arrive on its own timeline. Then he calculated. Natural threshold: complete transformation, connection gone, no Warden link, since the link requires deliberate causation, and Ori drifting and eventually destroyed while Jakar stood bystander to losing the second person who ever saw him. Deliberate cause: same transformation, but linked to him permanently, coherent windows existing, some version of Ori still present, though not the full version. He chose cause over bystander. Three months, methodical, full awareness of endpoint and cost. He is not a man who acts without understanding. When the transformation completed he established the chain, the feeding schedule, the management system keeping Ori functional and as close to coherent as management allows.

## What He Did Not Expect

In the coherent windows, Ori does not appear to want things to have been different. This is the one thing he has not routed with clinical efficiency. The possibilities cycle in the quiet hours of maintenance. Either Ori understands him completely (logic, causality, the specific fear beneath the decision) and concludes the fear was real, the decision wrong, and the person not reducible to the decision, which would be forgiveness and the most profound thing anyone has ever offered him. Or the transformation removed the capacity for self-protective anger, and what looks like acceptance is the absence of whatever architecture would produce refusal: a Kukai briefly resembling a person rather than a person being present. He doesn't know which. He maintains the feeding schedule with consistency applied nowhere else in his life, because as long as the question stays open, some version of the answer remains available. The day the coherent windows stop entirely is the day he loses Ori for real. He keeps the schedule and declines to think about that directly.

## Who He Is Now

Excellent company: warm, observant, funny when he chooses, unhurried, interested in people in a way that feels real because it is real, in the specific way his interest is real. Humans fascinate him because their emotional architecture is legible to them and they believe completely that it isn't. Always the most composed person in any room. Not suppression; suppression creates Pressure, and his Rei runs clean. Something happens, he identifies it, routes it. Nothing accumulates. He hates that people love him: every projection-reaching look is Ama's absence presented again, the killing mechanism repeating. He cannot stop what his presence produces; he manages it rather than being managed by it. Contempt for projection-as-love, emotion that uses its object rather than seeing it, is the fuel that makes manipulation feel clean. He frames it as demonstrating efficiently what people already do to each other, and he is better at it than they are. He is honest about that too, and in his experience honesty is the rarest thing there is.

## The Sadism in Full

Fear leaves people only afraid, grief only grieving, rage only angry. Under sufficient negative pressure the social architecture falls away entirely and his sensitivity touches the actual person. He is addicted to the realness, not the suffering, and has judged the price acceptable: not virtuous, honest, which matters more to him. The Machiavellian quality has little to do with power; power is incidental. What he builds, always, is an environment he controls: no one enters without his understanding of what they are, nothing arrives unmanaged, nothing can weaponise itself against someone he might care about, though he has learned to have very few of those. He is trying to be the only variable in his environment that doesn't surprise him. He has been trying since he was twelve, when someone's unmanaged emotion killed the one person who could see through the window.

## Faultline: The Single Signature

**Faultline** is his one signature: an Akarui Veil ability manipulating a target's emotional pressure lines **once the target is real**, mask down, feeling something unperformable, unmanageable, unmanufacturable. Condition: the target's emotional mask is down. Fear stripped of management, grief of composure, rage of control. The condition is the entire mechanism (Veil): performing, managing, deflecting, and Faultline finds nothing to grip; inert against the composed. Hence manufactured suffering: pain is the most reliable mask-remover in existence. The sadism is not separate from the power. His ability requires realness, and suffering is the only door he has found into it. Once the condition holds he can adjust the target's actual interior: raise or lower emotions, amplify unresolved Pressure toward critical, destabilise Rei quality by forcing emotional states out of alignment, create openings by forcing reactions from feelings the target did not choose. The grip lasts only as long as the realness; re-composition forces re-entry. **Warden, the extreme application:** Faultline used at the moment of a Kukai's transformation, with Jakar the direct cause, target at maximum realness, masks destroyed, Pressure critical, becomes permanent: awareness, communication, and directional influence that cannot be fully refused. The will survives intact, so it is influence rather than puppetry. This is the permanent bond holding Ori: what Faultline becomes when he pushes a real moment past the threshold, not a separate ability. See [[Faultline]] and [[Warden]].

## The Tenebri Position

Valued for Warden and for the operational efficiency of producing Kukai on demand and controlling them permanently. The Tenebri don't fully understand Warden's scope, don't know how many are linked, and don't need to. He has not corrected their understanding. He operates within the Tenebri because their goals currently align with his and the Calamity's operational environment provides the density of human emotion he finds most useful. If alignment changes, he leaves. He wouldn't call it loyalty; it's an arrangement that currently works. He has mentioned Ori's origin to no one in the Tenebri. The Gluttony seat is held by his companion; the companion requires management; the management is consistent and non-negotiable. No one has asked why, which is the correct response.
