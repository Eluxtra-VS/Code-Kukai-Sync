## Pressure

Hyoga suppresses nothing so much as he is consumed by something: fascination that behaves like compulsion. His interest in Kukai is real, unperformed excitement of the kind a person has after years of study, and it sits wrong against the subject matter, warmth where there should be distance. It shows in his body as enthusiasm that cannot be contained: talking too much, offering information nobody asked for, carrying field notes and hypotheses like other men carry weapons. Where the obsession came from, someone lost or something witnessed or experienced, is undocumented even within the file of his own life. It exists fully formed.

## Link to Ability

His ability is undetermined, but the system rule that Class follows deepest desire constrains what it can be: whatever Hyoga's Class proves to be, its root desire is comprehension (observation, documentation, understanding). Harm is incidental to him; his subjects are former people and his methodology is seeking them out rather than helping them. The natural shape such a desire produces under this system is an ability built around perceiving or preserving what Kukai transformation leaves behind, which would make him the perfect instrument of his patron's empirical authority. Until canon fixes it, his power remains an open question rather than an invention.

## Backstory

Hyoga has documented every Kukai encounter he has been able to access, seeking them out directly to observe rather than fight or contain, and carries field notes and hypotheses. He considers Jakar Offei the definitive empirical authority on Kukai, since Jakar's permanent bonds are the only documented ongoing two-way relationship with Kukai consciousness; whether Jakar considers him useful or merely convenient is unstated. Following the Yoshi Kusakabe incident (a manifestation, a purification rather than elimination, an incomplete K.E.F. sweep) he arrives on the Tidal Plate, finds Kaito's group before Ku can manage what they know, and answers their questions so thoroughly that vagueness becomes more dangerous than honesty.

## Design

A clown as presentation: the makeup is always on, the performance always running, the chosen face he never explains and feels no need to. Play the unsettling register rather than the frightening one: a beat of delay before the wrongness lands, genuine delight aimed at things that should not receive it. Visual motifs: immaculate face paint over unreadable skin, small notebook and pen handled with a scientist's care, gestures of showmanship framing lecture content. Colour should be warm and inviting against cold subject matter; his excitement must read as real, because it is.

# Full Detail

## Appearance

A clown, as a presentation. The makeup is always there; the performance is always running. He has decided, at some point he does not explain and does not seem to feel the need to explain, that this is the face the world gets. Whether this is irony, philosophy, or the most sincere version of himself is not something he clarifies. He is not frightening in the way that announces itself; he is unsettling in the way that takes a moment to land: warmth in how he talks about things that should not be talked about warmly, genuine delight sitting wrong against the subject matter. He is excited about Kukai the way a person is excited about something they have spent years studying. That excitement is not performed. The performance is the face; the interest underneath is real.

## Core Personality

Fascinated and methodical, theatrical in delivery, precise in content. He does not perform cruelty and has no interest in harm as an outcome; harm is incidental to what he actually wants, which is understanding. He would describe himself as a scientist and mean it. The problem is that his subjects are former people, his methodology is seeking them out rather than helping them, and his patron is the man who manufactures them deliberately. He talks too much in the specific way of someone who has thought about their subject constantly and rarely finds anyone willing to engage at his depth. When he meets Kaito's group, people who have encountered a Kukai personally and have questions they lack language for, he is visibly pleased, answers questions, offers information, with the enthusiasm of someone who has been waiting for an audience that can follow along. This is what forces Ku's hand.

## The Obsession

His specific interest is mechanics: what happens to the architecture of a person's Pressure during transformation; what remains of the original consciousness; how the body changes to accommodate what the Rei has become; why some Kukai are more coherent than others; what the coherent windows mean for the question of personhood. He has documented every Kukai encounter accessible to him and sought them out directly to observe. He carries field notes. He has hypotheses. Whether personal history explains the obsession's origin, someone lost, something witnessed, something experienced, is undocumented; the obsession exists fully formed, and its origin is a thread the story can pull.

## Relationship with Jakar

Hyoga considers [[Jakar Offei]] the definitive authority on Kukai, empirically rather than philosophically. Jakar's permanent bonds with deliberately created Kukai make him the only documented person with an ongoing, sustained, two-way relationship with Kukai consciousness; from Hyoga's perspective he is the primary source. Whether Jakar considers Hyoga useful, interesting, or merely convenient is not stated. Jakar does not accumulate followers in a way that implies warmth: the Ku-Clowns exist because his philosophy naturally attracts a specific kind of person, not because he recruited them.

## Narrative Function: Arc 3

He arrives on the Tidal Plate following the [[Yoshi Kusakabe]] incident: Kukai manifestation, purification rather than elimination, incomplete K.E.F. sweep, exactly the kind of event he investigates. He finds Kaito's group before Ku can establish a perimeter around what they know and do not know, and begins answering thoroughly, with delight, with accurate structural information framed in his own interpretive context. He provides enough of the shape of the Tenebri, the nature of the seven sins, and the scale of what exists beyond the Tidal Plate that the group holds a partial picture before Ku can manage what that picture looks like. Ku cannot leave them with Hyoga's version uncontested. The scene where Ku explains the Tenebri properly exists because Hyoga created a situation where vagueness became more dangerous than honesty.
