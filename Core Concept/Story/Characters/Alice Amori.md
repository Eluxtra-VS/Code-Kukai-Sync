## Pressure

Alice processes internally and comes out the other side having done it, no audience required. What accumulates anyway is the weight of the untold truth. She doesn't feel guilty for lying once; she feels it because every day of waiting makes the eventual truth heavier. It behaves pragmatically, like everything else about her: noted, set aside, outrun by the amount of work in front of her. The visible tells are small and specific. Portuguese slips out when she is tired, angry, tender, or losing control. At the hardest conversations the food is present but uneaten, and her hands stay busy when the words are hardest.

## Link to Ability

Alice has no ability, no Class, and no trajectory toward one. That absence matters. The rule that Class follows deepest desire never engages for her because her Pressure does not accumulate: she has a functional, private relationship with her own emotional states, so nothing sits unprocessed long enough to rot into a threshold crossing. No Thread, no Anchor; her Kyumin sensitivity is too low to register the hidden world at all. Her role in the system is contrast rather than expression: the Code understood correctly, lived for fifteen years in front of a son who inherited the internalising without inheriting the processing capacity. That gap is most of Kaito's problem.

## Backstory

Alice raised Kaito alone in the dock district of the Tidal Plate. She knew what Daiki was before she married him, though not fully, and made the choice with partial knowledge. She never regretted the love. What she regrets is the architecture that made the marriage function at distance, and the years of explaining his absence to a child without explaining the reason. Sixteen years married to a former Tenebri member, paying attention the whole time, gave her a detailed working knowledge of the hidden world. After Kaito Awakens, she realises the silence no longer protects him. It only leaves him moving through danger without a map.

## Design

Warmth built into structure: she checks whether someone has eaten before asking what is wrong, fixes a collar while scolding, says hard things with her hands busy. Work clothes worn honest: dock-side layers, strong hands, nothing decorative. Colour leans practical blues and warm domestic spice tones, and her spaces smell of garlic, coffee, rice and beans. Visual motifs for her key scene: late night, kitchen table, food present but untouched, Portuguese arriving only as control slips. Her design tell is contrast. Total steadiness in the body, one held truth visible only in what she does not say.

# Full Detail

## Overview

Alice raised Kaito alone in the dock district of the Tidal Plate. She knew what Daiki was before she married him, not fully but enough, and made the choice with partial knowledge. She has never regretted loving him. She regrets specific things: the architecture that made the marriage function at distance instead of proximity, the years of explaining Daiki's absence to a child without explaining the reason, and the specific exhaustion of knowing something your child does not while understanding why the timing is always wrong to say it. She does not spend time on regret. She has a lot to do.

## Brazilian Texture

Alice should feel Brazilian without becoming a caricature. The texture is domestic, practical, and specific. She drops Portuguese when tired, angry, or tender, and never as decoration. Small phrases land where English feels too formal: **meu filho**, **calma**, **vem cá**, **pelo amor de Deus**, **tá bom**. She cooks like someone who learned care through portions and leftovers: rice, beans, fish, garlic, coffee too late in the day. She hums old songs while working, sometimes without noticing. Her warmth is direct. She checks if someone has eaten before asking what is wrong, fixes a collar while scolding, says hard things with her hands busy. Kaito inherits her attentiveness without her steadiness. He learned how to notice from her; he did not learn how to stop turning noticing into self-audit.

## Who She Is

She is who the dock district calls when something needs doing and doing well: reliable in the full sense of showing up, doing the thing, and requiring no management. It isn't performed; it's how she is built. Her warmth is structural. She pays attention, remembers, notices when something is wrong, says something without making it a production. The part of Kaito that is good at caring about people came from watching her do it for fifteen years without calling it anything special. She doesn't complain because suppression isn't what she does; she has a functional and deeply private relationship with her own emotional states that doesn't require an audience. Kaito inherited the internal part without the processing capacity.

## What She Knows

Alice knows about the Tenebri. She knows about Rei, Reiva, Awakening, and the K.E.F., at the level of detail you'd expect from sixteen years married to a former member while paying attention. She isn't awakened and can't use any of it; she knows it exists and what it means. She knows what Daiki's ability does. She knows why Kaito's right eye is blue. She knows enough about the Black Box facility and the Sovereign's Crown to have specific opinions, none neutral. She hasn't told Kaito. It isn't that she thinks ignorance is harmless; she knows exactly what knowledge can do to a boy like him.

## Why She Does Not Tell Kaito

The answer she'd give if cornered: she is afraid Kaito will chase his father. That also isn't the whole answer. She wanted Kaito to stay below the threshold long enough to have a normal childhood and build a life that didn't orbit Reiva, Tenebri, the K.E.F., Pressure, and the old sins of adults who should have done better. She wanted him important in an ordinary way and out of the hell hole before the hidden world noticed him. She thought if she kept the story away from him, maybe the story wouldn't claim him. She was wrong. After the Awakening she understands the silence only leaves him unmapped in danger, but telling too early could point him straight at Daiki, who is more than a missing father. He is history, ability, Tenebri, Pride, danger, and the answer to the question Kaito has been carrying in his face his entire life.

## Reveal Timing (Recommended Canon)

Alice should tell Kaito after Arc 3's Hitori loss, early in the next major arc. By then he has already Awakened, seen what hidden Pressure can do, learned enough from Ku to know her knowledge isn't superstition, and Hitori's absence makes secrecy feel newly dangerous. Past that point her silence stops reading as protection. The reveal happens because Kaito comes home exhausted, grieving, close to chasing answers from worse sources, and Alice realises someone else (Ku, a Ku-Clown, the K.E.F., or Daiki himself) will tell it without a mother's care. The scene is deep, domestic, controlled until it isn't. Late night, kitchen table, food present but uneaten, Alice speaking plainly because ornament would be disrespectful, Portuguese slipping in only when she loses control. She tells him: Daiki is alive. Daiki was Tenebri. Daiki left, and leaving did not make him safe. Kaito's right eye is connected to the bond and to Daiki's history. She kept it from him because she was afraid he would chase the man before he understood the danger. Kaito resents her for a while. The resentment is the cost of silence arriving late rather than a moral failure on his part; he feels managed and edited the way he edits himself, and that parallel should hurt. The later softening comes when he learns something proving her fear wasn't abstract: seeing what All Seeing Eye does, how close Daiki still stands to Tenebri gravity, or that Daiki has been watching from a distance in a way that is both love and control. He keeps the right to be hurt, and he no longer gets to pretend the choice was easy.

## Her Relationship with Daiki

It isn't broken. It just isn't conventional. Daiki is on the Tidal Plate and Alice knows where. They speak with the specific infrequency of two people who built a relationship around the logistics of what is possible rather than what is wanted. She looks at him briefly when she chooses to, in the controlled, consensual way they developed over years of figuring out what safe contact looks like with an ability like his. She doesn't think about what would have been different. She thinks about what is and what needs doing in it. That's pragmatism, and it runs deeper than repression: she decided the shape of her life was the shape of her life, and the question was what to build inside it.
