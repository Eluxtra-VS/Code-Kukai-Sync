# Arc Treatment — In-Depth Draft (Arcs 1–3)

This is a rough, expanded depiction sitting one level below [[Story Arcs]] — that file is the skeleton, this is the first pass of muscle on it. Purpose: give enough scene-by-scene shape to actually start writing prose/script from, without locking dialogue or exact episode counts yet. Treat everything here as a mock draft — it should be argued with, cut, and rewritten as the real thing takes shape.

Canon check: this file follows [[Canon Rules]] where it conflicts with the older [[Story Arcs]] draft. The one deliberate correction: Hitori does **not** take the Black Box Test and does not produce a "no result" — he is never tested at all, per canon. The emotional beat that used to live in "the silence after his no-result" has been moved to live in Ku's decision not to test him, and the audience noticing the omission. That's a stronger, sadder beat anyway.

---

## ARC 1 — "The Plate"

**Thesis in motion:** before anyone in the group understands what any of this costs, someone they liked disappears into it. The Code is introduced as background texture — something people say and get wrong — long before anyone knows it's load-bearing.

### Episode 1 — Establishment

Cold open on the Tidal Plate at street level: docks, fog, the specific economy of a place that was built to be a punishment and became a home instead. Kaito helping his mother with the morning catch, moving too fast, already three conversations ahead of himself. First glimpse of the heterochromia and how people's eyes catch on it half a second too long — and how Kaito clocks that they clocked it.

Community centre introduced through Yoshi: he's already there, already knows everyone's name, already pressing something into someone's hands. He gives Kaito a tool "his stepson doesn't use anymore" — said lightly, landing heavier than it should. First glimpse of the stepson in the background, physically present, emotionally elsewhere. Not cruelty. Distance.

Friend group established through small overlapping errands rather than a sit-down intro scene: Mel handing someone a bandage before they've said they're hurt; Aiden loudly recounting a fight he "definitely won"; Hitori just there, fixing something, carrying something, the group's centre of gravity without anyone naming it as such. Kaito and Mel share one beat of proximity — nothing named, both slightly too aware of the other's location in the room.

Close on Kaito lying awake that night, replaying the day's conversations, editing lines he already said. First seed of the insomnia as a visual motif — the ceiling, the rewritten dialogue playing over it like subtitles only he can hear.

**Thread planted:** the Code, spoken casually and wrong, by at least two background characters — "stay quiet about it, that's the Code" energy. No one explains it. It's just how people talk here.

### Episode 2 — Texture

Slice-of-life deepening. Yoshi's stepson storyline gets one more beat: a small rejected gesture, filmed at a distance, so the audience feels the shape of the wound before Yoshi's internal read on it is confirmed. Yoshi compensates by trying harder at the centre — more gifts, more warmth aimed outward, none of it aimed at himself.

Mel's care-kit gets its close-up: what's actually in the satchel, how she's collected it, the specific choreography of her noticing a need and moving toward it before anyone asks. One beat where someone offers *her* something — rest, a compliment, help carrying something — and she deflects it into a task for someone else without appearing to notice she's doing it.

Aiden gets a showcase brawl or sparring scene, all flash, half-formed technique. He wins on presence, not precision. Someone comments that he's better when no one's watching and he laughs it off like it's an insult.

Hitori gets the anchor motif visually seeded for the first time — maybe literally, an anchor charm, a tattoo, a piece of dock equipment he's always near. The show lets it read as pure warmth here. He is the person things orbit.

Oral folklore fragment dropped in dialogue — "listen nights," "the fog takes what you don't say," something a grandmother or shopkeeper says without elaboration. Plants the true Code sideways, the way the lore describes it being passed down.

**Ends on:** Yoshi at the centre after closing, alone, quiet in a way the audience hasn't seen from him yet.

### Episode 3 — The Incident

Yoshi's Pressure crosses the threshold. No trigger event — no fight, no rejection scene staged for drama. Just the accumulated weight of a man who's been building the case against himself for years, and the day it stops needing new evidence. The transformation itself should be slow and physically wrong rather than explosive: something in him just keeps going past the point a body should stop.

The Kukai that results is disoriented — not hunting, reaching. Drawn to Pressure and awareness the way described in canon. It gravitates toward the friend group because they're present and because Aiden, reflexively, makes himself the loudest thing in the room. His wound (needing to be seen) and the Kukai's pull toward awareness are mechanically the same shape by accident. It works. For the first time, Aiden's need for attention has a use that isn't performance.

Ku and Sora arrive already in motion — they were tracking the signature before the group knew there was one. They don't explain themselves. They drive the Kukai off (not kill it — the mercy is visible even here, unexplained). The group finds them standing in the wreckage of what used to be a community centre gathering.

Aiden and Mel awaken in the immediate chaos: Aiden's Luz flares around the shackle-instinct, while Mel reaches for her Anchor on instinct. Kaito does not awaken during the encounter; he runs with Mel and carries the unresolved pressure away from the community centre. Later, during the rooftop crisis after the K.E.F. response begins, he sees what Yoshi's need for validation became and instead of learning "don't need other people," decides — under Pressure, wrongly — that the unacceptable thing in himself has to be corrected before it becomes visible. That's the Weaver resolution, and it should read as a *cost*, not a triumph.

**Ends on:** two strangers standing in wreckage, and a group that just watched someone they liked stop being a person.

### Episode 4 — The Run

K.E.F. response incoming — Ku knows the timeline before he explains anything, and moves the group before there's time to argue. First proper look at the institution: cold, procedural, uninterested in bystanders, treating the aftermath as a containment problem rather than a tragedy. A K.E.F. unit sweeps the community centre. The group watches from a distance, hidden, and understands for the first time that whatever this world actually is, it has people whose job is to make sure no one finds out.

The group ends up bound to Ku and Sora less by choice than by physics — splitting up is more dangerous than staying together. No one asked for this. Establish Sora properly here: competent, quiet, a half-step behind Ku, doing the thing that keeps everyone moving without asking for credit.

Small human beat: someone (Mel, probably) tries to take care of Ku or Sora the way she takes care of everyone, and gets gently, firmly redirected. First hint that Sora deflects care the same way Mel does — mirrored pattern, unremarked on yet.

**Ends on:** the group somewhere safe-adjacent, exhausted, and Ku finally about to say something.

### Episode 5 — Black Box (I)

Ku gives information the way he always will: exactly what the situation requires, nothing more. He demonstrates the Black Box Test on Sora first — a construct formed in the dark, the diagnostic shape explained through action rather than lecture. He doesn't explain where the name comes from. He never will, not out loud.

Aiden is tested first among the group — and it turns out he already knew, on some level, what he'd become at the incident. Confirmed: Akarui. Ku watches his reaction more than the result.

Mel is tested next. Reiki, confirmed through the way her Rei moves toward the satchel rather than through her own body. Ku notes it without comment — the shape of someone who routes everything outward.

**Character beat:** Hitori watches both tests. He isn't offered one. No one comments on it in the moment — it just doesn't happen, the way an omission doesn't announce itself. The audience doesn't have the frame yet to know why. Ku does.

### Episode 6 — Black Box (II) / Arc Close

After the rooftop crisis, Kaito is tested. Weaver, confirmed — and Ku goes quiet on this one longer than the others did. He doesn't explain why. (He's looking at someone managing the same way he once did, and doesn't have language for that yet either.)

The test sequence ends. No one tests Hitori. Ku doesn't offer, doesn't explain, doesn't meet his eyes for a second too long. Hitori doesn't ask. This is the arc's real closing image — not a fight, not a reveal, just a silence that means something none of them can name yet.

Quiet coda: the group walking back toward whatever's left of normal. Yoshi's gifts are still in their pockets, their bags, their rooms. No one throws them away. Hitori is quiet the whole walk. No one asks why — he's always quiet, this reads as nothing new to them. It should read as something to the audience.

**Arc 1 close.** The group is intact, alive, and newly orbiting two people they didn't choose. Something has already started in Hitori that the story won't name for a long time.

## ARC 2 — "The Pursuit"

**Thesis in motion:** learning what you're standing in the middle of, while the thing that wants you quiet gets closer. This arc is about proximity — to power, to danger, to each other — without full understanding.

### Episode 7 — Settling

The group adjusts to a life that's now shaped around Ku and Sora's caution. Training starts here, but not as a curriculum — Ku teaches reactively, whatever the day's situation demands, which means the group is always slightly behind the lesson they're learning. Sora is the one who actually makes the training stick — translating Ku's minimalism into something the others can use.

Establish the new rhythm of found-family logistics: where everyone sleeps, how money and food get handled, small domestic beats that let the audience breathe after Arc 1's velocity.

### Episode 8 — Adjacent Warmth

Kaito and Mel get a real scene alone — nothing declared, everything underneath. She offers him something small (rest, food, a moment of just sitting) and he tries to redirect it the way he redirects everything about himself. It doesn't fully land, but it doesn't fully bounce off either. First crack in both their patterns, small enough that neither of them would call it anything if asked.

**Mechanic tie-in:** Kaito's left eye (Rate) gets its first real combat use here, in a low-stakes training moment — establishing the visual language of his ability before it's used in anger.

### Episode 9 — Watched / Unwatched

Aiden trains alone, thinking no one's around, and notices the shackles hold clean when he's not performing for anyone. He clocks the discrepancy and immediately reaches for any explanation that isn't the true one. Sora catches the tail end of this and doesn't say anything — but the audience sees her clock it too, the way she clocks everything.

### Episode 10 — What Sora Fixes

A mission or training exercise goes slightly wrong — nothing catastrophic, but something breaks, physically or socially. Sora is already apologising before anyone's said a word to her. Mel is the one who notices — not calling it out directly yet, just watching Sora's hands, the way she disappears into repair work the second something's imperfect.

**Mechanic tie-in:** first real demonstration of Mend — and the cost. Tremors in her hands afterward, small enough that she hides them.

### Episode 11 — The Code, Personally

Kaito's Pressure tightens. The insomnia gets worse — more revision, less sleep, a scene built almost entirely out of the gap between what he says out loud and the subtitle-thoughts underneath it. He has a real conversation with Ku — not a lesson, closer to two people circling the same wound without naming it as the same wound. Ku doesn't open up. Kaito notices that Ku doesn't open up. Neither of them says so.

The Code stops being background dialogue here and starts pressing on Kaito specifically — someone he respects says something in passing about enduring quietly, and for the first time it doesn't sound like wisdom to him. It sounds like a description of what's wrong with him.

### Episode 12 — The Clown

Toward the tail end of the arc: the group's first encounter with a Ku-Clown, almost certainly Hyoga Kira. Brief, unsettling, theatrical. He isn't hostile — he's *delighted*. He knows details about the Yoshi incident he shouldn't have access to. He's documenting, not attacking. The scene should feel like meeting someone who's already three steps ahead of you in a conversation you didn't know you were having.

He leaves without violence. The group is more unsettled by that than they would be by a fight.

### Episode 13 — Contact / Arc Close

The K.E.F. makes direct contact — not a battle, but an encounter that makes the shift explicit: this group is no longer adjacent to the problem. They are the problem now. Someone official says enough, coldly, to make clear how much bigger this is than any of them understood.

Ku is pushed toward the moment where he has to explain more — the Tenebri, his own history, what the K.E.F. actually protects — and doesn't. He almost does. He starts a sentence and doesn't finish it. Sora watches him not finish it and says nothing, because she knows exactly what's underneath the unfinished sentence.

**Arc 2 close.** The group knows they're being hunted properly now. They don't yet know by what, or entirely why. Ku's silence is no longer protective — it's becoming a liability, and everyone in the room can feel it, even without the vocabulary for it yet.

## ARC 3 — "The Tenebri"

**Thesis in motion:** names make things real. Hitori finds out that what the group just named has a face he already knows.

### Episode 14 — The Clown Returns

Hyoga Kira reappears — this time declarative rather than observational. He tells the group things Ku has been keeping vague, with the specific theatrical precision of someone who wants to be understood and enjoys being the one who gets to do the explaining. He doesn't lie. He doesn't need to. The truth is unsettling enough on its own.

This forces Ku's hand. Vagueness is no longer protecting anyone — it's just leaving the group's understanding of the world in the hands of a Ku-Clown.

### Episode 15 — The Real Version

Ku sits the group down and gives them the Tenebri properly: seven seats, seven sins, the connection to the original Seven who ended the war and later went rogue, the scale of what's actually arrayed against them. Not a lecture — Ku doing the hardest thing he does, which is talk for longer than a sentence about something that costs him.

He names members as part of explaining the structure — not performing a reveal, just being thorough because the situation now requires thoroughness. Yeshi Tanaka's name is in there, said plainly, without any awareness of what it will do to the person listening.

Hitori is quiet the way Hitori is always quiet. Afterward, alone with Ku, he asks one question — small, specific, not dramatic. Something that confirms rather than reveals — he's been building toward this since the name landed. Ku answers honestly. He doesn't soften it because he doesn't fully understand yet what it's landing on.

### Episode 16 — The Spiral, Quietly

Hitori doesn't change on the outside. He shows up. He anchors. He's present in every scene the way he's always been present. But the arithmetic underneath has shifted completely — the uncle he's been worrying about, making excuses for, reaching across the distance for, is one of seven people whose Pressure has been weaponised against the world he lives in.

The show should let this sit visually — the anchor motif returns, but framed slightly differently this time. Something in how he's shot suggests weight rather than warmth, without anything being said.

### Episode 17 — Kaito Advances

A real fight scene where Kaito's suppression costs him mechanically — the State function faltering because he can't look honestly at what needs restoring, or the Rate function overcorrecting because he's editing himself mid-combat instead of acting. Ku or Sora sees it happen and recognises the shape of it immediately, because it's their own shape too.

Mel and Kaito's relationship tightens toward the edge of being named — a moment where deflection almost fails entirely, where care nearly lands cleanly for the first time. Pull back before it's said. Not yet.

### Episode 18 — What the Others Carry

Sora's arc gets pressed: something forces the K.E.F. order history closer to the surface — maybe a K.E.F. agent references it directly, maybe Ku almost finds out the part Sora's never told him. This should hurt because Ku loves her, not because he'd reject her.

Aiden gets a paired beat: a real fight where he lets someone else take the lead and doesn't announce that he's doing it. The shackles hold. He notices. He still doesn't say anything about it out loud — but for the first time, not saying anything doesn't look like avoidance. It looks like growth with no audience, which is the whole point of his arc.

### Episode 19 — Closing In

Tenebri periphery activity (Ku-Clowns, maybe a proxy encounter with someone lower in the faction) raises the stakes directly. The group is now unambiguously inside the conflict rather than running from its edges. Hitori is present for all of it, doing what he always does — steady, anchoring — while the internal picture keeps reorganising itself with no outlet.

### Episode 20+ — The Absence

Hitori's threshold doesn't announce itself. No confrontation, no crisis beat framed as the moment. The arc should end on structure rather than spectacle: a scene the audience expects him to be in, and he isn't. The group's small, ordinary confusion — *where's Hitori* — building into the specific dread of realising this isn't him being late.

Close on what's found instead of him — or what isn't found. The wings should not be shown fully clean yet; save the coherent-window and Relic material for later material beyond this draft. This arc ends on the shape of the loss, not its full aftermath.

**Arc 3 close.** The group has a face for the enemy, a reason to fight that's no longer abstract, and a hole where Hitori used to stand. Kaito and Mel are close enough to the truth of what they are to each other that the next arc will have to deal with it directly. Ku has said more than he's said in years and it still wasn't enough, in time, for the person who needed it soonest.

## Open Threads Carried Past Arc 3

- **Kaito / Mel:** unnamed but no longer deniable by either of them.
- **Ku:** has now broken his own silence once, under duress. Whether he does it again voluntarily is the next real test of his arc.
- **Sora:** the K.E.F. order truth is still not fully surfaced to Ku. It's closer to the surface than it's ever been.
- **Aiden:** has proof, now, that his power works without an audience. Hasn't metabolised what that means about the audience he's been performing for his whole life.
- **The Ku-Clowns / Hyoga:** now a known, ongoing threat rather than a one-off unsettling encounter.
- **Hitori as Kukai:** entirely unaddressed past this draft — the coherent windows, the wings, what the group does when they find out what happened to him. That's the next document.
