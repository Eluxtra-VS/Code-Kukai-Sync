# Comic / Manga Integration Plan

This file translates Code: Kukai from lore and character canon into a readable comic/manga production plan. Story canon still lives in the character, power-system, and arc files; this file explains how to stage that canon visually.

---

## Core Adaptation Rule

The metaphor is the mechanic, so the comic should never treat powers as separate from emotion.

Every major ability scene should answer three questions on the page:

1. **What is the character feeling?**
2. **How are they misreading or processing that feeling?**
3. **How does the power show the difference?**

If a fight does not reveal character, shorten it. If a quiet scene changes how a power works later, protect it.

---

## Format Target

Working format: serialized manga/comic chapters grouped into arcs.

- **Chapter length:** flexible, but each chapter should have one emotional turn.
- **Arc length:** preserve the current episode structure as chapter clusters rather than rigid television episodes.
- **Ending rhythm:** each chapter should end on either a question, an image, or a pressure shift — not always a cliffhanger.
- **Support resource note:** if published as episodes/chapters, keep the project rule that every episode ends with a crisis support resource.

## Visual Language

### The Code

The misread Code should appear in cramped compositions: characters framed by doorways, nets, dock beams, crowded panels, speech bubbles that leave little room to breathe.

The true Code should appear as space: wider gutters, quieter panels, open backgrounds, breath visible in fog, characters finally occupying the panel instead of being boxed by it.

### Pressure

Pressure is not a number. It should show through:

- Repeated panel shapes.
- Dialogue fragments returning as background text.
- Tight close-ups on hands, eyes, shoulders, jaw tension.
- Fog or shadow becoming denser around the character.
- Panel borders subtly warping near critical states.

### Luz and Kuro

- **Luz:** clean shapes when emotion is processed; flickering or wasteful shapes when emotion is mislabelled.
- **Kuro:** sharper, heavier, more invasive shapes. It should feel efficient in the short term and costly in the body.

## Kaito Page Rules

Kaito's visual motif is revision.

Use overwritten dialogue, repeated micro-panels, and alternate versions of the same speech bubble to show him editing himself before he speaks. The reader should understand that the spoken Kaito is often the second or third draft.

His insomnia should appear frequently but not every night. It worsens after emotionally intense days and after days where he speaks to many people. The visual grammar can be:

- Ceiling panel.
- Fragments of the day's conversations around him.
- His heterochromia rendered as two separate focal points.
- Dawn arriving without emotional resolution.

His core word is **Inadequacy**. The story should not ask the reader to think “inverted Pride” first. They should feel that Kaito is terrified of being seen incomplete.

### Divergence on the Page

- **Left eye / Rate:** motion lines desynchronise. A limb arrives late or too early. The target looks out of sync with intention.
- **Right eye / State:** panels echo previous states. A wound overlays with an intact version. Broken structure ghosts backward toward wholeness.

The right eye requires conscious focus. Show failure by having Kaito physically look but visually fail to center the thing in the panel. The wound or object is present, but the composition avoids it until he truly focuses.

## Ku Page Rules

Ku's visual motif is rhythm under containment.

He should often appear still while the page around him implies held motion: sound-wave paneling, heartbeat spacing, music-player imagery, repeated beat gutters.

Ku is a Weaver because his Pressure goes inward. Show this by keeping his emotional panels internal: chest, pulse, breath, silence. He does not externalise. He contains.

Beat-Break should read clearly:

1. First strike: ordinary impact.
2. Second strike: heartbeat panel appears between beats.
3. Third and onward: rhythm becomes layout.
4. Suppression breaks the rhythm: panel spacing stutters.
5. Honest emotion restores it: layout snaps clean.

---

## Aiden Page Rules

Aiden's visual motif is performance.

Early Aiden should dominate panels loudly but inefficiently. His constructs are large, decorative, and break with visual embarrassment. When he is not showing off, the page should become cleaner: fewer effects, better geometry, more useful negative space.

Core word: **Vainglory**.

He does not have real Pride at the start. He performs pride because he lacks self-belief. His Forge ability should show this difference:

- Performing: constructs look impressive, fail structurally.
- Sincere: constructs look simpler, coordinate better, hold.
- Mature: he builds the stage for others to succeed, not for himself to be admired.

If **Stagecraft** replaces or evolves **Shackles**, introduce it as a visual broadening, not a sudden new power.

---

## Mel Page Rules

Mel's visual motif is care redirected outward.

Her satchel/care-kit should be drawn consistently and lovingly. It is not just equipment; it is a portable history of attention.

Flare should feel like consent becoming visibility. When someone accepts care from Mel, a visual channel opens. When Mel refuses to include herself, the protective composition should literally exclude her from the safe shape.

Romance with Kaito should grow through failed deflections. Small panels. Hands almost touching. Food passed. Bandages offered. Rest suggested. Care landing halfway.

---

## Hitori Page Rules

Hitori's visual motif is the anchor.

At first, frame the anchor as warmth and stability. Later, repeat the same imagery but reveal weight. Same symbol, new meaning.

His transformation should not be spectacle-first. The horror is absence. The strongest chapter ending is not him screaming. It is the group expecting him in a panel and finding the panel composition wrong because he is not there.

Wings should be saved. Do not overexplain them early.

---

## Alice Reveal Chapter

Recommended placement: early Arc 4, after Hitori's loss.

The chapter should be quiet and domestic. Kitchen table. Food present but uneaten. Alice's hands busy until they cannot be. Portuguese appears when control thins, not as decoration.

Chapter turn:

1. Kaito comes home exhausted and close to chasing answers.
2. Alice realises silence is no longer protection.
3. She tells him Daiki is alive and was Tenebri.
4. Kaito feels managed and betrayed.
5. The chapter ends not with forgiveness, but with the table between them feeling larger than the room.

Later payoff: Kaito learns enough about Daiki's danger to understand Alice's fear. This should complicate the resentment, not erase it.

## Arc Completion Checklist

For each arc document, make sure every chapter has:

- Emotional thesis.
- Character whose Pressure changes.
- Power-system consequence, if any.
- Visual motif used or developed.
- Setup paid off or new setup planted.
- Final image.

Before scripting pages, create a chapter card with:

```text
Chapter title:
Arc:
POV/emotional center:
Pressure movement:
Power-system beat:
Visual motif:
Start image:
End image:
Must-include dialogue/information:
What changes by the end:
```
