# Kashi Kizu

## Basic Information

- **Full name:** Kashi Kizu - meaning, approximately, "defect" and "wound/scar." Together: the scarred flaw.
- **Path:** Akarui
- **Class:** Veil
- **Signature:** Fracture
- **Status:** Antagonist - independent
- **Connection:** Black Box facility survivor; former companion of Ku Mochizuki
- **Real Representative:** [[Vlad]]

---

## Overview

Kashi Kizu was one of three children in the Black Box facility who could not Awaken. He was there when Ku was there. He was present for the corridor event and walked out with the opposite conclusion.

He is not aligned with the Tenebri. He is not aligned with anyone. His ideology is his own, and he pursues it with the patience of someone who has never needed external validation for his conclusions.

---

## The Multiple Personalities

Kashi presents as one coherent person in most social contexts: articulate, precise, unnervingly calm. This is the dominant personality.

The others surface under specific conditions: high-stress combat, prolonged isolation, or moments where his framework is challenged by something it cannot process.

**The child from the facility** - frightened, observant, risk-avoidant. When dominant, Kashi does not fight unless forced. He watches and finds safe paths.

**The one that sounds like the girl** - brief, unsettling, and difficult to categorise. It knows things about the unnamed girl that Kashi should not know with certainty.

**The believer** - ecstatic about suppression and transformation. It is the most dangerous combat personality because it is not trying to win; it is trying to see someone become what it believes they should become.

The dominant personality knows about the others and manages them clinically. He does not experience them as a problem to solve. He experiences them as additional perspective.

---

## The Belief

Kashi watched the girl transform into something that destroyed the person who had done everything to her.

His conclusion: suppression is not a failure state. It is a method. Pressure held past every threshold builds into something resolution could never produce.

He extends this to Kukai. They are not failed humans to him. They are complete expressions of what humans become when they stop being limited by emotional resolution.

This logic is not entirely wrong on mechanical facts. Extreme unresolved Pressure does produce extreme transformation. Kashi removes the cost from the equation: the person, the loss, the grief, the absence of the self that existed before.

---

## Ability - Fracture

**Condition (Akarui Veil):** The target is actively suppressing an emotion in that moment.

The compression event must be present in their Rei architecture. Kashi cannot use Fracture on a target simply because they have a painful past. They have to be holding something down now.

**What Fracture does:**

Every act of suppression creates structural weakness in the target's Rei architecture: a point where flow is compressed, irregular, and inefficient. Kashi perceives these weaknesses as fractures.

He applies pressure at the exact flaw.

In combat, the immediate effect is cascading weakening:

- Ability output drops.
- Physical performance degrades.
- Rei costs increase.
- Defensive structure becomes less coherent.
- Continued suppression creates more fracture points.

Kashi can push a near-critical target closer to transformation, but this requires time, sustained proximity, and the believer personality is most likely to pursue that outcome.

**The horror:** The misread Code makes targets more vulnerable. The more faithfully someone suppresses, the more Fracture can find.

---

## Personality Shifts in Combat

The dominant personality uses Fracture analytically: find the highest-yield weakness and compromise the target efficiently.

The child personality uses Fracture defensively: target the fracture that creates the safest escape or most predictable response.

The believer uses Fracture toward transformation: target the configuration most likely to push Pressure past the threshold.

When Kashi shifts mid-combat, the same ability suddenly has a different intent.

---

## Relationship to Ku

Kashi does not hate Ku. In his framework, he almost respects him.

What he cannot process is that Ku saw the same evidence and arrived at the opposite conclusion. The girl's transformation made Ku commit to emotional resolution. It made Kashi believe transformation was the point.

They have the same data. They have different interpretations. Only one of them can be correct.

Whether one of Kashi's personalities carries something other than clinical assessment regarding Ku is an open story question. The one that sounds like the girl is the most likely candidate.
