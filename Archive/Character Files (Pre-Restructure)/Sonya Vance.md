# Sonya Vance

## Basic Information

- **Name:** Sonya Vance
- **Organisation:** Tenebri
- **Sin:** Greed
- **Origin:** Slums of East Savor Central
- **Path:** Reiki
- **Class:** Veil
- **Anchor:** Hat

---

## Origin

East Savor Central's slums sit next to the plate's most productive agricultural land. The people who live there grow what they cannot afford to eat, harvest what they cannot keep, and maintain the infrastructure of abundance for a plate whose wealth concentrates at its centre and western districts. The eastern slums are the hidden cost of Savor Central's identity as the plate that feeds the world — visible only to the people who live there and the people who come to take from them.

Sonya grew up in those slums. Not in isolation — in a specific community with specific people, the texture of a place that is poor in material terms and not poor in the ways that actually matter for a childhood. She knew her neighbours. She knew their children. She knew the specific rhythms of the eastern district — which days the trade caravans came, which inspectors could be reasoned with, which ones could not, what you did when the latter showed up.

She had family. She had enough.

---

## The Taking

The slums of East Savor Central are periodically cleared. Not destroyed — cleared. The official language is "agricultural expansion" or "resource optimisation" or "infrastructure development," and the result is the same each time: people are told they have a certain number of days, and then the days pass, and then whatever stood there is gone.

It happened once when Sonya was young. Her family relocated to another part of the eastern district. This is normal. This is how it works. They rebuilt.

It happened again when she was older.

Her family relocated further east. East is the direction you go when you have fewer options. The community she had grown up in scattered. Some of them found each other in the new location. Most did not.

It happened a third time.

The third time, her family was gone. Not all at once — through the accumulation of what repeated displacement does to people. Her mother, then her father, then the last of the people she would call family in any meaningful sense. Not through violence specifically. Through the specific attrition of people who are perpetually moved before they can build the things that keep people alive in the long run.

Sonya was sixteen and understood, at sixteen, that everything she built was temporary by structural design. Not by accident. By decision. By the specific calculation of people who had decided that the land in East Savor Central was worth more than the people on it and would continue to be worth more indefinitely.

## The Hat

She had found it before the third displacement — in a market stall, unremarkable, worn by someone who had not understood what it was. She paid a small amount for it and put it on and felt, for the first time she could identify, that something was responding to her. The first Anchor. She did not know this term. She knew that the hat felt right in a way she could not explain and kept.

The pocket dimension came first as a survival technique. She put things inside the hat and they stayed there — safe from the next displacement, protected from the next taking. The hat became the only permanent home she had. Not metaphorically. Actually. The things she needed were inside it regardless of where she physically was. She could not be fully displaced because she carried everything with her.

She taught herself magic next. Not as performance initially — as cover. If she appeared to be a travelling entertainer, the hat was a prop and no one asked why she kept it so close. The performance grew. She was talented. People paid to watch her, which meant she could eat and move without being dependent on the plate's infrastructure, which meant she was harder to displace than someone with a fixed address.

The magic became real performance because she was good at it and because it was hers — the one thing she had built that no one had taken yet.

---

## The Accumulation

Sonya's relationship with taking developed gradually and with a logic that is not difficult to follow.

Everything she had built had been taken. Not once — repeatedly, systematically, by people who experienced no consequence for taking it. The lesson she absorbed, because it was the accurate lesson from the evidence available to her, was that ownership is a fiction maintained by force and the people with enough force can revise it whenever they choose.

She revised it in the other direction.

The organisation she built — the network that operates beneath the Tenebri's visibility threshold, primarily through the Gear District — is an acquisition infrastructure. Things move through it. People's possessions, their assets, the products of their work, information they thought was private. Sonya does not experience this as theft in the moral sense. She experiences it as participation in the system that always existed — the system where things belong to whoever is willing and able to take them. She became willing. She became able.

What she did not expect: the accumulation does not produce the feeling she was pursuing. She thought, at some point in the past, that enough would feel like enough. That the hat full of things that cannot be taken would eventually constitute security.

The hat is full. She is not secure. The next taking is always possible. The only answer to that is more.

This is the sin of Greed understood correctly — not a love of having, but a terror of losing that can never be satisfied by accumulation because the terror is not actually about the things.

## The Veil Condition

**The condition is someone knowingly giving something up — the moment of voluntary relinquishment, freely made.**

When someone willingly parts with something of genuine value — not surrendering under duress, but choosing to give — the Rei in that moment carries a specific configuration: openness, trust, the specific lightness of someone who has decided that what they are parting with is less important than what the giving serves.

Sonya's Veil finds that configuration and extracts through it. Not what they gave. Something of equivalent value that they had not intended to give — a technique's mechanic, a piece of knowledge, a capability. The extraction is proportional to the sincerity of what they were giving.

The counter is to give nothing freely. Which is exactly what someone in her position — someone who has lost everything enough times to stop trusting the gesture — would assume everyone eventually learns.

The horror: the ability requires genuine generosity to fire. It cannot operate on people who have already closed down. It operates on exactly the kind of person she can no longer be.

## Mel Silva — The Connection

Mel's father worked for Sonya's organisation. The specifics of his entry are consistent with how most people enter it — desperation, a promise that sounded like a solution, insufficient information about what the solution would require.

People do not leave Sonya's organisation voluntarily. Mel's father attempted to. The attempt resulted in his daughter being sent to the Tidal Plate — not as punishment, but as collateral management. A person with a child in a distant location is a person with a reason to keep their mouth shut.

Sonya is not unaware of what this means for Mel. She has not thought about it significantly. She operates at scale and the specific consequences to individuals at the edges of that scale are not details she tracks.

This is not cruelty. It is the specific moral compression that comes from having decided that the system always takes and the only question is who is doing the taking. Mel's father tried to leave. Mel's displacement was the consequence. The system worked as intended. Sonya was the system.
