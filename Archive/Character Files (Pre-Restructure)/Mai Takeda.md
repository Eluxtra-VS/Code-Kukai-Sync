# Mai Takeda

## Basic Information

- **Name:** Mai Takeda
- **Organisation:** Tenebri
- **Sin:** Pride
- **Origin:** Japanese and Korean heritage
- **Path:** Akarui
- **Class:** Forge
- **Ability:** Monarch
- **Relation:** Older sibling to Aiden Takeda
- **Real Representative:** [[Bella]]

---

## Overview

Mai Takeda is the person Aiden was raised alongside — the older sibling whose competence, presence, and relationship with recognition became the template for what Aiden spent his adolescence trying to reproduce. The standard held up to Aiden was always adjacent to Mai's shape: this is what it looks like when the ability is handled correctly, when power and confidence arrive together without apology.

What Aiden absorbed was the surface. He absorbed the way recognition found Mai rather than being chased by her, the way rooms oriented toward her, the way her competence was visible without requiring announcement. He has been trying to reproduce this his entire life without understanding that what he is imitating was already the endpoint of a corruption, and that Mai's ease with recognition is not the same as contentment — it is the certainty that has replaced the need for contentment entirely.

She has a God complex in the precise sense. Not arrogance that defends against insecurity. Arrogance that has consumed the insecurity entirely and left behind a settled, structural belief that she is above the category of people who need to compete. She does not measure herself against others. She does not need to.

## Monarch — The Ability

Mai creates and controls mechanical ants — Forge constructs made of dense, solidified Rei with an internal structure fine enough to appear mechanical. They are produced in large numbers and operate autonomously under her general command.

**What they are:** Akarui Forge at high mastery produces complex, self-modifying constructs that operate independently. The ants are the extreme expression of this — constructs small enough to be placed invisibly on any surface, numerous enough to cover a significant area simultaneously, and internally sophisticated enough to interface with the Rei medium in whatever they contact.

**What they do:** When the ants make contact with a person — on skin, on clothing, embedded in the environment they are standing in — they interface with the Rei flowing through that person's body. The interference is not crude disruption. It is adjustment. The ants carry Rei in specific emotional configurations, and proximity to that Rei, sustained through the ants' presence, produces corresponding emotional states in the target.

This is Forge being used as the delivery mechanism for Rei adjustment. The constructs themselves are not Veil — they are solid, real, can be destroyed. But what they deliver, once in contact, operates through the Rei medium.

At low saturation: mild emotional adjustment. Confidence becomes slightly diminished. Calm becomes accessible where anxiety was building. The changes are subtle enough that targets often do not notice.

At medium saturation: significant emotional reshaping. The target's Rei quality degrades as their emotional architecture is rewritten by the ants' sustained presence. Positive emotions become harder to access. The specific emotional state Mai finds most useful — a specific, structured awe that presents as the appropriateness of deference — becomes dominant.

At high saturation — full coverage: The target bows. Not because they are forced. Because the Rei running through them has been adjusted to produce the genuine feeling that this is right. They know they are bowing. They cannot access the emotional architecture that would prevent it. They are doing it themselves. This is the part Mai finds interesting.

**The constructs:** The ants are not invisible — they are visible as small, dark mechanical objects with a faint metallic sheen. Destroying them individually is possible. Destroying them comprehensively in the numbers Mai produces at full deployment is the tactical problem. A hundred can be swept aside. Several thousand, seeded into a room over the preceding hour, are a different question.

## The God Complex

Mai does not perform superiority. She operates from it as a baseline assumption, the way a person operates from the assumption that the ground is solid — without checking, without thinking about it, because it has never been otherwise.

She is not theatrical about this. Theatrical requires an audience whose judgment matters. She is simply doing what she does, at the level she does it, with the precision that comes from having decided that almost no situation requires her genuine attention. Most things can be handled by a version of herself that is present without being invested.

When something does require her genuine attention — rare, but possible — the difference is dramatic. She becomes fully present in a way that most people around her have never seen before. This is more unsettling than the baseline, not less.

## The Aiden Mirror

Mai's relationship with recognition is the endpoint of the road Aiden is walking.

She does not pursue recognition. She does not need to. Recognition arrives because the things she builds are genuinely extraordinary, and she knows they are extraordinary without requiring anyone to confirm it. This is what Aiden has been trying to reproduce: the ease of someone whose relationship with their own capability does not depend on external confirmation.

What he has not understood: Mai arrived here through the complete calcification of the Pride she started with. She does not need recognition because she no longer has the capacity to find it meaningful. She is not free of the desire — she is past the desire, and past it in a direction that took everything worth having with it.

When Aiden eventually understands what he is looking at — his older sibling, the template he has spent years imitating, in the context of who she actually is and what she actually does — the arc's question becomes whether he can look at the endpoint of his own desire and choose to stop.
