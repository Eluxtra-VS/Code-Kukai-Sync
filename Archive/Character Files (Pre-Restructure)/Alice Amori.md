# Alice Amori

## Basic Information

- **Name:** Alice Amori
- **Location:** Tidal Plate — dock district
- **Occupation:** Dock worker, fishing trade
- **Family:** Daiki Amori (husband, estranged), Kaito Amori (son)
- **Status:** Kyumin — low sensitivity, no Awakening trajectory
- **Background:** Brazilian-Tidal Plate household; Portuguese survives in her speech, food, habits, and private tenderness more than in public identity

---

## Overview

Alice Amori raised Kaito alone in the dock district of the Tidal Plate. This is the summary of her life in practical terms. The full version is more complicated.

She knew what Daiki was before she married him — not fully, not everything, but enough. She made the choice with partial knowledge and has never regretted loving him in the simple way that would make the story easier. She regrets specific things: the architecture that made the marriage function at distance rather than proximity, the years of explaining Daiki's absence to a child without explaining the reason, and the specific exhaustion of knowing something your child does not while understanding why the timing is always wrong to say it.

She does not spend time on regret. She has a lot to do.

---

## Brazilian Texture

Alice should feel Brazilian without becoming a caricature. The texture is domestic, practical, and specific.

She drops Portuguese when tired, angry, or tender — not constantly, not as decoration. Small phrases land where English feels too formal: **meu filho**, **calma**, **vem cá**, **pelo amor de Deus**, **tá bom**. She cooks like someone who learned care through portions and leftovers. Rice, beans, fish, garlic, coffee too late in the day. She hums old songs while working, sometimes without noticing.

Her warmth is not soft-focus. It is direct. She checks if someone has eaten before asking what is wrong. She fixes a collar while scolding. She says hard things with her hands busy.

Kaito inherits her attentiveness, but not her steadiness. He learned how to notice from her. He did not learn how to stop turning noticing into self-audit.

---

## Who She Is

Alice is the person the dock district calls when something needs doing and doing well. Not because she is the strongest or fastest or most experienced — because she is reliable in the specific, comprehensive sense of someone who shows up, does the thing, and does not require management. She has been this way her entire life. It is not performed. It is simply how she is built.

She is not warm in the soft sense. She is warm in the structural sense — she pays attention to people, remembers things, notices when something is wrong, and says something about it without turning it into a production. She is the person Kaito learned his genuine attentiveness from. The part of Kaito that is actually good at caring about people — beneath the audit, beneath the performance — came from watching her do it for fifteen years without calling it anything special.

She does not complain. Not because she suppresses — because she has a functional and deeply private relationship with her own emotional states that does not require an audience. The Code's misreading did not produce her silence. She is simply a person who processes things internally and emerges having done it. Kaito inherited the internal part without inheriting the processing capacity, which is most of his problem.

---

## What She Knows

Alice knows about the Tenebri. She knows about Rei, Reiva, Awakening, and the K.E.F., at a level of detail that someone who has been married to a former Tenebri member for sixteen years and paid attention would have. She is not awakened. She cannot use any of it. She knows it exists and what it means.

She knows what Daiki's ability does. She knows why Kaito's right eye is blue. She knows enough about the Black Box facility and the Sovereign's Crown to have specific opinions, none of them neutral.

She has not told Kaito.

Not because she thinks ignorance is harmless. Because she knows exactly what knowledge can do to a boy like him.

---

## Why She Does Not Tell Kaito

Alice is afraid Kaito will chase his father.

That is the cleanest answer and the one she would give if cornered. It is also not the whole answer.

She wanted Kaito to stay below the threshold. Not forever in ignorance, but long enough to have a normal childhood, long enough to build a life that did not orbit Reiva, Tenebri, K.E.F., Pressure, and the old sins of adults who should have done better. She wanted him to become important in an ordinary way: skilled, stable, educated enough or connected enough to leave the Tidal Plate. To get out of the hell hole before the hidden world noticed him.

She thought if she kept the story away from him, maybe the story would not claim him.

She was wrong.

After Kaito Awakens, Alice realises the silence no longer protects him. It only leaves him moving through danger without a map. But telling him everything too early could still point him directly toward Daiki, and Daiki is not only a missing father. Daiki is history, ability, Tenebri, Pride, danger, and the answer to the question Kaito has been carrying in his face his entire life.

Alice's guilt is not that she lied once. It is that every day she waited made the eventual truth heavier.

---

## Reveal Timing — Recommended Canon

Alice should tell Kaito after Arc 3's Hitori loss, early in the next major arc.

That timing works because Kaito has already seen what hidden Pressure can do. He has already Awakened. He has already learned enough from Ku to know Alice's knowledge is not random superstition. And Hitori's absence makes secrecy feel newly dangerous. If Alice waits past that point, her silence stops reading as protection and starts reading as authorial convenience.

The reveal should not happen as punishment. It should happen because Kaito comes home exhausted, grieving, and closer than ever to chasing answers from worse sources. Alice realises that if she does not tell him now, someone else will — Ku, a Ku-Clown, the K.E.F., or Daiki himself — and they will not tell it with a mother's care.

The scene should be deep, domestic, and controlled until it is not. Late night. Kitchen table. Food present but uneaten. Alice speaking plainly because ornament would be disrespectful. Portuguese slipping in only when she is losing control.

She tells him:

- Daiki is alive.
- Daiki was Tenebri.
- Daiki left, but leaving did not make him safe.
- Kaito's right eye is connected to the bond and to Daiki's history.
- She kept it from him because she was afraid he would chase the man before he understood the danger.

Kaito resents her for a while. He should. The resentment is not a moral failure; it is the cost of the silence arriving late. He feels managed, edited, protected in the same way he edits himself — and that parallel should hurt.

The later softening should come when Kaito learns something about Daiki that proves Alice's fear was not abstract. Either he sees what All Seeing Eye does, learns how close Daiki still is to Tenebri gravity, or realises that Daiki has been watching from a distance in a way that is both love and control. Kaito then understands that Alice was not hiding a simple father from him. She was trying to delay a dangerous inheritance.

He still has the right to be hurt. But he no longer gets to pretend the choice was easy.

---

## Her Relationship with Daiki

It is not broken. It is simply not conventional.

Daiki is on the Tidal Plate. Alice knows where. They speak with the specific infrequency of two people who have built a relationship around the logistics of what is possible rather than what is wanted. She looks at him briefly when she chooses to — in the controlled, consensual way they developed over years of figuring out what safe contact looks like with an ability like his.

She does not think about what would have been different. She thinks about what is and what needs doing in it. This is not repression. It is the specific pragmatism of someone who decided, at a certain point, that the shape of her life was the shape of her life and the question was what to build inside it.
