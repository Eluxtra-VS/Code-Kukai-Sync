# Hitori Tanaka

## Basic Information

- **Full name:** Hitori Tanaka
- **Location:** Tidal Plate
- **Path:** Kyumin — does not Awaken; becomes Kukai
- **Relic:** Wings
- **Family:** Yeshi Tanaka (uncle; Tenebri, Sin of Sloth)
- **Real Representative:** [[Lucas]]

---

## Core Personality

Steady. Present. Dependable.

Hitori is the person the group leans on without thinking about it. He shows up. He stays. When things fall apart he is already moving toward whoever needs steadying. This is not performance — he genuinely cares for the people around him.

It is also the thing that is quietly consuming him.

---

## The Uncle

Hitori knows his uncle is struggling. He does not know why in full — not at the start of the story.

What he knows: Yeshi Tanaka has been different since his wife died. The Gear District wore him down for years before that; the death was the thing that finished whatever was left. Hitori has watched his uncle go quieter, more still, less interested in anything around him. He checks in when he can. Yeshi answers. He does not say much, but he answers.

What Hitori does not know at story start: Yeshi is Tenebri.

---

## The Spiral

When Ku and Sora join the group, they bring context that the friend group did not have before — specifically, knowledge of the Tenebri: who they are, what they represent, what they have done, how they are structured. The seven sins. The members. The faction's history.

Hitori hears this and begins connecting pieces he was not looking for.

His uncle's withdrawal. The timing. The things Yeshi said and didn't say about where he went, who he saw, what kept him occupied. Hitori does not announce what he's thinking. He is not the kind of person to announce things. He just starts thinking differently — assembling a picture from fragments he already had, against a frame he was just handed.

He confirms it. He is not wrong.

What follows is not a dramatic confrontation or a crisis event. It is a quiet reframing of everything Hitori thought he understood about his family, the Tidal Plate, and the weight he has been carrying. The uncle he has been worrying about — the man he has been trying to reach across the distance for years — is one of the people responsible for the shape of the world the group is now navigating.

This is not the thing that breaks him. Hitori has been accumulating weight for a long time. This is the weight that arrives when there is no more room for it.

He becomes Kukai not long after.

---

## Pressure Profile

**Type: Suppressed freedom**

Hitori wants to be free. Not dramatically — free in the simplest possible sense. No obligations, no one depending on him, just himself, somewhere quiet, not needed by anyone.

He has wanted this for as long as he can remember. He has never once moved toward it.

Instead he stays. Every time the group needs an anchor he is there. He does not resent the people he loves. If he resented them it would be easier to leave. He loves them more than he wants what he wants, and he has never figured out what to do with that except keep going.

---

## The Anchor

Hitori is represented visually by an anchor throughout the story. It appears in his imagery, in how others describe him, in the weight he carries in every scene.

The story lets this sit for a long time as something stable and positive.

The moment before the threshold — in the final period before his Pressure passes the point of no return — the image pulls back. The anchor is still there. But so is Hitori, behind it, dragging it. He was never holding the group in place. He was dragging himself forward by the weight of everyone he refused to leave behind. He knew this. He never stopped.

---

## Why He Does Not Awaken

Hitori's Reiva has been watching him for years. It is waiting for the moment he resolves his Pressure — the moment he makes a choice about what he wants rather than what he stays for.

That moment does not come. Not because he is incapable of it. Because every time it approaches, he looks at the people around him and stays for them instead.

His Reiva does not abandon him. It watches the Pressure climb past the point where Awakening becomes possible and into the territory that only produces one kind of outcome. The Reiva cannot prevent it. It can only be present for it.

---

## The Transformation

Hitori does not transform in a dramatic moment. There is no explosion, no crisis, no catastrophic break. It is the quietest transformation in the story.

He simply reaches a point where the Pressure has no more room. Where the weight he has been carrying — all the freedom he chose not to take, all the wants he suppressed for the people he loved — has accumulated past any threshold he can survive as the person who made those choices.

What comes through is not rage. Not grief. It is absence — the specific, hollow freedom of a thing that is no longer choosing to stay because it can no longer remember how to choose at all.

The terrible thing the story does not let the audience look away from: in a specific, broken sense, it resolves his Pressure. He is not affiliated anymore. There is no group to anchor. He is not obligated to anyone. He got what he wanted. The story does not frame this as a victory. What he became has no capacity to want anything. The freedom is real. There is no one left inside it to experience it.

---

## As Kukai

Hitori's Kukai form is notable for the absence of urgency that characterises most Kukai. He does not pursue aggressively. He does not hunt in the way Kukai driven by rage or grief hunt.

He drifts.

The white part is his back — the wings that were always there, always folded, always the thing he never used. They cannot be shapeshifted away. Every form he assumes has that white on the back, the specific shape of something that was supposed to carry him somewhere and never did.

His coherent windows are the most haunting in the story. In them, Hitori does not appear to want anything differently. He does not appear to regret staying. He appears, briefly, to be the same person he always was — and then the window closes, and what remains drifts on.

---

## The Relic — Wings

When Hitori is eventually killed, the white wings solidify over decades into a Relic. Whoever carries it can move in ways that feel like absolute freedom — faster, lighter, unencumbered in ways that have nothing to do with physical weight.

He spent his entire life as an anchor for other people. What he left behind lets people fly.
