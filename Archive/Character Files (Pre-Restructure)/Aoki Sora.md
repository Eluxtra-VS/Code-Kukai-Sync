# Aoki Sora

## Basic Information

- **Full name:** Aoki Sora
- **Role:** Former K.E.F. operative; later member of Kaito's group
- **Path:** Weaver
- **Class:** Vigor *(revised 2026-08-20 — was Forge; class follows desire, not function)*
- **Thread:** Hands
- **Signature:** Mend (with Unmake mode)
- **Pressure Type:** Self-blame as default worldview
- **Relationship to Ku:** Not blood related, but emotionally his little sister

---

## Core Personality

Quietly competent. Attentive. Always already taking responsibility.

Sora is the person who fixes the thing before anyone notices it broke. A strap, a plan, a wound, a route, a silence in a conversation. She moves toward damage with the automatic assumption that if she is there, then the damage is in some way hers to address.

This makes her reliable. It also makes her dangerous to herself.

Her first instinct when something goes wrong is not panic. It is causality. Where was she standing? What did she miss? What should she have done three seconds earlier, three days earlier, three years earlier?

If Sora is in the room, and something breaks, some part of her believes the room has already named her.

---

## Relationship With Ku

Sora and Ku are not blood related. That almost does not matter.

Ku is her older brother in every way that counts: the person she goes to first, the person whose silence she can read, the person who taught her when to move and when to stay still, the person she trusts enough to argue with because she knows the argument will not cost the relationship.

She is not his student in the clean mentor sense. She is his little sister: close enough to challenge him, close enough to know when he is lying by omission, close enough to be one of the few people whose disappointment can actually reach him.

Ku sees her as family. He would not say that easily. Sora knows anyway.

Their closeness predates Kaito's group. When they join the group mid-story, they arrive as a unit with history the others can feel before they understand it.

---

## K.E.F. History

Sora worked within the K.E.F. alongside Ku. She came to him for advice often enough that the pattern became obvious to both of them and neither named it.

They were assigned missions together repeatedly. Some of that was institutional scheduling. Some of it was because they worked well together. Some of it may have been because the K.E.F. understood that pairing Sora with Ku made both easier to predict.

Sora believed in the work longer than Ku did. Or, more accurately, she needed to believe in it. If the K.E.F. was wrong, then the things she had done for it became harder to survive.

---

## The Order

The K.E.F. ordered the elimination of a civilian who had independently discovered the Crown's deliberate neglect of the Tidal Plate.

Ku refused immediately.

Sora did not.

She knew the order was wrong. But refusing immediately would mean admitting the institution was already past the line, and if the institution was past the line, then Sora had to look back at every mission she had justified under its authority.

So she tried to carry it out.

She reached the civilian. She was in position. She could not do it.

The civilian survived and disappeared. Ku left loudly. Sora left through a door she had been walking toward for longer than she had admitted.

What Sora carries is not failure. It is the fact that she tried.

She has not told Ku the full truth of that moment.

---

## Why She Is a Weaver

Sora internalises everything. Not just blame in the obvious sense, but structure. When something breaks, she locates the repair inside herself first.

The Weaver path fits because her Pressure says: if I had been better, this would not have happened.

Her Thread is her **hands** because hands are where responsibility becomes action. Hands hold, fix, reach, fail to catch, fail to stop, fail to save. Sora's hands are the place she has always put the question: what should I have done?

## Signature - Mend (and Unmake)

**Mend** is Weaver Vigor through the hands. The class change matters: Sora's desire is not to build something real (Forge) — it is for her hands to **be enough** not to ruin what they touch (Vigor). The ability is the amplification of the self through the hands.

**Mend (constructive):** Sora can reshape damaged structures by touching them and forcing their Rei toward functional wholeness. This is not the same as Kaito's State function, which moves Rei through its own history. Sora does not restore the past. She creates a repair.

What she can mend:

- Broken tools and structures.
- Torn clothing, straps, bindings, weapons.
- Damaged Forge constructs.
- At higher mastery, surface-level wounds and Rei channel disruptions.

What she cannot do easily:

- Reverse death.
- Undo deep psychological Pressure.
- Restore something she does not understand.
- Fix a person who is refusing to be fixed.

The limitation matters. Sora's arc requires her to learn that not everything broken is asking for her hands.

**Unmake (destructive):** the same hands, reversed. On touch, she unravels the target's Rei integrity — structural cohesion loosens, techniques destabilise, an opponent's output degrades the longer she holds contact. Against a person, it does not kill; it *undoes* — strength, focus, and coherence drain as their Rei comes apart in her hands.

Unmake is the inverted mirror of Beat-Break: Ku's power amplifies when his rhythm is **honest**; Sora's destructive mode amplifies when her self-belief is **dishonest**. In a spiral, after a failure she has decided is her fault, Unmake flows like breathing. When she is grounded and honest, Mend is what wants to come out of her hands instead. Her worst belief — *I ruin everything I touch* — is literal fuel. Unmake is what the hands do when they agree with the wound.

---

## Cost

Mend carries the emotional cost of responsibility.

At low control, every repair leaves feedback in her hands: tremors, numbness, stiffness, pain, or temporary loss of fine motor control. The more she believes the break was her fault, the more aggressively the Thread consumes her.

Unmake's cost is different and worse: every use proves the wound right. Each unmaking is evidence that her hands are dangerous — feeding the belief that fuels the ability. It is a self-reinforcing loop only her resolution can break.

At higher control, the cost reduces when she treats damage as information rather than accusation.

Her power improves when she stops asking, "How is this my fault?" and starts asking, "What does this need?"

## Arc Direction

Early: Sora is the capable one behind the capable one. Ku moves first, Sora makes sure the movement holds.

Middle: The group starts noticing that Sora apologises for things no one accused her of. Her Mend is useful, but the more useful it becomes, the easier it is for her to hide inside repair work.

Pressure point: The truth of the K.E.F. order comes out, especially that she tried before she stopped. This should hurt Ku because he loves her, not because he rejects her.

Breakthrough: Sora fails to fix something and does not collapse into self-blame. She stays present without taking ownership of the whole damage. Unmake loses its grip on her hands.

Resolution: Mend becomes cleaner when she understands that repair is care, not confession. She can still unmake — when it is the honest choice, not the self-hating one.

---

## Relationship With Kaito's Group

Sora joins mid-story with Ku. She is not part of the original four.

To the group, she first reads as quiet competence. Over time, she becomes the person who notices practical danger first and emotional danger second - unless the emotional danger is her own.

Hitori probably trusts her quickly because she does not demand attention. Mel notices the self-erasure before most. Aiden initially underestimates her because she does not perform competence. Kaito recognises the self-correction pattern and does not like how familiar it feels.
