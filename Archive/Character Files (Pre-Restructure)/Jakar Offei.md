# Jakar Offei

## Basic Information

- **Name:** Jakar Offei
- **Organisation:** Tenebri
- **Sin:** Lust
- **Origin:** Northern Gear District — family from the Crash Region
- **Path:** Akarui
- **Class:** Veil
- **Energy:** Kuro exclusively
- **Signature:** Faultline *(unified 2026-08-20 — Warden is its extreme application, the permanent Kukai bond, not a separate signature)*

---

## Appearance

Jakar is the kind of person rooms notice before he opens his mouth.

His locs fall past his shoulders — kept with the specific intentionality of someone who has long understood that appearance is infrastructure. His features carry the Crash Region's lineage visibly. His presence has a quality that is not performed and cannot be turned off — the specific gravity of someone whose physical existence produces attention the way fire produces light. Automatic. Unconditional. Not about anything he does.

He knows this with a precision that has long since passed clarity into something colder. He knows the exact effect he has walking into a space. He knows the specific way eyes move when he enters. He knows the quality of the attention — that it is not about him, that it has never been about him, that it is about the surface he is standing behind.

He does not dress for attention. He does not need to. The attention arrives regardless. He has simply learned to make it functional.

## The Parents

His parents came from the Crash Region with the last wave of evacuees — his father with the music, his mother with the fighting forms, both with the specific grief of people who had watched their home renamed into a warning. They settled in the northern industrial quarter of the Gear District in rooms not built for either thing they had brought.

His father played music loud enough to cover the machinery. This was a deliberate choice, made every day, which is its own kind of statement about what matters.

His parents were warm people. Genuinely warm — the kind of warmth that comes from a culture that understood, at its root, that feeling things is not weakness, that expression is not indulgence, that joy and grief and love are meant to move through the body and be visible. They had lost too much to be healed people. But they were expressive people, which meant their Pressure had somewhere to go. Which meant it moved.

Jakar grew up watching this. He absorbed it the way children absorb the emotional architecture of the people they are raised by — not as a lesson, as a fact of how the world works. Feelings were visible. Feelings belonged to the person who felt them. Feelings moved and then they passed.

He did not know, as a child, that this was unusual. He would understand it later, with a clarity that cost him everything.

## The Sensitivity

Jakar was aware of other people's emotional states before he had language for what awareness meant.

His parents' grief registered in his body when he sat next to them — not as a concept but as a texture, a specific weight in the air that he felt against his skin. The workers in the district's lower levels carried exhausted resentment like a temperature. His teachers carried ambition of various qualities, some sharp and clean, some sour with suppression. He felt all of it without being able to name any of it.

He adjusted. He learned to read rooms before he entered them. He learned which spaces felt heavier than others. He learned that if he was careful about where he positioned himself in a conversation, he could feel the edges of what people were actually carrying beneath what they were saying.

He thought this was something everyone did. He thought the extra layer of information was universal. He would understand later — again, at the worst possible time — that it was not.

## What Attention Feels Like from the Inside

By the time Jakar was ten, the attention had become constant enough to study.

He could feel, through his sensitivity, what was happening in people when they looked at him. The specific Rei configuration of the attention — what it felt like when someone was drawn to his face, his presence, the surface of what he was. It had a texture he learned to recognise instantly: a kind of reaching that was not toward him but toward the image of him, toward the thing they had decided he was before he spoke.

The attention felt like being looked through. Like he was a window and what people were interested in was the light coming through the glass.

He found this exhausting and then, as he got older, he found it useful. If people were going to project onto him regardless of what he did, the only rational response was to manage what they projected. He began to understand his appearance as a tool with specific applications. He began to use it deliberately.

He did not become a manipulator because he was ambitious. He became a manipulator because he concluded, from evidence, that unmanaged emotion directed at him produced chaos, and managed emotion directed at him produced predictability. Predictability was something he could survive. Chaos had already shown him what it produced.

It had not shown him yet. That was coming.

## Ama

Her name was Ama. She had grown up in the buildings adjacent to his — close enough that they had been within each other's perceptual range since before either of them could articulate what perceptual range meant. She was not beautiful in the way that produced the specific attention Jakar received. She was precise and occasionally devastating about things that deserved precision and devastation. She had no particular interest in what Jakar looked like because she had been watching him make faces at walls since before either of them understood why he had the sensitivity and she didn't.

She saw through the surface. Not past it — through it, to the person behind the window, the actual Jakar who was exhausted by what other people did with his face and quietly horrified by how rarely anyone asked what was behind it.

She was the only person in his life who argued with him as if his conclusions mattered. Not his presence — his conclusions. She would tell him when he was wrong and expect him to engage with the correction rather than deflect it. She treated his intelligence as something real and accountable, which no one else did, because no one else was paying attention to his intelligence.

Through his Veil sensitivity he could feel, when he was with her, the specific texture of what she felt. It was not the projection-reaching of everyone else. It was something he could not fully name then — something that had his actual shape rather than the shape people made of him. Something that knew the difference between the glass and what was behind it.

He did not understand how much weight he had placed on that until it was gone.

## The Man

There was a man in the district. Older. Resources sufficient to act on his fixations. He had been in proximity to Jakar for months before Jakar registered the specific quality of the attention — and when he registered it, he recognised it immediately. The projection. The reaching toward something that was not actually Jakar. The kind of emotion that does not see its object and does not care that it does not.

Jakar managed it the way he had learned to manage all of it — careful distance, careful deflection, the specific skill of someone who has been doing this since childhood. He was good at this. He had been practicing since he was old enough to understand what the attention was.

What he did not account for: Ama.

Ama saw what was happening. She saw Jakar managing it alone, with the efficiency of someone who had been managing it alone for years, and she said something. Directly, publicly, with the precision she applied to everything that deserved precision. She told the man to leave Jakar alone.

Jakar was not there when it happened. He was told afterward.

He was twelve years old. The telling was not gentle because there was no gentle version of it.

## What Twelve Felt Like

He felt the absence before he fully processed it. Through the sensitivity — the constant low-level awareness of the emotional texture of the spaces he moved through — there was a shape missing. A specific Rei configuration that had been present in his environment for as long as he could remember, that he had absorbed as a baseline condition of being alive, that was not there.

He processed the information about why it was not there. He processed it alone, because the only person he would have processed it with was the reason he was processing it.

The specific horror of his position, which he understood at twelve with a clarity that twelve-year-olds should not have: she was dead because someone had looked at him the way people always looked at him. The projection-reaching. The emotion that did not see its object. He was the object. He was the surface onto which someone had projected something powerful enough to produce violence when it met resistance. Ama had been the resistance.

He had not caused it. He had not chosen it. He had not done anything except exist in the specific way he existed, which attracted the specific attention it always attracted, which had done the specific thing it had always had the potential to do.

He was twelve and he understood that his presence — the surface of what he was, the thing he had never chosen and could not change — was a weapon that other people fired without knowing they were holding it. And the people it hit were the ones who tried to stand between it and him.

He also understood something else. In the period immediately after Ama's death — in the raw, unmanaged grief of people in the district who had known her — he could feel, through his sensitivity, the specific texture of what they were actually experiencing. Not the performed versions. Not the social management of emotion. The real thing. Grief stripped to its structure. Fear of what the world actually was beneath the version they had been telling themselves. The specific weight of people being precisely what they were, unable to be anything else, the surface fallen completely away.

He had never felt so much genuine contact with other people in his life.

He did not understand what that meant yet. He was twelve. He filed it away with the specific, automatic efficiency of a child who has learned to file things that do not have homes yet.

## The Development

He was used as a tool between the ages of fourteen and seventeen. This is the clinical way to say it. People with resources and without scruples identified what his sensitivity could do — that proximity to him in difficult moments produced emotional shifts in targets who did not know he existed, that he could make difficult people cooperative, resistant people uncertain, witnesses confused about what they had seen.

He did not agree to this. He was fourteen and he did not have the leverage to not do it.

He spent three years doing it. He became very good at it. The skill he developed during this period — adjusting emotional states in targets, finding the specific Rei distortion that corresponded to each emotional configuration, learning to apply pressure or release to specific channels with precision — was not something he had been taught. He reverse-engineered it from necessity, in circumstances that did not allow for failure.

What he also developed: a comprehensive understanding of what people's emotions actually were when they were manufactured versus genuine. He had spent years feeling the projection-reaching of people who looked at his surface. He now spent years manufacturing specific emotional states in targets and feeling those manufactured states from the inside of the production. He knew, with a specificity no one else had, the texture of an emotion that was being shaped from outside versus one that arose from the person's actual interior.

The difference was unmistakable. Manufactured emotions felt like furniture — arranged correctly, present, functional, not belonging to the room. Genuine emotions, especially the negative ones, felt like the room itself. The walls. The floor. The thing that was actually there before anything was arranged in it.

At seventeen, he stopped doing what he was being told to do and started making his own decisions about what he did with the ability. This is not a heroic transition. His own decisions were not better. They were simply his.

## What Negative Emotions Actually Are

This is the thing that the simple reading of Jakar — sadist, manipulator, power-hungry — misses entirely.

When Jakar amplifies someone's negative emotions — when he pushes fear or grief or rage through them until it is all they can hold — he can feel, through his Veil sensitivity, the moment the surface drops.

The projection falls away. The management falls away. The specific performance that people do constantly — the version of themselves they have decided to present, the arrangement of furniture in a room that is not actually theirs — falls away completely. What he feels in that moment is the person. Not the surface. Not the projection. Not the window. The thing behind the window, raw and real and stripped to what it actually is.

For a man whose entire life has been spent being seen as a surface — who has felt the projection-reaching of hundreds of people and the genuine contact of exactly one, and lost her at twelve — the moment when someone's mask drops under manufactured pressure is the only moment he has ever felt in genuine contact with another person since Ama died.

He does not enjoy their pain. He is ravenous for the realness underneath it.

The pain is the price of the realness. He pays it without hesitation because the realness is the only thing he has ever found worth having, and suffering is the only state in which people cannot maintain their projections. When someone is in genuine fear or grief, they are only themselves. They cannot be in love with his face. They cannot be reaching toward the image of him. They are stripped to what is actually there, and what is actually there is — briefly, completely, really — present.

He is a sadist because suffering is the only door he has ever found into genuine human contact.

This does not make it acceptable. It makes it tragic in a way that is hard to look at directly, which is probably why he has never examined it.

## Ori

Ori was not the first person Jakar felt genuine contact with since Ama. Ori was the second.

Ori had been in proximity to Jakar for almost a year before the decision. They had arrived in the same social environment through the specific geography of the northern district — adjacent buildings, overlapping schedules, the ordinary accumulation of time spent near someone.

What made Ori different was not warmth. Ori was not warm toward Jakar in the soft sense. Ori argued with him. Called out the manipulation when they saw it operating — which Ori did see, with a directness that most people did not have the clarity to bring. Told Jakar when his conclusions were wrong and expected him to engage with the correction rather than route around it. Treated his intelligence as something real and accountable.

Through his sensitivity Jakar could feel what Ori actually felt when they were together. Not the projection-reaching. Not the image-hunger. Something that had the actual shape of him — that knew the difference between the surface and what was behind it, and was interested in what was behind it, and found the thing behind it worth arguing with.

He did not know what to do with this. He had learned, in the years since Ama, that genuine connection was dangerous — not for him but for the person connecting. Ama had seen through the surface and been killed by someone who could not. The safer architecture was the one he had built: controlled emotions, managed projections, nothing genuine enough to threaten anyone.

He watched Ori's Pressure building from the outside. It was not building because of him — it was building from Ori's own life, the specific accumulation of things Ori was carrying that had nothing to do with Jakar. He could feel it through his sensitivity the way you can feel a storm in the air before it arrives. The direction was already established. The threshold was approaching on its own timeline.

He made a calculation.

If Ori reached the threshold through natural progression, the transformation would be complete and the connection would be gone. No Warden link — Warden requires deliberate causation. Ori would become a Kukai, drift, hunt, and eventually be destroyed by someone, and Jakar would have been a bystander to the loss of the second person who had ever genuinely seen him.

If Jakar was the cause — if he pushed the Pressure across the threshold himself, deliberately — the Warden link would form. Ori would still transform. But Ori would be linked to him permanently. The coherent windows would exist. Some version of Ori would still be present. Not the full version. Not what Ori was. But not gone.

He chose to be the cause rather than the bystander.

He spent three months doing it. Methodically. With full awareness of what the endpoint would be and full awareness of what it would cost Ori to get there. He is not the kind of person who does things without understanding them. He understood this completely.

When the transformation was complete he established the chain. The feeding schedule. The management system that keeps Ori functional and present and as close to coherent as management can keep a Kukai.

## What He Did Not Expect

In the coherent windows, Ori does not appear to want things to have been different.

This is the one thing Jakar has not been able to process with clinical efficiency. He has routed everything else. He has not routed this.

The possibilities, which he cycles through in the specific quiet hours of maintenance:

Ori understands him completely — understands the logic, the causality, the specific fear underneath the decision — and has concluded that the fear was real and the decision was wrong but the person who made it is not reducible to the decision. This would be the version where Ori forgives him. This would be the version where Ori's not wanting things differently is the most profound thing anyone has ever offered him.

Or: the transformation removed Ori's capacity for the self-protective anger that would be justified. What looks like acceptance is the absence of the architecture that would produce refusal. The coherent windows are not Ori choosing to be present — they are a Kukai briefly resembling a person, which is not the same as a person being there.

He does not know which. He has maintained the feeding schedule with a consistency he has not applied to anything else in his life because as long as the question is open — as long as the coherent windows exist — some version of the answer is still available.

The day the coherent windows stop entirely is the day Jakar will have actually lost Ori. He does not think about this directly. He maintains the schedule.

## Who He Is Now

Jakar is excellent company. He is warm, observant, genuinely funny when he chooses to be, unhurried in conversation, and interested in people in a way that feels real — because it is real, in the specific way that his interest is real. He finds humans fascinating precisely because their emotional architecture is so legible to him and they believe so completely that it is not.

He is always the most composed person in any room. Not because he suppresses — suppression creates Pressure and Jakar's Rei runs clean. He processes with the efficiency of someone who has been doing it alone for a long time. Something happens, he identifies its nature, routes it. Nothing accumulates.

He hates that people love him. This is the consistent fact beneath every other fact about him. Every person who looks at him with projection-reaching — the image-hunger, the wanting that is not about him — is Ama's absence presented again. The mechanism that killed her, repeating. He cannot make it stop. He cannot change what his presence produces in people. He can only be the one managing it rather than being managed by it.

The contempt he carries for this mechanism — for projection-as-love, for emotion that uses its object rather than seeing it — is the specific fuel that makes the manipulation feel clean. He is not doing something to people. He is demonstrating, efficiently and repeatedly, what they were already doing to each other and would continue doing to anyone who stood in the way.

He is simply better at it. And he is honest about it. And honesty, in his experience, is the rarest thing there is.

## The Sadism — Complete Picture

Jakar chases negative emotions in the people around him because of what they reveal.

In fear, people are only afraid. In grief, people are only grieving. In rage, people are only angry. The social architecture — the performance, the projection, the specific ongoing fiction of who everyone is pretending to be — falls away completely under sufficient negative emotional pressure. What he feels through his sensitivity in those moments is genuine contact with the actual person.

He is addicted to this. Not to their suffering. To the realness that suffering strips them down to.

He understands that this requires their suffering to produce. He has concluded that this is an acceptable price. He does not believe this conclusion is virtuous. He believes it is honest, which to him is more important.

The Machiavellian quality — the manipulation, the deliberate engineering of conditions, the comprehensive management of everyone around him — is not about power. Power is incidental. What he is building, at all times, is an environment he controls. An environment no one enters without his understanding of what they are. An environment where no one can love his surface and produce, from that love, the weapon that killed Ama.

If he knows what everyone is feeling and why, and if he is the one shaping those feelings, then nothing arrives without his awareness. Nothing is unmanaged. Nothing can weaponise itself against someone he might care about — though he has learned to have very few of those.

He is not trying to be powerful. He is trying to be the only variable in his environment that does not surprise him. He has been trying to do this since he was twelve years old and someone's unmanaged emotion killed the one person who could see through the window.

## Faultline — the Single Signature

**Faultline** is his one signature: an Akarui Veil ability that manipulates a target's emotional pressure lines **once the target is real** — once their emotional mask is genuinely down and they are feeling something they cannot perform, manage, or manufacture.

**Condition: the target's emotional mask is down.** Fear stripped of management. Grief stripped of composure. Rage stripped of control. The condition is the entire mechanism (Veil): if the target is performing, managing, or deflecting, Faultline finds nothing to grip. The ability is inert against a composed person.

This is why he manufactures suffering. Pain is the most reliable mask-remover in existence — fear and grief strip people to what they actually are faster than anything else he has found. The sadism is not separate from the power: **his ability requires realness, and suffering is the only door he has ever found into it.**

Once the condition is met, he can adjust the target's actual interior: increase or lower emotions, amplify unresolved Pressure toward critical, destabilise Rei quality by forcing emotional states out of alignment, create openings by making the target react from a feeling they did not choose. The grip only holds while the realness holds — the moment the target re-composes, the condition must be re-entered.

**Warden — the extreme application:** when Faultline is used at the moment of a Kukai's transformation — when Jakar is the direct cause and the target is at maximum realness, masks destroyed, Pressure critical — the link becomes permanent: awareness, communication, and directional influence that cannot be fully refused. Not puppetry; the will remains. This is the permanent bond that holds Ori. It is not a separate ability; it is what Faultline becomes when he pushes a real moment past the threshold. ## The Tenebri Position

Jakar is valued by the Tenebri for Warden and for the specific operational efficiency of someone who can produce Kukai on demand and control them permanently. The Tenebri do not fully understand Warden's scope. They do not know how many are linked. They do not need to.

Jakar has not corrected their understanding.

He operates within the Tenebri because the Tenebri's goals are presently aligned with his own and because the Calamity's operational environment provides the specific density of genuine human emotion that he finds most useful. He will not remain if alignment changes. He would not describe this as loyalty. He would describe it as an arrangement that currently works.

He has not mentioned Ori's origin to anyone in the Tenebri. The Gluttony seat is held by his companion. The companion requires management. The management is consistent and non-negotiable. No one has asked why. This is the correct response.
