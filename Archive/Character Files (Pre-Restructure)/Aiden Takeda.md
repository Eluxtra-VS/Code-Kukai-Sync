# Aiden Takeda

## Basic Information

- **Location:** Tidal Plate
- **Story-start status:** Kyumin
- **Awakening:** Arc 1, during the Yoshi Kusakabe incident
- **Path:** Akarui
- **Class:** Forge
- **Signature:** Working signature — Shackles / Stagecraft (Forge concept under revision)
- **Pressure Type:** Vainglory — the performance of pride covering the absence of self-belief
- **Family:** Mai Takeda (older sibling; Tenebri, Sin of Pride)
- **Real Representative:** [[Joshua]]

---

## Core Personality

Confident. Loud. Performative.

Aiden leads with his chin. He acts like he believes in himself more than anyone in the room. He does not.

The performance is the point. Aiden does not have true Pride at the start of the story; he has **Vainglory** — the imitation of pride, built for witnesses. He performs certainty because silence would reveal the absence underneath it. He wants people to look at him because being seen feels, temporarily, like proof that he exists correctly.

He has mistaken attention for self-belief. Recognition gives him a short burst of relief, but never roots. That is why he keeps needing more of it.

---

## Awakening

Aiden awakens during the Yoshi incident.

The Kukai is drawn toward human Pressure and awareness. Aiden responds in the only language he knows: he makes himself impossible to ignore.

For once, this is not just performance. It works. His need to be seen becomes a shield because it pulls danger toward him and away from the group.

That is why he becomes Akarui. His first resolution is external: project outward, take the space, put himself between danger and everyone else.

The tragedy is that the same wound that saves people here can still corrupt him later if he mistakes the successful moment for proof that he should keep chasing attention.

---

## The Mechanical Truth

Aiden's power responds directly to whether he is performing.

When conscious of being watched and trying to impress, his Luz output is unstable. He overextends. He reaches for techniques he has not consolidated because they look better. He loses fights he should win because the constructs he builds are designed to be impressive rather than effective.

When he is not showing off — when no one is watching, or when he forgets to care that they are — his Forge output becomes coordinated, economical, and genuinely formidable. The constructs are less flashy but more structurally sound. They interlock. They serve the fight instead of asking to be admired.

His flaw is not that he has too much pride. His flaw is that he does not believe in himself enough to stop performing belief.

His power is not broken. His relationship with his power is.

---

## Signature Direction — Forge as Vainglory

Aiden should remain Forge. Forge is exactly right for him because he is trying to make something visible enough to be believed. The question is what he builds.

The current working signature, **Shackles**, still functions if the emphasis shifts: the shackles are not only about trapping opponents. They are about forcing the encounter to stay with him. Aiden's fear is not losing. It is becoming ignorable. A shackle says: stay here, look here, this matters because I am here.

**Condition:** Contact. When one of his Forge constructs touches a target directly, a shackle can form at the point of contact.

**Effect:** Immobilises the contacted point. A shackle on a limb prevents that limb from moving. Multiple shackles compound.

**Arc limitation:** Shackles formed while Aiden is showing off are visually larger, louder, and less stable. They hold briefly and break under pressure because the construct is built for display before function. Shackles formed when he is not thinking about being watched, when the contact is tactical rather than theatrical, are smaller, cleaner, and hold properly.

**Possible evolved name:** **Stagecraft**. If Shackles ever feels too narrow, Stagecraft may represent him more fully: Aiden builds the stage he wishes could make him real. Early Stagecraft creates flashy but sloppy constructs. Mature Stagecraft creates clean, coordinated Forge architecture that lets others move better, not just him look better.

He experiences the inconsistency as his ability being unreliable. He is right that it is unreliable. He is wrong about why.

## The Sibling Mirror

Mai Takeda is Aiden's older sibling and the endpoint of the road he is walking.

He has absorbed the surface of her: recognition arriving without being chased, rooms orienting toward her, competence that does not need announcement. What he has not understood is that Mai's ease is not health. It is Pride calcified past the point of needing to feel recognised.

When he learns what she is, the question is not whether he can fight her. It is whether he can look at the endpoint of his own desire and choose to stop before he gets there.

---

## Kaito Contrast

Aiden and Kaito both touch Pride, but in opposite directions.

Aiden's Vainglory performs outward: "See me, so I can borrow belief from your attention."

Kaito's Shame-Pride edits inward: "Do not see anything incomplete in me."

This makes them useful foils without giving them the same wound.

---

## Character Arc

Early: Fights as exhibition. Shackles are inconsistent. He blames technique, opponents, anything external.

Middle: Begins training alone and notices the difference. Refuses to name what it means.

Late: Fights alongside someone, lets them take the main role, and does not announce it. The shackles hold perfectly.

Resolution: Aiden does not become less loud by becoming smaller. He develops real self-belief, which means he no longer needs every action to prove he exists. Pride becomes rooted instead of borrowed.
